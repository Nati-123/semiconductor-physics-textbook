Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: With N_D and N_A both at their minimum (equal), confirm E_F sits almost exactly on the dashed E_i (intrinsic Fermi level) line.
  • Predict: Before raising N_D, predict which direction E_F will move.
  • Observe: Raise N_D and watch E_F rise toward E_C; then raise N_A instead (with N_D low) and watch E_F fall toward E_V.

Connects to Chapter 10: Once \(n_0\) is known (from the exact carrier concentration equation), the Fermi level follows directly from \(E_C-E_F=k_BT\ln(N_C/n_0)\). The intrinsic Fermi level, \(E_i\), is the special case where \(n_0=p_0=n_i\); it sits close to the middle of the band gap, offset slightly toward the band with the smaller effective density of states. Doping shifts \(E_F\) away from \(E_i\) toward whichever band edge corresponds to the majority carrier.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects the effective masses and band gap used to compute N_C, N_V, and E_i's offset from exact midgap.
Temperature slider
Sets T, which affects both nᵢ and the k_BT scale in the E_F formula.
N_D and N_A sliders
Set donor and acceptor concentrations, determining n₀ and therefore E_F's position.

Challenge Questions

1. As N_D is increased far above N_A, what happens to E_F?

Heavy n-type doping raises n0 toward N_D, which (via E_C-E_F=k_BT ln(N_C/n_0)) shrinks E_C-E_F, moving E_F up toward E_C.

Drag N_D to its maximum with N_A at its minimum and watch the E_F line.
