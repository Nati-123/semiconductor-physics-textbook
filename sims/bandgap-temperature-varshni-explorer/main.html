Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: With all three curves visible, drag the temperature marker slider from 0 K to 600 K and watch all three band gaps shrink together.
  • Predict: Before checking, predict whether the three curves stay perfectly parallel or shrink by different amounts.
  • Observe: Toggle each material's checkbox off and on to isolate a single curve for closer inspection.

Connects to Chapter 7: All three materials follow the same functional form, the Varshni equation \(E_g(T)=E_g(0)-\alpha T^2/(T+\beta)\), but each has its own fitted constants \(E_g(0)\), \(\alpha\), and \(\beta\), so the curves shrink at slightly different rates. Overlaying all three curves on one plot makes it easy to compare not just their gaps at any one temperature, but how each material's gap responds to temperature change.

What Each Control Does

Material checkboxes
Show or hide each material's Varshni curve independently.
Temperature marker slider
Moves a vertical reference line and shows each visible material's band gap at that exact temperature.

Challenge Questions

1. Do the three Varshni curves ever cross each other within 0–600 K?

Across the full 0–600 K range shown, the three curves keep the same relative ordering — GaAs highest, silicon in the middle, germanium lowest — they do not cross.

Drag the temperature marker across the full range and watch whether any curve overtakes another.
