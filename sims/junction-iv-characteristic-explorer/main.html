Try This

  • Try this: Leave the doping slider N at its default and slowly drag the V marker slider from the far left (deep reverse breakdown) all the way to the far right (forward bias), watching the dot travel across all three regions of the log|J| chart in one continuous sweep.
  • Predict: Before dragging the marker from \(V=0\) toward \(+0.5\ \text{V}\), predict how many decades \(|J|\) will rise by the time \(V\) reaches \(0.6\ \text{V}\).
  • Observe: Now sweep the doping slider N from \(10^{14}\) to \(10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\) and watch \(V_{BR}\) collapse by orders of magnitude while the breakdown region on the chart slides sharply rightward, and watch the mini-diagram's dominant mechanism flip from Avalanche to Zener once \(V_{BR}\) crosses about 6 V.

Connects to Chapter 15: This MicroSim is the single synthesis view for the chapter's closing result, the complete junction I-V characteristic: \(I(V)\approx I_0(e^{V/V_T}-1)\) for \(V>-V_{BR}\), and a rapid breakdown current increase for \(V\le-V_{BR}\). It combines the ideal diode equation (forward diffusion, exponential rise), reverse saturation current (the nearly voltage-independent \(-I_0\) plateau), and reverse breakdown (avalanche or Zener, set by the same doping-dependent \(V_{BR}\) estimate as the Reverse Breakdown Mechanism Explorer) into the one curve that defines diode behavior throughout every later device chapter.

What Each Control Does

N
Sets the lightly-doped side concentration, which drives both the estimated breakdown voltage \(V_{BR}\propto1/N\) and (mildly, illustratively) the saturation current \(J_0\).
V (marker)
Positions the live voltage marker on the I-V chart and in the junction cross-section diagram; its range is recomputed from the current \(V_{BR}\) so it always spans well past breakdown through forward bias.

Challenge Questions

1. As the V marker is dragged from 0 V toward +0.6 V, what happens to the current density J?

Correct. J = J0(e^(V/VT) − 1) rises exponentially in forward bias, which is why it plots as a straight line on a log|J| axis — this is the Forward Conduction region.

Look at the ideal diode equation's exponential term — what happens to e^(V/VT) as V increases well past zero?

2. For a fixed N, which region of the chart shows |J| pinned nearly constant across a wide range of (negative) V?

Correct. Once reverse bias exceeds a few VT, J = J0(e^(V/VT) − 1) ≈ −J0 to high precision, so |J| barely changes with V until breakdown is reached — the flat reverse saturation plateau.

Move the V marker slowly through negative voltages, well before reaching −VBR, and watch how little the readout J(V) changes.

3. Increasing the doping slider N moves the Breakdown region on the chart in which direction?

Correct. Since VBR ≈ εEcrit²/(2qN), heavier doping (larger N) gives a smaller VBR, so the dashed breakdown boundary and the whole Breakdown region slide toward V = 0.

Recall from the Reverse Breakdown Mechanism Explorer: is VBR proportional to N, or inversely proportional to N?
