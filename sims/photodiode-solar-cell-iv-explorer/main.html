Try This

  • Try this: Raise the photocurrent IL and watch the whole curve shift further down, increasing both Isc and Voc.
  • Predict: Before lowering I0, predict whether Voc will increase or decrease.
  • Observe: The shaded region (V>0, I<0) is where the device delivers power to an external circuit — this is the operating region for a solar cell, never used by a diode operated purely as a light sensor.

Connects to Chapter 17: A photodiode combines the ideal diode equation with a photocurrent, \(I=I_0(e^{V/V_T}-1)-I_L\). Operated to sense light (typically reverse-biased), or operated near zero/forward bias to deliver power, the same structure becomes a solar cell, characterized by \(V_{oc}\) and \(I_{sc}\).

What Each Control Does

I0
Dark saturation current — smaller I0 gives larger Voc for the same IL.
IL
Photocurrent, set by illumination intensity — directly sets Isc.

Challenge Questions

1. What is Isc, the short-circuit current, equal to?

Correct. At V=0, the diode term vanishes, leaving I = -IL, so the short-circuit current magnitude equals the photocurrent.

Set V=0 in the diode equation I = I0(e^(V/VT)-1) - IL — what remains?

2. Why must the solar cell's maximum power point lie strictly between V=0 and V=Voc?

Correct. P=IV=0 at V=0 (I is nonzero but V=0) and at V=Voc (V is nonzero but I=0), so the maximum power point is somewhere strictly in between.

Compute P=I×V at V=0 and at V=Voc — what do you get in each case?
