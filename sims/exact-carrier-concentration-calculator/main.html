Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The two towers always reach the same total height (dashed green line) — that is charge neutrality, \(p_0+N_D=n_0+N_A\), holding no matter how the sliders are set.
  • Each tower is stacked from two pieces: the fixed, fully-ionized dopant concentration on the bottom and the corresponding carrier concentration on top.
  • The info panel's colored line compares the exact n₀ to the common shortcut n₀≈N_D−N_A, and reports the percent difference between them.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Pick the "Intrinsic" preset and confirm n₀ ≈ p₀ ≈ nᵢ, with both towers short and nearly all carrier (no dopant) height.
  • Predict: Before switching to the "n-type" preset, predict which tower will grow taller and which carrier will become negligible.
  • Observe: Switch between the "Compensated" and "Nearly compensated" presets and watch the approximation-error readout — it stays small when doping dominates nᵢ, but grows as N_D and N_A approach each other.
  • Explore: Drag N_D and N_A independently (breaking out of the presets) and watch the majority/minority carrier labels swap the moment N_A crosses N_D.

Connects to Chapter 10: Solving the mass action law and charge neutrality condition together (Chapter 9) gives one exact formula for electron concentration, valid at any doping level: \(n_0=\frac{(N_D-N_A)+\sqrt{(N_D-N_A)^2+4n_i^2}}{2}\). This single equation smoothly reproduces both the near-intrinsic limit (\(n_0\to n_i\) as doping vanishes) and the familiar extrinsic approximation (\(n_0\approx N_D-N_A\) when doping dominates), unifying results that Chapters 7-9 treated as separate cases. The two charge-balance towers are simply \(p_0+N_D\) and \(n_0+N_A\) drawn side by side — the same charge neutrality condition, made visual.

What Each Control Does

Preset dropdown
Jumps directly to intrinsic, n-type, p-type, compensated, or nearly-compensated doping; switches to "Custom" once you move a slider.
Material dropdown
Selects the effective masses and band gap used to compute nᵢ(T).
Temperature slider
Sets T, recomputing nᵢ(T) and therefore n₀ and p₀.
N_D and N_A sliders
Set donor and acceptor concentrations (assumed fully ionized); net doping is N_D − N_A.

Challenge Questions

1. Predict first: when N_D and N_A are exactly equal, what does the exact formula give for n₀?

With N_D−N_A=0, the formula reduces to n0=√(4ni²)/2=ni — the fully compensated sample behaves as if intrinsic, exactly as Chapter 8 predicted qualitatively.

Set N_D and N_A to the same value and read the n₀ readout.

2. For which doping situation is the approximation n₀≈N_D−N_A least accurate?

When N_D and N_A nearly cancel, the net doping N_D−N_A can become comparable to (or smaller than) nᵢ, and the 4nᵢ² term under the square root is no longer negligible — the approximation breaks down exactly where the "Nearly compensated" preset sits.

Try the "Nearly compensated" preset and read the approximation-error percentage.

Key Takeaway: n₀≈N_D−N_A is not a separate rule from the exact equation — it is the same equation's limit once doping dominates nᵢ. Charge neutrality (the two towers matching) holds exactly at every doping level; only the simplified shortcut for n₀ has a validity range, and that range is exactly where the square-root term collapses to |N_D−N_A|.
