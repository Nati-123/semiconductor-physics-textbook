Try This

  • Try this: Look at where the purple (exact Fermi-Dirac) and gold (Boltzmann approximation) curves overlap almost perfectly, versus where they diverge.
  • Predict: Before dragging the temperature slider up, predict whether the "good approximation" shaded zone will widen or narrow in energy.
  • Observe: Drag the marker slider (E − E_F) to find the boundary of the shaded zone — the point where the Boltzmann approximation starts to disagree with the exact function by more than 5%.

Connects to Chapter 9: The exact Fermi function, \(f(E)=1/[1+e^{(E-E_F)/k_BT}]\), is difficult to integrate analytically against the density of states. Whenever \(E-E_F\gg k_BT\) (conduction-band states in a non-degenerate semiconductor), the exact function is very well approximated by the much simpler Boltzmann approximation, \(f(E)\approx e^{-(E-E_F)/k_BT}\) — this is exactly the substitution used to derive the effective-density-of-states formula for \(n_0\) later in this chapter, and it is only valid outside a heavily-doped, degenerate regime like the one Chapter 8 warned about.

What Each Control Does

Temperature slider
Sets k_BT, which rescales both curves and the width of the good-approximation zone in energy.
Marker slider (E − E_F)
Reads off both f(E) values and their percent difference at a chosen energy.

Challenge Questions

1. Where does the Boltzmann approximation most closely match the exact Fermi function?

When E−E_F is a few times k_BT or more, the "+1" in the Fermi function's denominator becomes negligible compared to the exponential, and the Boltzmann approximation becomes excellent.

Compare the two curves at large positive E−E_F versus near E−E_F=0.

2. What happens to the shaded good-approximation zone's width (in eV) as temperature increases?

The approximation's validity boundary sits at a fixed multiple of k_BT, so as temperature (and therefore k_BT) increases, that boundary moves to a larger energy in eV, widening the shaded zone.

Drag the temperature slider from low to high and watch the shaded zone's right edge.
