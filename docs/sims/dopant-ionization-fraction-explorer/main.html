Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: With the default ionization energy, find the temperature marker position where the ionization fraction crosses 50%.
  • Predict: Before increasing the ionization energy slider, predict whether the 50%-ionized temperature will rise or fall.
  • Observe: Drag the temperature marker across the full range and watch the fraction rise from nearly 0 (freeze-out) to nearly 1 (fully ionized).

Connects to Chapter 8: The fraction of dopant atoms that are actually ionized, \(f_{ion}(T)\), is not always 1 — at low temperature, many donor or acceptor atoms remain un-ionized (frozen out), simply because there is not enough thermal energy to overcome the dopant's ionization energy. This MicroSim uses a simplified sigmoid model of \(f_{ion}(T)\), calibrated so a dopant's characteristic "50% ionized" temperature scales with its ionization energy, consistent with the hydrogenic ionization energies from Chapter 7.

What Each Control Does

Ionization energy slider (E_D)
Sets the dopant's ionization energy, shifting the entire ionization-fraction curve along the temperature axis.
Temperature marker slider
Reads off the exact ionization fraction at a chosen temperature.

Challenge Questions

1. What does a low ionization fraction (close to 0) at low temperature represent physically?

A low ionization fraction means most dopant atoms still hold their loosely-bound electron (or hole) because there is not enough thermal energy available — the freeze-out condition.

Recall Chapter 7's donor/acceptor ionization discussion — ionization requires thermal energy comparable to the ionization energy.
