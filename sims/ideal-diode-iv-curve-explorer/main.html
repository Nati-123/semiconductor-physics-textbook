Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Switch to Semi-Log View and watch the forward region collapse into a straight line.
  • Predict: Before raising temperature T, predict whether the forward curve at a fixed voltage will shift to higher or lower current.
  • Observe: Dragging the voltage marker into reverse bias (negative V) shows J leveling off at −J0 almost immediately, well before reaching -2 V.

Connects to Chapter 15: The ideal diode equation, \(J=J_0(e^{V/V_T}-1)\), combines the law of the junction with the saturation current \(J_0\) derived from the injected minority carrier profile's gradient. It captures the diode's entire behavior: zero current at equilibrium, exponential forward conduction, and small, nearly voltage-independent reverse current.

What Each Control Does

J0
Sets the saturation current density (log scale), shifting the whole curve up or down.
T
Sets temperature, which sets the thermal voltage VT and therefore the forward curve's steepness.
View
Switches between linear and semi-log (log₁₀|J|) display.
V marker
Reads off J at a chosen voltage.

Challenge Questions

1. In the semi-log view, what does the forward region of the I-V curve look like?

Correct. Since J grows exponentially with V, log(J) grows linearly with V, producing a straight line on the semi-log plot.

Recall that a semi-log plot turns exponential growth into a straight line — that's exactly why it's used here.

2. What value does J approach under reverse bias, well before breakdown?

Correct. Once V is a few VT negative, e^(V/VT) is essentially zero, so J approaches the small, fixed value −J0 — the reverse "saturation" current.

Set V to a large negative value on the marker and read off where J levels out.
