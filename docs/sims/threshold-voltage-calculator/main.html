Connects to Chapter 16: The threshold voltage \(V_T=V_{FB}+2\phi_F+Q_{dep,max}/C_{ox}\) is the gate voltage at which strong inversion begins, combining the flat-band voltage, the bulk potential doubled, and the maximum depletion charge converted to a voltage via the oxide capacitance.

What Each Control Does

Gate material
Sets the gate work function, which sets VFB.
NA
Substrate doping, affecting both φF and Qdep,max.
tox
Oxide thickness, setting Cox = εox/tox.

Challenge Questions

1. Making the oxide thinner (increasing Cox) has what effect on the Qdep,max/Cox term?

Correct. Since Qdep,max does not depend on oxide thickness, a larger Cox (thinner oxide) directly shrinks the ratio Qdep,max/Cox.

Recall that Cox appears in the denominator of this term — what happens to a fraction when its denominator grows?

2. Which term in the threshold voltage formula depends on the gate material?

Correct. VFB = ΦM − ΦS is the only term containing the gate's work function ΦM; 2φF and Qdep,max/Cox depend only on substrate doping and oxide thickness.

Look at which formula explicitly contains ΦM (the gate material's work function).
