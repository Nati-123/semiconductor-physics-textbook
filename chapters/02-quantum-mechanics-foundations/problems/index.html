    End-of-Chapter Problems: Quantum Mechanics Foundations

    Work through these problems to reinforce your understanding of the quantum mechanics foundations from Chapter 2.

    Easy

    Problem 1

    State the de Broglie hypothesis in words (no equation).

    Show Solution

    Every particle with momentum, not just light, has an associated wave character; its wavelength is inversely proportional to its momentum. This extends wave-particle duality, first observed for light, to all matter.

    Problem 2

    Write the de Broglie wavelength formula and identify the SI unit of each quantity in it.

    Show Solution

    \(\lambda = h/p = h/(mv)\); wavelength in meters (m), Planck's constant \(h\) in joule-seconds (J·s), momentum \(p\) in kg·m/s, mass \(m\) in kilograms (kg), speed \(v\) in meters per second (m/s).

    Problem 3

    Write the numerical values of Planck's constant \(h\) and the reduced Planck's constant \(\hbar\), with units.

    Show Solution

    \(h = 6.626\times10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}\); \(\hbar = h/2\pi = 1.0546\times10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}\).

    Problem 4

    State the Heisenberg uncertainty principle as an inequality, and explain in one sentence what it means.

    Show Solution

    \(\Delta x\,\Delta p \geq \hbar/2\): the uncertainties in a particle's position and momentum cannot both be made arbitrarily small at the same time — their product has a fundamental, non-negotiable lower bound.

    Problem 5

    What boundary conditions must the particle-in-a-box wavefunction \(\psi(x)\) satisfy at \(x=0\) and \(x=L\), and why?

    Show Solution

    \(\psi(0)=0\) and \(\psi(L)=0\), because the potential is infinite outside the well, so the particle can never be found at or beyond the walls.

    Problem 6

    Write the normalization condition for a wavefunction \(\psi(x)\) defined over all space, and explain its physical meaning in one sentence.

    Show Solution

    \(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(x)|^2\,dx = 1\): since the particle must be found somewhere, the total probability of finding it anywhere in space must equal exactly 1.

    Problem 7

    An electron has momentum \(p = 2.0\times10^{-24}\) kg·m/s. Find its de Broglie wavelength.

    Show Solution

    \(\lambda = h/p = (6.626\times10^{-34})/(2.0\times10^{-24}) = 3.31\times10^{-10}\ \text{m} = 0.331\ \text{nm}\).

    Problem 8

    For a particle in a box, the ground-state energy is \(E_1 = 0.20\) eV. Using \(E_n = n^2 E_1\), find \(E_3\).

    Show Solution

    \(E_3 = 3^2 E_1 = 9(0.20\ \text{eV}) = 1.80\ \text{eV}\).

    Medium

    Problem 9

    Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron with kinetic energy \(KE = 50\) eV.

    Show Solution

    \(KE = 50\times1.602\times10^{-19} = 8.01\times10^{-18}\ \text{J}\). \(v=\sqrt{2KE/m}=\sqrt{2(8.01\times10^{-18})/(9.109\times10^{-31})}=4.19\times10^{6}\ \text{m/s}\). \(p=mv=3.82\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg·m/s}\). \(\lambda=h/p=(6.626\times10^{-34})/(3.82\times10^{-24})=1.73\times10^{-10}\ \text{m}=0.173\ \text{nm}\).

    Problem 10

    An electron is confined to a region \(\Delta x = 5.0\times10^{-11}\) m. Estimate the minimum uncertainty in its momentum, and convert this to a velocity uncertainty.

    Show Solution

    \(\Delta p \geq \hbar/(2\Delta x) = (1.0546\times10^{-34})/(2\times5.0\times10^{-11}) = 1.05\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg·m/s}\). \(\Delta v = \Delta p/m = (1.05\times10^{-24})/(9.109\times10^{-31}) = 1.16\times10^{6}\ \text{m/s}\).

    Problem 11

    Verify that \(\psi_2(x) = \sqrt{2/L}\,\sin(2\pi x/L)\) is properly normalized on \(0<x<L\).

    Show Solution

    \(\int_0^L|\psi_2|^2\,dx = \dfrac{2}{L}\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin^2(2\pi x/L)\,dx\). Using \(\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12(1-\cos2\theta)\), the integral evaluates to \(L/2\) (the cosine term vanishes at both limits since \(\sin(4\pi)=\sin(0)=0\)). So the total is \((2/L)(L/2)=1\), confirming normalization.

    Problem 12

    Find the ground-state energy of an electron confined to a box of width \(L=2.0\) nm.

    Show Solution

    \(E_1 = \dfrac{h^2}{8mL^2} = \dfrac{(6.626\times10^{-34})^2}{8(9.109\times10^{-31})(2.0\times10^{-9})^2} = 1.51\times10^{-20}\ \text{J} = 0.0940\ \text{eV}\).

    Problem 13

    For an electron confined to a box of width \(L=0.5\) nm, find the wavelength of a photon absorbed in a transition from \(n=1\) to \(n=2\).

    Show Solution

    \(E_1 = h^2/(8mL^2) = 1.504\ \text{eV}\). \(E_2 = 4E_1 = 6.016\ \text{eV}\). \(\Delta E = 3E_1 = 4.512\ \text{eV} = 7.23\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}\). \(\lambda = hc/\Delta E = (6.626\times10^{-34})(2.998\times10^{8})/(7.23\times10^{-19}) = 2.75\times10^{-7}\ \text{m} = 275\ \text{nm}\) (ultraviolet).

    Problem 14

    If the width \(L\) of a particle-in-a-box is halved, by what factor does the ground-state energy \(E_1\) change?

    Show Solution

    Since \(E_1 \propto 1/L^2\), halving \(L\) multiplies \(L^2\) by \(1/4\), so \(E_1\) increases by a factor of \(4\) — the ground-state energy quadruples.

    Problem 15

    An electron of energy \(E=1.0\) eV approaches a rectangular barrier of height \(V_0=2.0\) eV and width \(L=0.5\) nm. Estimate the transmission probability.

    Show Solution

    \(V_0-E = 1.0\ \text{eV} = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}\). \(\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar = 5.12\times10^{9}\ \text{m}^{-1}\). \(\kappa L = (5.12\times10^{9})(5.0\times10^{-10}) = 2.56\). \(T\approx e^{-2\kappa L} = e^{-5.12} \approx 5.95\times10^{-3}\), about a 0.6% chance of tunneling through.

    Problem 16

    A proton (\(m_p=1.673\times10^{-27}\) kg) and an electron each have kinetic energy \(1.0\) eV. Which has the longer de Broglie wavelength, and by roughly what factor?

    Show Solution

    Since \(\lambda = h/\sqrt{2mKE}\) for fixed \(KE\), wavelength scales as \(1/\sqrt{m}\). The electron, being far less massive, has the longer wavelength, by a factor of \(\sqrt{m_p/m_e} = \sqrt{1836} \approx 42.8\). Numerically, \(\lambda_e \approx 1.23\ \text{nm}\) versus \(\lambda_p \approx 0.0286\ \text{nm}\).

    Difficult

    Problem 17

    For the ground state (\(n=1\)) of a particle in a box of width \(L\), find the probability of finding the particle in the middle third of the box, \(L/3 < x < 2L/3\).

    Show Solution

    \(P = \displaystyle\int_{L/3}^{2L/3}\dfrac{2}{L}\sin^2(\pi x/L)\,dx = \dfrac{1}{L}\Big[x-\dfrac{L}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x/L)\Big]_{L/3}^{2L/3}\). At \(x=2L/3\): \(\sin(4\pi/3)=-\sqrt3/2\), giving bracket value \(0.805L\). At \(x=L/3\): \(\sin(2\pi/3)=+\sqrt3/2\), giving bracket value \(0.195L\). So \(P = (0.805L-0.195L)/L \approx 0.609\), about 60.9% — noticeably higher than the classical (uniform) probability of 1/3, since the ground-state probability density peaks at the center of the box.

    Problem 18

    Show that the normalization constant \(A=\sqrt{2/L}\) for \(\psi_n(x)=A\sin(n\pi x/L)\) on \(0<x<L\) is the same for every positive integer \(n\), not just \(n=1\).

    Show Solution

    Require \(A^2\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin^2(n\pi x/L)\,dx=1\). Using \(\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12(1-\cos2\theta)\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin^2(n\pi x/L)\,dx = \dfrac12\Big[x-\dfrac{L}{2n\pi}\sin(2n\pi x/L)\Big]_0^L = \dfrac12\big[L-0-0+0\big]=\dfrac{L}{2}\), since \(\sin(2n\pi)=0\) for every integer \(n\). So \(A^2(L/2)=1\) regardless of \(n\), giving \(A=\sqrt{2/L}\) for every eigenstate.

    Problem 19

    An electron in a box has ground-state energy \(E_1=0.50\) eV. Find the box width \(L\), then find the wavelength of a photon that would excite the electron from \(n=1\) to \(n=3\).

    Show Solution

    Solving \(E_1=h^2/(8mL^2)\) for \(L\): \(L^2 = h^2/(8mE_1) = (6.626\times10^{-34})^2/[8(9.109\times10^{-31})(8.01\times10^{-20})] = 7.52\times10^{-19}\ \text{m}^2\), so \(L \approx 8.67\times10^{-10}\ \text{m} = 0.867\ \text{nm}\). For the \(n=1\to n=3\) transition: \(E_3=9E_1=4.50\ \text{eV}\), so \(\Delta E=8E_1=4.00\ \text{eV}=6.41\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}\). \(\lambda=hc/\Delta E = 3.10\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}=310\ \text{nm}\) (ultraviolet).

    Problem 20

    Use the Heisenberg uncertainty principle with \(\Delta x \approx L = 1.0\) nm to estimate the minimum kinetic energy of an electron confined to a box of width \(L\), and compare this order-of-magnitude estimate to the exact ground-state energy \(E_1=0.376\) eV computed in Worked Example 7.

    Show Solution

    \(\Delta p \geq \hbar/(2\Delta x) = (1.0546\times10^{-34})/(2\times10^{-9}) = 5.27\times10^{-26}\ \text{kg·m/s}\). Estimating \(KE\approx(\Delta p)^2/(2m) = (5.27\times10^{-26})^2/(2\times9.109\times10^{-31}) \approx 1.53\times10^{-21}\ \text{J} \approx 0.0095\ \text{eV}\). This is roughly 40 times smaller than the exact value of \(0.376\) eV. The uncertainty principle only guarantees a lower bound and correct order-of-magnitude scaling with \(L\); it does not reproduce the exact numerical prefactor, which requires solving the boundary-value problem exactly, as done in the Particle in a Box section.

    Problem 21

    A tunneling barrier has decay constant \(\kappa = 3.0\times10^{9}\ \text{m}^{-1}\). Find the barrier width \(L\) required for the transmission probability to equal \(1.0\times10^{-6}\).

    Show Solution

    From \(T\approx e^{-2\kappa L}\), take the natural log of both sides: \(\ln T = -2\kappa L\), so \(L = -\ln(T)/(2\kappa) = -\ln(1.0\times10^{-6})/(2\times3.0\times10^{9}) = 13.82/(6.0\times10^{9}) = 2.30\times10^{-9}\ \text{m} = 2.30\ \text{nm}\).
