Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The error curve is high at low N_D (where nᵢ is comparable to net doping) and falls toward zero as N_D grows far past nᵢ — it never reaches exactly zero, but becomes negligible.
  • The green shaded region marks where the shortcut n₀≈N_D−N_A meets your selected tolerance; the dashed orange line is that tolerance threshold.
  • The crossover reading tells you the smallest N_D (at the current N_A, material, and temperature) where the approximation is trustworthy.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: With N_A at its minimum, drag N_D from 10⁶ up to 10¹⁹ cm⁻³ and watch the error fall from very large to essentially zero.
  • Predict: Before switching the tolerance dropdown from 5% to 1%, predict whether the crossover N_D will move higher or lower.
  • Observe: Raise N_A toward N_D (making the sample nearly compensated) and watch the whole error curve shift right — a much heavier N_D is now needed before the shortcut is trustworthy.
  • Compare: Switch material from Silicon to Germanium (much larger nᵢ) and notice the crossover point shifts to higher N_D — a bigger nᵢ demands more doping before it becomes negligible in the square root.

Connects to Chapter 10: The exact equation is \(n_0=\frac{(N_D-N_A)+\sqrt{(N_D-N_A)^2+4n_i^2}}{2}\). Whenever \((N_D-N_A)\gg n_i\), the term under the square root is dominated by \((N_D-N_A)^2\), so the square root collapses to \(|N_D-N_A|\) and \(n_0\to N_D-N_A\) — the familiar shortcut. This MicroSim makes that "≫" precise: it is not a fixed doping level, but a level relative to \(n_i\), which is why the crossover point moves with material and temperature.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects the effective masses and band gap used to compute nᵢ(T), which sets where the error curve falls.
Temperature slider
Sets T; higher T raises nᵢ, shifting the whole curve toward higher N_D.
N_A (fixed) slider
Sets a fixed acceptor concentration; raising it toward N_D shifts the curve right (worse approximation at a given N_D).
N_D slider
Moves the marker along the error curve and updates the numeric readout below.
Tolerance dropdown
Sets the acceptable error threshold (1%, 5%, or 10%) shown as the shaded region and dashed line.

Challenge Questions

1. Predict first: as N_D increases far above nᵢ, what happens to the approximation error?

As N_D−N_A grows far past nᵢ, the 4nᵢ² term under the square root becomes negligible compared to (N_D−N_A)², so the exact formula converges to the approximation and the error shrinks toward zero.

Drag N_D to its maximum and watch the error curve.

2. Why does raising N_A (toward N_D) make the approximation worse at a fixed N_D?

The approximation's accuracy depends on |N_D−N_A| relative to nᵢ, not on N_D alone. Raising N_A shrinks the net doping toward zero, which is exactly the near-compensated regime where the shortcut fails.

Raise the N_A slider with N_D fixed and watch the error curve shift right.

Key Takeaway: "Sufficiently doped" is a comparison to nᵢ, not an absolute doping level — the same N_D that is a perfectly safe approximation in silicon at 300 K could be a poor approximation in a material or temperature where nᵢ is much larger. Always check net doping against nᵢ before assuming n₀≈N_D−N_A.
