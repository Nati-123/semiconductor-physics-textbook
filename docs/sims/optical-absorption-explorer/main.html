Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Switch between the GaAs and Si-near-band-edge presets and compare the penetration depths.
  • Predict: Before dragging the α slider to its minimum, predict whether the curve will fall off faster or slower.
  • Observe: The curve always falls to about 37% (1/e) of its starting value exactly at x = 1/α, regardless of the α value chosen.

Connects to Chapter 17: Optical absorption follows the Beer-Lambert law, \(I(x)=I_0e^{-\alpha x}\), where the absorption coefficient \(\alpha\) sets how quickly light is absorbed with depth. Direct-gap materials have a much larger \(\alpha\) near their band edge than indirect-gap materials, since no phonon assist (Chapter 13) is needed for the underlying photon absorption event.

Challenge Questions

1. What does the penetration depth 1/α represent?

Correct. At x=1/α, I(x)/I0 = e^(-1) ≈ 0.37, the standard 1/e definition of penetration depth.

Look at where the dashed line falls on the curve — what fraction of the starting intensity remains there?

2. Why does GaAs typically have a much larger α near its band edge than silicon?

Correct. Direct-gap absorption is a much more probable process than indirect-gap absorption, which requires a momentum-conserving phonon, exactly as with indirect recombination in Chapter 13.

Recall the direct vs. indirect gap distinction from Chapter 6 and its effect on recombination efficiency in Chapter 13 — the same logic applies to absorption.
