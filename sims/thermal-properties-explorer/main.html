Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Switch material from Silicon to GaAs (much lower κ) at the same power and watch ΔT roughly triple.
  • Predict: Before raising depletion width W, predict whether Igen will grow, shrink, or stay the same.
  • Observe: At typical parameter values, the generation-current bar sits several orders of magnitude above the fixed diffusion-current reference bar — a direct visual of how much generation current can dominate real diode leakage.

Connects to Chapter 17: Thermal conductivity \(\kappa\) governs how efficiently a device sheds heat, \(\Delta T=Pt/(\kappa A)\). Separately, thermal generation rate \(G_{th}=n_i/\tau_0\) inside a depletion region produces a real generation current \(I_{gen}=qG_{th}WA\), a leakage mechanism beyond the ideal diffusion-based saturation current derived in Chapter 15.

What Each Control Does

P
Power dissipated through the slab, directly proportional to ΔT.
Material
Sets thermal conductivity κ, inversely proportional to ΔT.
τ0
Generation lifetime; longer τ0 means fewer thermally-generated carriers per second.
W
Depletion width; Igen scales directly with W.

Challenge Questions

1. Doubling the depletion width W has what effect on the generation current Igen?

Correct. Igen = qGthWA is directly proportional to W, so doubling W doubles Igen.

Look at where W appears in the Igen formula — is it linear, squared, or in the denominator?

2. Why does reverse bias on a real silicon diode tend to increase leakage current beyond the ideal diode equation's prediction?

Correct. Reverse bias widens W (Chapter 15), and since Igen is proportional to W, generation current increases beyond what the ideal diode equation alone predicts.

Recall how reverse bias affects depletion width, and how that width enters the Igen formula.
