Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Drag the time slider from 0 toward 8 hours and watch the marker slide along a curve that bends from steep (linear) to flattening (parabolic).
  • Predict: Before switching temperature to 1100°C, predict whether the same oxidation time will produce a thicker or thinner oxide.
  • Observe: Growth rate (the curve's slope) is fastest at very short times and continuously slows as oxide thickens.

Connects to Chapter 19: Thermal oxidation follows the Deal-Grove law \(x_{ox}^2+Ax_{ox}=Bt\), reducing to a linear regime \(x_{ox}\approx(B/A)t\) for thin oxide and a parabolic regime \(x_{ox}\approx\sqrt{Bt}\) for thick oxide.

What Each Control Does

Temperature
Oxidation furnace temperature preset, which sets the Deal-Grove rate constants A and B.
Time
Oxidation time in hours, moving the marker along the growth curve.

Challenge Questions

1. Doubling oxidation time in the parabolic regime multiplies thickness by approximately:

Correct. Since x_ox ≈ √(Bt), doubling t multiplies thickness by √2.

Try comparing the marker's height at t and 2t once the curve has flattened into the parabolic regime.

2. Why does growth rate slow down as oxide thickens?

Correct. Once oxide is thick, oxidant transport through the existing oxide layer becomes the rate-limiting step.

Think about what a growing oxygen or water molecule must pass through to reach the unreacted silicon underneath.
