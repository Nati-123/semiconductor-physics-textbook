Connects to Chapter 3: This MicroSim renders the three cubic Bravais lattices from the chapter — Simple Cubic, Body-Centered Cubic (BCC), and Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) — using the standard touching-sphere conditions \(2r=a\) (SC), \(4r=\sqrt{3}\,a\) (BCC), and \(4r=\sqrt{2}\,a\) (FCC) to compute the atomic packing fractions 0.524, 0.680, and 0.740.

What Each Control Does

Crystal structure dropdown
Switches the displayed unit cell between Simple Cubic, Body-Centered Cubic, and Face-Centered Cubic, updating the atom positions, colors, and the readout panel below the canvas.
Click-and-drag on the 3D view
Rotates the unit cell freely so you can inspect atom positions from any angle (uses p5.js \(\texttt{orbitControl()}\)).
Scroll / pinch on the 3D view
Zooms the camera in and out.

Challenge Questions

1. How many atoms per unit cell does the Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) structure contain?

8 corner atoms × 1/8 each = 1, plus 1 full body-center atom = 2 atoms per cell, matching the readout panel.

Select BCC and read the "Atoms per unit cell" value directly from the readout panel below the canvas.

2. Which structure has the highest coordination number?

FCC has coordination number 12 — each atom touches 12 nearest neighbors, the closest possible packing among these three structures.

Cycle through all three structures in the dropdown and compare the "Coordination number" line in the readout panel.

3. In Simple Cubic, what fraction of each corner atom actually belongs to a single unit cell?

Each corner is shared by 8 adjacent unit cells, so only 1/8 of each corner atom counts toward this cell — 8 × 1/8 = 1 atom total for SC.

Think about how many unit cells meet at a single corner of a cube packed in 3D space.

4. Why does Face-Centered Cubic have a higher packing fraction (0.740) than Simple Cubic (0.524)?

The FCC touching condition \(4r=\sqrt{2}\,a\) lets relatively larger atoms fit for a given \(a\), filling 74% of the volume versus 52.4% for SC.

Compare how atoms touch along the cube edge (SC) versus along the face diagonal (FCC) using the sphere positions shown.

5. How many face-center atoms are added in the FCC unit cell, and what color are they in this MicroSim?

A cube has 6 faces, and FCC places one atom at the center of each — shown in orange, each shared between 2 neighboring cells (6 × 1/2 = 3 additional atoms, plus 1 from the corners = 4 total).

Select Face-Centered Cubic and count the orange spheres, then check the legend below the readout panel.
