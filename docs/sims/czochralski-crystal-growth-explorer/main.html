Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Click Start and set pull rate near 4 mm/min — watch the ingot grow with a smooth, uniform width and low defect level.
  • Predict: Before dragging pull rate to 10 mm/min, predict whether defect level will rise or fall.
  • Observe: Far from the ideal pull rate, the ingot's simulated diameter wobbles and defect level climbs — real crystal growth has a similarly narrow process window.

Connects to Chapter 19: Czochralski crystal growth pulls a rotating seed crystal from a crucible of molten silicon; pulling too fast narrows the ingot and increases defect density, while pulling too slowly wastes furnace throughput.

What Each Control Does

Pull rate
Speed at which the seed crystal is withdrawn from the melt, in mm/min.
Rotation rate
Rotation speed of the seed and ingot, in rpm, which helps average out melt temperature non-uniformity.

Challenge Questions

1. What happens to defect level when pull rate is far from the ideal value?

Correct. Pulling too fast or too slow away from the ideal rate increases crystal defect density in this simplified model.

Try dragging pull rate far above or below 4 mm/min and watch the defect level readout.

2. Why is float-zone refining, not Czochralski growth, used when extremely high purity is required?

Correct. Avoiding crucible contact avoids the trace contamination that limits Czochralski silicon's purity.

Think about what a crucible wall could introduce into the melt.
