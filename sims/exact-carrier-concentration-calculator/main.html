Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Set N_D and N_A to their minimum (equal, both 10¹³ cm⁻³) and confirm n₀ ≈ p₀ ≈ nᵢ — the intrinsic limit.
  • Predict: Before raising N_D far above N_A, predict what will happen to n₀ and p₀.
  • Observe: Raise N_D and watch n₀ smoothly transition from ≈nᵢ toward ≈(N_D−N_A), while p₀ falls correspondingly — all from one exact formula, no separate cases needed.

Connects to Chapter 10: Solving the mass action law and charge neutrality condition together (Chapter 9) gives one exact formula for electron concentration, valid at any doping level: \(n_0=\frac{(N_D-N_A)+\sqrt{(N_D-N_A)^2+4n_i^2}}{2}\). This single equation smoothly reproduces both the near-intrinsic limit (\(n_0\to n_i\) as doping vanishes) and the familiar extrinsic approximation (\(n_0\approx N_D-N_A\) when doping dominates), unifying results that Chapters 7-9 treated as separate cases.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects the effective masses and band gap used to compute nᵢ(T).
Temperature slider
Sets T, recomputing nᵢ(T) and therefore n₀ and p₀.
N_D and N_A sliders
Set donor and acceptor concentrations (assumed fully ionized); net doping is N_D − N_A.

Challenge Questions

1. When N_D and N_A are exactly equal, what does the exact formula give for n₀?

With N_D−N_A=0, the formula reduces to n0=√(4ni²)/2=ni — the fully compensated sample behaves as if intrinsic, exactly as Chapter 8 predicted qualitatively.

Set N_D and N_A to the same value and read the n₀ readout.
