    Quiz: Physics and Math Foundations

    Test your understanding of the physics and mathematics foundations covered in Chapter 1 with these 20 questions.

    Question 1

    According to Newton's second law as used in this chapter, how is the net force on an object related to its mass and acceleration?

    1. Force equals mass divided by acceleration
    2. Force equals mass times acceleration
    3. Force equals mass plus acceleration
    4. Force equals acceleration divided by mass
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Newton's second law states \(\vec{F}=m\vec{a}\): the net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration. This relationship carries over directly to carriers in a semiconductor, where mass is often replaced by an effective mass \(m^*\).

    Concept Tested: Force

    Question 2

    Which statement correctly describes electric charge as introduced in this chapter?

    1. Charge can take any continuous value, not just multiples of a fixed unit
    2. Charge is only ever positive, never negative
    3. Charge is created and destroyed continuously as carriers move
    4. Charge is quantized in integer multiples of the elementary charge, e = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: D

    Electric charge is a fundamental, conserved property of matter that is quantized: \(q=ne\), where \(e=1.602\times10^{-19}\) C. Options A and C contradict quantization and conservation of charge; option B ignores that charge comes in both signs.

    Concept Tested: Electric Charge

    Question 3

    How is electric potential (voltage) defined in this chapter?

    1. Potential energy per unit charge
    2. Force per unit charge
    3. Charge per unit energy
    4. Energy per unit time
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Electric potential is defined as \(V=U/q\), potential energy per unit charge. Option B describes the electric field, not potential; options C and D do not match any quantity defined in this chapter.

    Concept Tested: Electric Potential

    Question 4

    What physical role does the Boltzmann constant play, as described in this chapter?

    1. It converts electric field into electric potential
    2. It converts frequency into photon energy
    3. It relates absolute temperature to the average thermal energy of particles
    4. It relates electric charge to electric current
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    The Boltzmann constant, \(k_B=1.381\times10^{-23}\) J/K, bridges macroscopic temperature and microscopic particle energy, as in \(\langle KE\rangle = \tfrac{3}{2}k_BT\). Option B describes Planck's constant instead.

    Concept Tested: Boltzmann Constant

    Question 5

    What does the divergence of a vector field measure at a point?

    1. The field's rotational tendency around the point
    2. The net outward flow of the field per unit volume at the point
    3. The direction of steepest increase of a scalar quantity
    4. The magnitude of the field at that exact point only
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Divergence, \(\nabla\cdot\vec{F}\), measures whether a point behaves as a source (positive divergence) or sink (negative divergence) of the field. Option A describes curl, and option C describes the gradient.

    Concept Tested: Divergence

    Question 6

    Two point charges are separated by distance r. If that distance is tripled while the charges stay the same, what happens to the electrostatic force between them, according to Coulomb's Law?

    1. It becomes 1/3 of its original value
    2. It becomes 3 times its original value
    3. It stays the same
    4. It becomes 1/9 of its original value
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: D

    Coulomb's Law states \(F \propto 1/r^2\). Tripling \(r\) increases \(r^2\) by a factor of 9, so the force drops to \(1/9\) of its original value. This inverse-square sensitivity is why electrostatic force falls off quickly with distance.

    Concept Tested: Coulomb's Law

    Question 7

    Why is the electric field considered a more general description of electrostatic influence than the electrostatic force from Coulomb's Law?

    1. The electric field is defined independent of any particular test charge, describing a property of space itself
    2. The electric field only exists between two specific point charges
    3. The electric field depends on the mass of the source charge
    4. The electric field is a scalar quantity, unlike force
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Unlike Coulomb's Law, which describes force between two specific charges, the electric field \(\vec{E}\) is defined without reference to any particular test charge, making it a property of space created by the source charge(s). It is a vector quantity, not a scalar, so option D is incorrect.

    Concept Tested: Electric Field

    Question 8

    According to Euler's formula introduced in this chapter, how is the complex exponential e^(iθ) related to trigonometric functions?

    1. e^(iθ) equals sin θ divided by cos θ
    2. e^(iθ) equals cos θ plus i sin θ
    3. e^(iθ) equals cos θ minus sin θ
    4. e^(iθ) equals the magnitude of a complex number only
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Euler's formula states \(e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta + i\sin\theta\), connecting complex exponentials to trigonometric functions. This is the identity behind writing traveling waves as complex exponentials in later chapters.

    Concept Tested: Complex Numbers

    Question 9

    How does the electric field relate to the electric potential via the gradient, as presented in this chapter?

    1. The electric field equals the positive gradient of the potential
    2. The electric field equals the curl of the potential
    3. The electric field equals the negative gradient of the potential
    4. The electric field equals the divergence of the potential
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    The chapter states \(\vec{E}=-\nabla V\): the electric field is the negative gradient of the potential, pointing from high potential toward low potential. Curl and divergence are different operators with different physical meanings.

    Concept Tested: Gradient

    Question 10

    A photon's wavelength is decreased while the speed of light stays constant. Based on E = hc/λ, what happens to its energy?

    1. The photon's energy decreases
    2. The photon's energy stays exactly the same
    3. The photon's energy becomes zero
    4. The photon's energy increases
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: D

    Since \(E_{\text{photon}}=hc/\lambda\), energy is inversely proportional to wavelength. Decreasing \(\lambda\) therefore increases the photon's energy — shorter-wavelength light carries more energy per photon.

    Concept Tested: Photon Energy

    Question 11

    Given the potential V(x,y,z) = 3x²y + 2z, what is ∂V/∂x?

    1. 6xy
    2. 3x²
    3. 6x
    4. 3y²
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Treating \(y\) and \(z\) as constants and differentiating \(3x^2y\) with respect to \(x\) gives \(6xy\); the term \(2z\) has no \(x\)-dependence and drops out. This is the same partial-derivative technique used to compute a gradient.

    Concept Tested: Partial Derivatives

    Question 12

    A spherical Gaussian surface encloses a fixed point charge. If the radius of the sphere is doubled while the enclosed charge stays the same, what does Gauss's Law say happens to the total electric flux through the surface?

    1. The flux quadruples
    2. The flux is cut in half
    3. The flux stays exactly the same
    4. The flux drops to zero
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    Gauss's Law, \(\oint\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{A}=Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0\), shows flux depends only on the enclosed charge, which is unchanged. The electric field magnitude does decrease, but the larger surface area exactly compensates, keeping flux constant.

    Concept Tested: Gauss's Law

    Question 13

    In this chapter's notation, what distinguishes a vector quantity like force from a scalar quantity like energy?

    1. A vector has both magnitude and direction; a scalar has magnitude only
    2. A vector is always negative; a scalar is always positive
    3. A vector can only describe electric quantities; a scalar cannot
    4. A vector has magnitude only; a scalar has both magnitude and direction
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Vectors, such as force and electric field, have both magnitude and direction. Scalars, such as energy and electric potential, have magnitude only. Option D reverses this distinction.

    Concept Tested: Vectors

    Question 14

    What does the curl of the static electric field equal, according to this chapter?

    1. The charge density divided by permittivity
    2. The electric flux through a closed surface
    3. The gradient of the electric potential
    4. Zero
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: D

    For the electric field produced by static charges, \(\nabla\times\vec{E}=0\). This is what allows an electrostatic field to always be written as the gradient of a scalar potential.

    Concept Tested: Curl

    Question 15

    How is electric flux through a flat surface in a uniform field defined in this chapter?

    1. The charge enclosed divided by the surface area
    2. The field's component perpendicular to the surface, multiplied by the area
    3. The field's component parallel to the surface, multiplied by the area
    4. The total charge on the surface
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Electric flux is defined as \(\Phi_E = \vec{E}\cdot\vec{A} = EA\cos\theta\), the component of the field perpendicular to the surface times the surface area. The parallel component (option C) contributes nothing to flux.

    Concept Tested: Electric Flux

    Question 16

    Why is the permittivity of free space, ε₀, classified as a fundamental physical constant rather than a property specific to one experiment?

    1. Because its value changes depending on which material is being tested
    2. Because it is measured in volts rather than farads per meter
    3. Because it is the same fixed numerical value in every calculation involving electrostatics in a vacuum
    4. Because it only applies inside semiconductor crystals
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    Fundamental physical constants, like \(\varepsilon_0 = 8.854\times10^{-12}\) F/m, are fixed numerical quantities that appear throughout physics and do not vary between experiments or materials, unlike measured properties such as doping concentration.

    Concept Tested: Fundamental Physical Constants

    Question 17

    A crystal has a uniform temperature and exchanges no net energy with its surroundings, but a second sample of the same material has hot and cold regions. Which sample is in thermal equilibrium, and why?

    1. The first sample, because it has a single well-defined temperature and no net energy exchange with its surroundings
    2. The second sample, because temperature differences drive useful energy flow
    3. Neither sample, because thermal equilibrium requires an applied voltage
    4. Both samples, because thermal equilibrium only depends on total energy, not its distribution
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Thermal equilibrium requires a single, well-defined temperature throughout the system and no net energy exchange with its surroundings. A sample with hot and cold regions has not reached this state, regardless of its total energy.

    Concept Tested: Thermal Equilibrium

    Question 18

    If two like-signed point charges are moved closer together, what happens to their electrostatic potential energy, based on U = q_test V(r)?

    1. It decreases
    2. It becomes negative
    3. It remains exactly zero
    4. It increases
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: D

    Since \(U=\tfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\tfrac{qq_{\text{test}}}{r}\) and both charges share a sign, \(U\) is positive and grows larger as \(r\) decreases. It takes work to push like charges closer together.

    Concept Tested: Electrostatic Potential Energy

    Question 19

    Using V = V_T ln(I/I₀ + 1), find V (in volts) when I = 99 I₀ and V_T = 0.0259 V.

    1. 0.0259 V
    2. 0.119 V
    3. 2.59 V
    4. 9.9 V
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Substituting: \(V = 0.0259\ln(99+1) = 0.0259\ln(100) = 0.0259\times4.605 \approx 0.119\) V. This mirrors the algebra used to solve the diode equation for voltage given current.

    Concept Tested: Exponentials and Logarithms

    Question 20

    A charged particle is released from rest near a fixed point charge of the same sign and moves away under the resulting repulsive force. Which statement best explains the underlying reason its kinetic energy increases while its electrostatic potential energy decreases?

    1. Total charge is being converted into mass as the particle moves
    2. The particle is gaining energy from an external battery not described in the scenario
    3. Conservation of mechanical energy requires that a decrease in potential energy appear as an equal increase in kinetic energy, since no external force removes energy from the system
    4. Kinetic and potential energy are independent quantities with no required relationship
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    In an isolated system, mechanical energy (kinetic plus potential) is conserved. As the particle moves away and its electrostatic potential energy drops, that same amount of energy must appear as kinetic energy — the two are not independent, ruling out option D.

    Concept Tested: Mechanical Energy
