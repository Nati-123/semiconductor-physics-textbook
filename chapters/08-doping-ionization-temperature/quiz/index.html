    Quiz: Doping, Ionization, and Temperature Regimes

    Test your understanding of doping types, ionization, and temperature regions covered in Chapter 8 with these 20 questions.

    Question 1

    What is the majority carrier in n-type material?

    1. Holes
    2. Free electrons
    3. Protons
    4. Neutrons
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    N-type material is doped with donors, so free electrons are its majority carrier.

    Concept Tested: N-Type Doping

    Question 2

    What is the majority carrier in p-type material?

    1. Free electrons
    2. Holes
    3. Protons
    4. Photons
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    P-type material is doped with acceptors, so holes are its majority carrier.

    Concept Tested: P-Type Doping

    Question 3

    What does "dopant ionization" refer to?

    1. The permanent destruction of a dopant atom
    2. The temperature-dependent process of a donor releasing its electron or an acceptor capturing one
    3. A process that only happens at absolute zero
    4. Breaking a covalent bond in pure silicon
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Dopant ionization is the temperature-dependent release of a donor's electron (or capture of an electron by an acceptor), not related to breaking a covalent bond.

    Concept Tested: Dopant Ionization

    Question 4

    What quantity determines how much thermal energy a dopant needs before it substantially ionizes?

    1. Its ionization energy
    2. Its atomic radius
    3. Its melting point
    4. Its color
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    A dopant's ionization energy, compared to available thermal energy k_BT, determines its ionization fraction at a given temperature.

    Concept Tested: Ionization Energy

    Question 5

    In the extrinsic temperature region, what does majority carrier concentration approximately equal?

    1. The intrinsic carrier concentration
    2. The doping concentration
    3. Zero
    4. The silicon atomic density
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    In the extrinsic region, dopants are essentially fully ionized, so majority carrier concentration ≈ doping concentration.

    Concept Tested: Doping Concentration

    Question 6

    What determines majority carrier type in a compensated semiconductor?

    1. N_D alone
    2. N_A alone
    3. The net doping, N_D − N_A
    4. The crystal's color
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    Because donor electrons and acceptor holes can recombine, only the net doping, N_D − N_A, determines the majority carrier type and concentration.

    Concept Tested: Compensated Semiconductor

    Question 7

    A sample has N_D = N_A exactly. How does it behave?

    1. As a metal
    2. As if intrinsic
    3. As a pure insulator
    4. It has no electrons at all
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    With net doping exactly zero, the sample behaves electrically as if intrinsic, despite containing both dopant species.

    Concept Tested: Compensated Semiconductor

    Question 8

    What happens to carrier concentration in the freeze-out regime as temperature increases?

    1. It decreases
    2. It rises steeply as dopants progressively ionize
    3. It stays exactly constant
    4. It becomes negative
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    As temperature rises through the freeze-out regime, more dopant atoms gain enough thermal energy to ionize, so carrier concentration rises steeply.

    Concept Tested: Freeze-Out Regime

    Question 9

    Why is the extrinsic temperature region generally preferred for device operation?

    1. Carrier concentration is approximately constant and set by design, not by small temperature changes
    2. It is the coldest possible temperature
    3. It is the only region where atoms exist
    4. Devices cannot operate in any other region at all
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    In the extrinsic region, carrier concentration ≈ N_D and is stable against small temperature fluctuations, giving predictable device behavior.

    Concept Tested: Extrinsic Temperature Region

    Question 10

    Why does carrier concentration eventually rise again at very high temperature, regardless of doping?

    1. Donor atoms multiply
    2. Thermally-generated intrinsic carriers eventually exceed the doping concentration
    3. The band gap becomes infinite
    4. It does not rise again
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Intrinsic carrier concentration grows exponentially with temperature and eventually exceeds any fixed doping concentration, entering the intrinsic temperature region.

    Concept Tested: Intrinsic Temperature Region

    Question 11

    What defines a degenerate semiconductor?

    1. A material with no dopants at all
    2. Doping so heavy the Fermi level is pushed to or past a band edge
    3. A material with an infinite band gap
    4. A material at absolute zero only
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    A degenerate semiconductor is doped heavily enough that the Fermi level reaches or crosses a band edge, invalidating non-degenerate statistics.

    Concept Tested: Degenerate Semiconductor

    Question 12

    Approximately what is silicon's effective conduction-band density of states, N_C, at room temperature?

    1. 2.8×10^19 cm^-3
    2. 5×10^22 cm^-3
    3. 10^10 cm^-3
    4. 1.12 eV
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Silicon's N_C is approximately 2.8×10^19 cm^-3 at room temperature; doping near or above this value produces a degenerate semiconductor.

    Concept Tested: Degenerate Semiconductor

    Question 13

    Why are heavily-doped MOSFET source/drain contacts deliberately made degenerate?

    1. To maximize resistivity
    2. To minimize resistivity for a good low-resistance contact
    3. Degeneracy is unavoidable and serves no purpose
    4. To eliminate all carriers
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Very heavy doping maximizes carrier concentration, minimizing contact resistivity — more important there than preserving non-degenerate statistics.

    Concept Tested: Degenerate Semiconductor

    Question 14

    A dopant with a larger ionization energy, compared to one with a smaller ionization energy, will show significant ionization at what kind of temperature?

    1. A lower temperature
    2. A higher temperature
    3. Exactly the same temperature always
    4. Ionization energy has no effect on this
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    A larger ionization energy requires more thermal energy (a higher temperature) before the ionization fraction becomes significant.

    Concept Tested: Ionization Energy

    Question 15

    Increasing N_D in the freeze-out/extrinsic/intrinsic carrier-concentration curve does what to the extrinsic plateau?

    1. Lowers it
    2. Raises it
    3. Has no effect on it
    4. Removes it entirely
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Since the extrinsic plateau height is set by N_D itself, increasing N_D raises the plateau.

    Concept Tested: Doping Concentration

    Question 16

    A silicon sample has N_A = 6×10^16 cm^-3 and N_D = 1×10^16 cm^-3. What is the material type?

    1. n-type
    2. p-type
    3. Intrinsic
    4. Degenerate metal
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Since N_A > N_D, net doping is positive for acceptors, making this p-type material.

    Concept Tested: P-Type Doping

    Question 17

    What minority carrier exists in n-type material?

    1. Free electrons
    2. Holes
    3. Donor ions
    4. Photons
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Holes are the minority carrier in n-type material, present in small numbers relative to the majority electrons.

    Concept Tested: N-Type Doping

    Question 18

    Why does complete ionization become a good approximation near and above room temperature for typical silicon dopants?

    1. Because measured donor/acceptor ionization energies (tens of meV) are comparable to room-temperature thermal energy (~26 meV)
    2. Because dopant atoms disappear above 0°C
    3. Because silicon melts at room temperature
    4. It is not actually a good approximation at room temperature
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Because typical ionization energies are only a few times k_BT at room temperature (or less), essentially all dopants are ionized at and above room temperature.

    Concept Tested: Dopant Ionization

    Question 19

    What happens to the freeze-out region's temperature extent if a dopant with a larger ionization energy is used (all else equal)?

    1. The freeze-out region shrinks to zero
    2. The freeze-out region stretches to higher temperature
    3. The freeze-out region disappears
    4. There is no freeze-out region for any dopant
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    A larger ionization energy means more thermal energy (higher temperature) is needed to ionize the dopant, stretching the freeze-out region to higher temperature.

    Concept Tested: Freeze-Out Regime

    Question 20

    Which chapter develops the exact, quantitative carrier-concentration equations that make this chapter's relationships precise?

    1. Chapter 9
    2. Chapter 3
    3. Chapter 1
    4. No later chapter builds on this one
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Chapter 9, "Carrier Concentration Statistics," develops the exact Fermi-Dirac and density-of-states equations that make this chapter's relationships quantitatively precise.

    Concept Tested: Doping Concentration
