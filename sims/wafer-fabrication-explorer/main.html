Challenge Questions

1. What does the wire saw's "kerf" represent?

Correct. Kerf is the material width consumed by each saw cut, reducing the number of usable wafers an ingot can yield.

Look at the gaps drawn between slices in step 2.

2. Why is the final CMP (chemical-mechanical polishing) step essential before photolithography?

Correct. As numerical aperture increases and depth of focus shrinks, any part of the wafer outside the focal plane prints poorly — CMP's flatness is what makes fine-feature lithography possible.

Recall how depth of focus shrinks as lithography resolution improves.
