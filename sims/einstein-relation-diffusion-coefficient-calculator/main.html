Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The card shows the computation in sequence: mobility first (from Chapter 11's Matthiessen's rule), then k_BT, then their product D.
  • The curve plots D across the full temperature range at once, with a marker showing the current slider position.
  • Enabling "Compare electrons vs. holes" overlays both carriers' D(T) curves together.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: At room temperature (300 K) and low doping, note the computed diffusion coefficient and compare it to silicon's commonly cited \(D_n\approx35\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s}\).
  • Predict: Before raising the doping concentration, predict whether D will rise or fall, given how mobility responds to doping.
  • Observe: Raise the temperature and watch D increase — both because \(k_BT\) grows directly and because mobility itself changes with temperature.
  • Compare: Enable "Compare electrons vs. holes" and confirm D_n sits above D_p at every temperature, tracking the same electron/hole mobility gap from Chapter 11.

Connects to Chapter 12: The Einstein relation, \(D=\mu k_BT/q\), connects the diffusion coefficient \(D\) directly to Chapter 11's mobility \(\mu\) — both are set by exactly the same scattering physics, since a carrier that scatters less both drifts faster in a field and diffuses faster down a concentration gradient. This is why measuring mobility alone is enough to predict diffusion behavior, without a separate diffusion experiment.

What Each Control Does

Carrier type dropdown
Selects electron or hole diffusion, with its own mobility model (ignored while comparison mode is on).
Compare electrons vs. holes checkbox
Overlays both carriers' D(T) curves together instead of showing one at a time.
Doping slider
Sets N, which affects mobility via impurity scattering.
Temperature slider / 77 K, 300 K, 500 K presets
Set T, which determines mobility (Matthiessen's rule, Chapter 11) and therefore D via the Einstein relation.

Challenge Questions

1. According to the Einstein relation, what two quantities is the diffusion coefficient directly proportional to (at fixed mobility)?

D = μk_BT/q shows D is directly proportional to both mobility μ and absolute temperature T (with q as a fixed constant of proportionality).

Look at the Einstein relation formula shown on screen and identify which quantities appear in the numerator.

2. Predict first: why does D_n exceed D_p at every temperature in comparison mode?

Since D=μk_BT/q and k_BT/q is the same for both carriers at a given T, the entire D_n vs. D_p gap comes directly from μ_n exceeding μ_p — exactly the same mobility gap from Chapter 11's Matthiessen's-rule model.

Compare this sim's D_n/D_p gap to Chapter 11's mobility explorer's electron/hole comparison mode.

Key Takeaway: The Einstein relation means there is no independent "diffusion physics" to learn — every factor that changes mobility (temperature, doping, scattering mechanism, carrier type) changes the diffusion coefficient by exactly the same proportion, scaled by k_BT/q.
