## Easy

### Problem 1

Define, in one sentence each, the difference between a crystal lattice and a crystal structure.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">A crystal lattice is an infinite, purely geometric array of points with no atoms attached. A crystal structure is what results when a basis (one or more atoms) is attached to every point of that lattice.</p>

### Problem 10

Derive the touching condition (relating atomic radius \(r\) to lattice constant \(a\)) for the body-centered cubic structure.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">In BCC, the corner atoms touch the body-center atom along the cube's body diagonal, which has length <span class="arithmatex">\(\sqrt3\,a\)</span>. That diagonal spans a radius from each corner atom plus the full diameter of the body-center atom, four atomic radii in total: <span class="arithmatex">\(4r = \sqrt3\,a\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 15

A metal crystallizes in the FCC structure with atomic radius \(r = 0.128\) nm. Find its lattice constant.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>

### Problem 16

Explain why the zincblende structure lacks the inversion symmetry that the diamond structure has.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">In diamond, every atom is chemically identical, so inverting the crystal through any atom maps it exactly onto itself. In zincblende, the two interpenetrating sublattices hold two different atomic species, so inverting through one species would map its neighbors onto positions occupied by the other species — the crystal is not invariant under inversion, since the two species are not interchangeable.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 17

Derive the packing fraction of the body-centered cubic structure from its touching condition.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">The touching condition is <span class="arithmatex">\(4r=\sqrt3\,a\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(r=\sqrt3\,a/4\)</span>. With 2 atoms per cell, each of volume <span class="arithmatex">\(\tfrac43\pi r^3\)</span>: packing fraction <span class="arithmatex">\(=\dfrac{2\times\tfrac43\pi r^3}{a^3} = \dfrac{\tfrac83\pi\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3\,a}{4}\right)^3}{a^3} = \dfrac{\tfrac83\pi\cdot\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{64}a^3}{a^3} = \dfrac{\sqrt3\,\pi}{8}\approx 0.680\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 18

Gallium arsenide (GaAs) crystallizes in the zincblende structure with lattice constant \(a=0.565\) nm. Estimate its mass density, given that the average atomic mass of the two-atom Ga-As basis is 72.3 u (the average of Ga at 69.7 u and As at 74.9 u), and there are 8 atoms per conventional cell. Use \(N_A=6.022\times10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Cell volume: <span class="arithmatex">\(a^3 = (0.565\times10^{-7}\ \text{cm})^3 = 1.804\times10^{-22}\ \text{cm}^3\)</span>. Mass of 8 atoms at average mass 72.3 u: <span class="arithmatex">\(m = 8\times\dfrac{72.3\ \text{g/mol}}{6.022\times10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}} = 8\times1.201\times10^{-22}\ \text{g} = 9.60\times10^{-22}\ \text{g}\)</span>. Density: <span class="arithmatex">\(\rho = m/a^3 = (9.60\times10^{-22})/(1.804\times10^{-22}) = 5.32\ \text{g/cm}^3\)</span>, close to the accepted value for GaAs of about 5.32 g/cm\(^3\).</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 20

Two crystals have the same atomic radius \(r\). One is simple cubic and the other is face-centered cubic. Find the ratio of their lattice constants, \(a_{\text{FCC}}/a_{\text{SC}}\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">From the touching conditions: <span class="arithmatex">\(a_{\text{SC}}=2r\)</span> and <span class="arithmatex">\(a_{\text{FCC}}=4r/\sqrt2 = 2\sqrt2\,r\)</span>. Ratio: <span class="arithmatex">\(a_{\text{FCC}}/a_{\text{SC}} = 2\sqrt2\,r/(2r) = \sqrt2 \approx 1.414\)</span>. The FCC cell is larger, but it also contains 4 atoms rather than 1, which is why it still achieves a higher packing fraction.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 21

Silicon has lattice constant \(a=0.543\) nm and diamond cubic structure (8 atoms/cell). Using its actual atomic mass of 28.09 u, verify the accepted silicon density of 2.33 g/cm\(^3\) to within 1%, and then estimate what its density would be if it crystallized instead in a simple cubic structure with the same lattice constant.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Diamond cubic: cell volume <span class="arithmatex">\(a^3=1.601\times10^{-22}\ \text{cm}^3\)</span>, mass of 8 atoms <span class="arithmatex">\(=8\times(28.09/6.022\times10^{23})=3.732\times10^{-22}\ \text{g}\)</span>, giving <span class="arithmatex">\(\rho=3.732\times10^{-22}/1.601\times10^{-22}=2.33\ \text{g/cm}^3\)</span>, matching the accepted value within rounding. If the same lattice constant instead described a simple cubic structure with only 1 atom per cell, the mass would be 8 times smaller while the cell volume stays the same, so the density would be <span class="arithmatex">\(2.33/8 \approx 0.29\ \text{g/cm}^3\)</span> — dramatically lower, illustrating how strongly the atoms-per-cell count (and hence the specific crystal structure) determines a material's density at a fixed lattice constant.</p>
</div>
</details>
