What to Observe

  • The gold tangent line's slope at the marker position is exactly \(dp/dx\) — steeper tangent, larger diffusion current.
  • D_p is not a fixed constant here — it is computed live from the Einstein relation using the temperature and doping sliders, so changing T changes J_diffusion even at a fixed gradient.
  • The card shows the full computation chain: μ_p → D_p → dp/dx → J_diffusion, then combined with J_drift into J_total.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Drag the position marker along the concentration curve and watch the tangent line's steepness track the diffusion current readout.
  • Predict: Before shortening the decay length L, predict whether the diffusion current near x=0 will grow or shrink.
  • Observe: Shorten L (steeper decay) and confirm diffusion current increases near the origin, where the concentration gradient is steepest.
  • Try this: Add a nonzero electric field and watch the total current combine both drift and diffusion contributions.
  • Explore: Raise the temperature slider without touching the gradient and confirm J_diffusion changes — a direct consequence of D_p's own temperature dependence via the Einstein relation.

Connects to Chapter 12: Fick's law states that diffusion current is proportional to the local concentration gradient, \(dp/dx\) — steeper gradients drive more current. Combined with Chapter 11's drift current, the total current density at any point is simply the sum, \(J=J_{\text{drift}}+J_{\text{diffusion}}\), the fundamental equation underlying carrier transport in every semiconductor device studied in the chapters ahead. Here, \(D_p\) itself comes from the Einstein relation rather than being assumed fixed, tying this MicroSim directly to the previous section's result.

What Each Control Does

Decay length L slider
Sets how quickly the hole concentration profile decays with position, controlling the concentration gradient's steepness.
Position marker slider
Selects the position at which the local gradient and diffusion current are evaluated.
Electric field slider
Adds a uniform drift current contribution, combined with diffusion into the total.
Temperature and N_A sliders
Set the hole mobility (Matthiessen's rule) and therefore D_p via the Einstein relation.

Challenge Questions

1. Where along the concentration profile is diffusion current largest?

Fick's law makes diffusion current proportional to the gradient dp/dx, not the concentration itself — the steepest part of the curve produces the largest diffusion current.

Drag the position marker to different points and compare the tangent line's steepness to the diffusion current readout.

2. Predict first: if you raise the temperature slider without moving the position marker or changing L, what happens to J_diffusion?

J_diffusion = -qD_p(dp/dx); even with dp/dx fixed, D_p = μ_p·k_BT/q changes with T both directly (the k_BT factor) and indirectly (mobility's own T-dependence), so J_diffusion changes too.

Watch the "D_p = ..." readout below the title as you drag the temperature slider.

Key Takeaway: Diffusion current depends on two independent things — how steep the concentration profile is (geometry) and how easily carriers move through the material (D_p, itself set by temperature and doping through mobility). Changing either one changes J_diffusion, even if the other stays fixed.
