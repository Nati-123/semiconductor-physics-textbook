  • Observe: Switch to "GaAs (Compound)" and see the lattice alternate between two atom types in a checkerboard pattern, with every bond connecting one Ga atom to one As atom.

Connects to Chapter 7: An elemental semiconductor like silicon is built from a single atomic species repeated throughout the crystal. A compound semiconductor like gallium arsenide (GaAs) is built from two different atomic species that alternate on the lattice — every bond connects a Group III atom (Ga) to a Group V atom (As), whose average of 4 valence electrons per atom mimics silicon's own 4 valence electrons.

Challenge Questions

1. In the GaAs lattice, what does every bond connect?

GaAs alternates two species on its lattice sites, so every bond connects one gallium atom to one arsenic atom.

Look at the two alternating colors/labels in the GaAs lattice and see which pairs are always bonded together.

2. What makes silicon an "elemental" semiconductor?

"Elemental" means the crystal is built from a single chemical element — silicon atoms bonded only to other silicon atoms.

Compare how many distinct atom labels appear in the Silicon lattice versus the GaAs lattice.
