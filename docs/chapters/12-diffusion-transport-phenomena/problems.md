### Problem 9

A p-type sample has \(p=1\times10^{15}\ \text{cm}^{-3}=1\times10^{21}\ \text{m}^{-3}\), thickness \(t=250\ \mu\text{m}\), carries \(I=1.5\) mA in field \(B=0.25\) T. Find the Hall voltage.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(R_H=1/(qp)=1/[(1.602\times10^{-19})(1\times10^{21})]\approx6.24\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3/\text{C}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(V_H=R_HIB/t=(6.24\times10^{-3})(1.5\times10^{-3})(0.25)/(2.5\times10^{-4})\approx9.36\times10^{-3}\ \text{V}=9.36\ \text{mV}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 11

### Problem 14

Using \(\mu_n=1000\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V·s}\), \(v_{sat}=1\times10^7\) cm/s, and \(E=2000\) V/cm, compute the naive linear drift velocity and the actual saturating drift velocity, and compare.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Linear: <span class="arithmatex">\(v_d=\mu E=(1000)(2000)=2\times10^6\ \text{cm/s}\)</span>. Saturating: <span class="arithmatex">\(v_d=\dfrac{2\times10^6}{\sqrt{1+(2\times10^6/1\times10^7)^2}}=\dfrac{2\times10^6}{\sqrt{1.04}}\approx1.96\times10^6\ \text{cm/s}\)</span> — about 2% below the linear prediction, still close since the field is well below the saturation regime.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 15

Explain, in terms of scattering, why drift velocity cannot increase without bound as electric field increases.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">As field increases, carriers gain more kinetic energy between collisions, which makes scattering more frequent and more effective at randomizing their motion, offsetting further increases in drift velocity and causing it to level off toward a saturation value instead of growing linearly forever.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 16

Silicon has \(\mu_n\approx1350\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V·s}\) and GaAs has \(\mu_n\approx8500\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V·s}\), both at 300 K. Find the ratio \(D_{n,GaAs}/D_{n,Si}\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Since <span class="arithmatex">\(D=\mu k_BT/q\)</span> and \(k_BT/q\) is the same for both at the same temperature, the ratio of D's equals the ratio of mobilities: <span class="arithmatex">\(8500/1350\approx6.3\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

## Difficult

### Problem 17

A Hall measurement on an unknown-type silicon sample at \(I=3\) mA, \(B=0.4\) T, \(t=180\ \mu\text{m}\) gives \(V_H=-11.2\) mV (using the standard sign convention where positive \(V_H\) indicates p-type). Determine the carrier type, then find the carrier concentration.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">A negative Hall voltage indicates n-type (electron) conduction, where <span class="arithmatex">\(R_H=-1/(qn)\)</span>. Solving <span class="arithmatex">\(R_H=V_Ht/(IB)=(-0.0112)(1.8\times10^{-4})/[(3\times10^{-3})(0.4)]\approx-1.68\times10^{-3}\ \text{m}^3/\text{C}\)</span>. Then <span class="arithmatex">\(n=-1/(qR_H)=-1/[(1.602\times10^{-19})(-1.68\times10^{-3})]\approx3.71\times10^{21}\ \text{m}^{-3}=3.71\times10^{15}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 18

A hole concentration profile is \(p(x)=P_0e^{-x/L}\) with \(P_0=2\times10^{16}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\), \(L=5\ \mu\text{m}\), and \(D_p=12\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s}\). Find the diffusion current density at \(x=0\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Differentiating: <span class="arithmatex">\(dp/dx=-(P_0/L)e^{-x/L}\)</span>. At <span class="arithmatex">\(x=0\)</span>: <span class="arithmatex">\(dp/dx=-P_0/L=-(2\times10^{16})/(5\times10^{-4}\ \text{cm})=-4\times10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-4}\)</span>. Then <span class="arithmatex">\(J_{p,\text{diff}}=-qD_p(dp/dx)=-(1.602\times10^{-19})(12)(-4\times10^{19})\approx76.9\ \text{A/cm}^2\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 19

Using \(\mu_n=1350\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V·s}\) and \(v_{sat}=1\times10^7\) cm/s, find the field at which the actual (saturating) drift velocity is exactly 90% of \(v_{sat}\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Let <span class="arithmatex">\(x=\mu E/v_{sat}\)</span>. Then <span class="arithmatex">\(v_d/v_{sat}=x/\sqrt{1+x^2}=0.9\)</span>. Squaring: <span class="arithmatex">\(x^2=0.81(1+x^2)\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(0.19x^2=0.81\)</span>, <span class="arithmatex">\(x^2\approx4.26\)</span>, <span class="arithmatex">\(x\approx2.06\)</span>. Then <span class="arithmatex">\(E=xv_{sat}/\mu=(2.06)(1\times10^7)/1350\approx1.53\times10^4\ \text{V/cm}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 20

A semiconductor region has both an electric field \(E=300\) V/cm and a hole concentration gradient \(dp/dx=-2\times10^{18}\ \text{cm}^{-4}\) at some point, with \(p=5\times10^{15}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\), \(\mu_p=470\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V·s}\), \(D_p=12\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s}\). Find the total hole current density, and state whether drift or diffusion dominates at this point.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Drift: <span class="arithmatex">\(J_{p,\text{drift}}=qp\mu_pE=(1.602\times10^{-19})(5\times10^{15})(470)(300)\approx1.13\times10^2\ \text{A/cm}^2\)</span>. Diffusion: <span class="arithmatex">\(J_{p,\text{diff}}=-qD_p(dp/dx)=-(1.602\times10^{-19})(12)(-2\times10^{18})\approx3.85\ \text{A/cm}^2\)</span>. Total: <span class="arithmatex">\(J_p\approx113+3.85\approx117\ \text{A/cm}^2\)</span>. Drift dominates heavily here (roughly 97% of the total), since the field and doping combine to produce a much larger drift term than this relatively modest concentration gradient.</p>
</div>
</details>
