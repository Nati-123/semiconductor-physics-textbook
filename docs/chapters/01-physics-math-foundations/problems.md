### Problem 17

Given \(V(x,y,z) = 5x^2y - 2z^3\) (volts), find the electric field vector \(\vec{E} = -\nabla V\) at the point \((1,2,1)\) meters.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(\partial V/\partial x = 10xy\)</span>, <span class="arithmatex">\(\partial V/\partial y = 5x^2\)</span>, <span class="arithmatex">\(\partial V/\partial z=-6z^2\)</span>. At <span class="arithmatex">\((1,2,1)\)</span>: <span class="arithmatex">\(10(1)(2)=20\)</span>, <span class="arithmatex">\(5(1)^2=5\)</span>, <span class="arithmatex">\(-6(1)^2=-6\)</span>. So <span class="arithmatex">\(\vec{E} = -(20\hat{x}+5\hat{y}-6\hat{z})\ \text{V/m} = -20\hat{x}-5\hat{y}+6\hat{z}\ \text{V/m}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 18

Derive, starting from Coulomb's law, the expression for the electric potential \(V(r)\) of a point charge \(q\), showing the integration step explicitly.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Starting from <span class="arithmatex">\(\vec{E} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r^2}\hat{r}\)</span>, integrate along a radial path from infinity to <span class="arithmatex">\(r\)</span>: <span class="arithmatex">\(V(r) = -\int_\infty^r \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l} = -\int_\infty^r \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^2}dr' = \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\dfrac{1}{r'}\right]_\infty^r = \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\dfrac{1}{r}-0\right) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r}\)</span>, matching the boxed formula in the Electric Potential subsection.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 19

A particle has average kinetic energy \(\langle KE\rangle = 0.05\) eV. Estimate the temperature in kelvin using the kinetic theory of gases result \(\langle KE\rangle = \tfrac{3}{2}k_BT\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(T = \dfrac{2\langle KE\rangle}{3k_B}\)</span>. Converting <span class="arithmatex">\(0.05\)</span> eV to joules: <span class="arithmatex">\(0.05\times1.602\times10^{-19}=8.01\times10^{-21}\ \text{J}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(T = \dfrac{2(8.01\times10^{-21})}{3(1.381\times10^{-23})} \approx 387\ \text{K}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

Compute the divergence of the vector field \(\vec{F} = x^2\hat{x} + y^2\hat{y} + z^2\hat{z}\) and evaluate it at the point \((1,1,1)\).

### Problem 21

Using Euler's formula, show that \(\cos\theta = \dfrac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Euler's formula gives <span class="arithmatex">\(e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\)</span> and <span class="arithmatex">\(e^{-i\theta}=\cos\theta-i\sin\theta\)</span>. Adding: <span class="arithmatex">\(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}=2\cos\theta\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(\cos\theta = \dfrac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}\)</span>, as required.</p>
</div>
</details>
