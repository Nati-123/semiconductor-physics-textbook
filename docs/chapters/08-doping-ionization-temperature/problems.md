### Problem 11

Explain why device engineers prefer to operate circuits in the extrinsic temperature region rather than the freeze-out or intrinsic regions.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">

### Problem 12

Silicon's effective conduction-band density of states is \(N_C\approx2.8\times10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\). Is a sample doped at \(N_D=1\times10^{18}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\) degenerate? Justify using the preview formula.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Not degenerate. <span class="arithmatex">\(N_C/N_D=2.8\times10^{19}/1\times10^{18}=28\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(\ln(28)\approx3.3\)</span>, giving <span class="arithmatex">\(E_C-E_F\approx(0.0259)(3.3)\approx0.086\)</span> eV — the Fermi level is still comfortably below E_C, in the non-degenerate regime.</p>

### Problem 17

Using the simplified ionization-fraction model \(f_{ion}(T)=1/(1+B\,e^{E_D/k_BT})\) with \(B=0.02\), find the temperature at which \(f_{ion}=0.5\) for a dopant with \(E_D=0.06\) eV, using \(k_B=8.617\times10^{-5}\) eV/K.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>

A sample is doped with \(N_D=6\times10^{16}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\) and \(N_A=6\times10^{16}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\) but is later found to have a small measurable net p-type carrier concentration of \(2\times10^{14}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\). ### Problem 19

Explain, using the degenerate-regime preview formula, why lowering temperature (at fixed \(N_D\) well below \(N_C\)) makes a sample *more* likely to appear degenerate by this formula, even though physically it does not become degenerate at low temperature. What does this reveal about the formula's limitations?

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">The formula <span class="arithmatex">\(E_C-E_F=k_BT\ln(N_C/N_D)\)</span> has k_BT as a multiplicative prefactor, so lowering T shrinks E_C−E_F toward zero even if N_C/N_D itself does not change — misleadingly suggesting the Fermi level approaches E_C. This reveals that the formula (and the N_C value itself, which is also temperature-dependent) is only strictly valid near the temperature it was derived for, and freeze-out (not degeneracy) is the actual dominant effect at low temperature for non-degenerate doping levels — a reminder that this chapter's formulas are previews, not the full quantitative treatment Chapter 10 provides.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 20

A device is operated across a wide temperature range and must remain in the extrinsic temperature region throughout. Explain qualitatively how the required doping concentration \(N_D\) should be chosen relative to the maximum operating temperature, and relative to the minimum operating temperature, to keep the device extrinsic across the whole range.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">To stay extrinsic at the maximum operating temperature, N_D must be chosen large enough that the intrinsic carrier concentration at that temperature still remains well below N_D (raising N_D pushes the intrinsic region's onset to a higher temperature). To stay extrinsic (not frozen out) at the minimum operating temperature, N_D and the dopant's ionization energy must be chosen so that the freeze-out region's upper boundary is still below the minimum operating temperature (a lower ionization-energy dopant, or a not-too-heavy doping level, helps keep freeze-out confined to temperatures below the operating range). Both constraints together bound the usable doping concentration and dopant choice for a given temperature range.</p>
</div>
</details>
