    End-of-Chapter Problems: Physics and Math Foundations

    Work through these problems to reinforce your understanding of the physics and mathematics foundations from Chapter 1.

    Easy

    Problem 1

    Convert \(90^\circ\) to radians.

    Show Solution

    \(90^\circ \times (\pi/180^\circ) = \pi/2\ \text{rad} \approx 1.571\ \text{rad}\).

    Problem 2

    State Newton's second law and identify the SI unit of each quantity in it.

    Show Solution

    \(\vec{F}=m\vec{a}\); force in newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg), acceleration in meters per second squared (m/s²).

    Problem 3

    What is the SI unit of electric potential, and what two quantities define it?

    Show Solution

    Volt (V); defined as potential energy per unit charge, \(V = U/q\).

    Problem 4

    Write the value of the elementary charge \(e\) in coulombs.

    Show Solution

    \(e = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \text{C}\).

    Problem 5

    Simplify \(e^{3}\cdot e^{-5}\).

    Show Solution

    \(e^{3}\cdot e^{-5} = e^{3-5} = e^{-2} \approx 0.135\).

    Problem 6

    What is the complex conjugate of \(z = 2-5i\)?

    Show Solution

    \(z^{*} = 2+5i\).

    Problem 7

    Differentiate \(f(x) = 7x^3\).

    Show Solution

    \(f'(x) = 21x^2\).

    Problem 8

    State Coulomb's law in words (no equation).

    Show Solution

    The electrostatic force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them, acting along the line joining them.

    Medium

    Problem 9

    Two charges of \(+4\) nC and \(+4\) nC are separated by 3 nm. Find the electrostatic force between them.

    Show Solution

    \(F = (8.99\times10^9)\dfrac{(4\times10^{-9})^2}{(3\times10^{-9})^2} = 1.60\times10^{-2}\ \text{N}\) (repulsive, both positive).

    Problem 10

    A test charge of \(+1.5\times10^{-19}\) C experiences a force of \(6.0\times10^{-15}\) N in an electric field. Find the field magnitude.

    Show Solution

    \(E = F/q = (6.0\times10^{-15})/(1.5\times10^{-19}) = 4.0\times10^{4}\ \text{V/m}\).

    Problem 11

    Solve \(50 = e^{x/0.05}\) for \(x\).

    Show Solution

    \(\ln(50) = x/0.05 \Rightarrow x = 0.05\times3.912 = 0.1956\).

    Problem 12

    Find \(\partial f/\partial x\) and \(\partial f/\partial y\) for \(f(x,y) = x^3y^2 + 4y\).

    Show Solution

    \(\partial f/\partial x = 3x^2y^2\); \(\partial f/\partial y = 2x^3y+4\).

    Problem 13

    Evaluate \(\int_0^{2} 3x^2\,dx\).

    Show Solution

    \(\int_0^2 3x^2\,dx = \big[x^3\big]_0^2 = 8-0 = 8\).

    Problem 14

    Write \(z = -1 + i\) in polar form.

    Show Solution

    \(r=\sqrt{(-1)^2+1^2}=\sqrt2\); \(\theta = 135^\circ = 3\pi/4\ \text{rad}\) (second quadrant). So \(z=\sqrt2\,e^{i3\pi/4}\).

    Problem 15

    A Gaussian sphere of radius 1 nm encloses a charge of \(-2e\). Find the total flux through the sphere.

    Show Solution

    \(\Phi_E = Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0 = (-2\times1.602\times10^{-19})/(8.854\times10^{-12}) = -3.62\times10^{-8}\ \text{V·m}\).

    Problem 16

    Find the photon energy, in eV, corresponding to a wavelength of 1100 nm. Is this photon energy above or below silicon's 1.12 eV band gap?

    Show Solution

    \(E = hc/\lambda = (6.626\times10^{-34})(2.998\times10^8)/(1100\times10^{-9}) = 1.81\times10^{-19}\ \text{J} = 1.13\ \text{eV}\), just above silicon's 1.12 eV band gap (so this photon would still be absorbed, barely).

    Difficult

    Problem 17

    Given \(V(x,y,z) = 5x^2y - 2z^3\) (volts), find the electric field vector \(\vec{E} = -\nabla V\) at the point \((1,2,1)\) meters.

    Show Solution

    \(\partial V/\partial x = 10xy\), \(\partial V/\partial y = 5x^2\), \(\partial V/\partial z=-6z^2\). At \((1,2,1)\): \(10(1)(2)=20\), \(5(1)^2=5\), \(-6(1)^2=-6\). So \(\vec{E} = -(20\hat{x}+5\hat{y}-6\hat{z})\ \text{V/m} = -20\hat{x}-5\hat{y}+6\hat{z}\ \text{V/m}\).

    Problem 18

    Derive, starting from Coulomb's law, the expression for the electric potential \(V(r)\) of a point charge \(q\), showing the integration step explicitly.

    Show Solution

    Starting from \(\vec{E} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r^2}\hat{r}\), integrate along a radial path from infinity to \(r\): \(V(r) = -\int_\infty^r \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{l} = -\int_\infty^r \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r'^2}dr' = \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\dfrac{1}{r'}\right]_\infty^r = \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left(\dfrac{1}{r}-0\right) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r}\), matching the boxed formula in the Electric Potential subsection.

    Problem 19

    A particle has average kinetic energy \(\langle KE\rangle = 0.05\) eV. Estimate the temperature in kelvin using the kinetic theory of gases result \(\langle KE\rangle = \tfrac{3}{2}k_BT\).

    Show Solution

    \(T = \dfrac{2\langle KE\rangle}{3k_B}\). Converting \(0.05\) eV to joules: \(0.05\times1.602\times10^{-19}=8.01\times10^{-21}\ \text{J}\). \(T = \dfrac{2(8.01\times10^{-21})}{3(1.381\times10^{-23})} \approx 387\ \text{K}\).

    Problem 20

    Compute the divergence of the vector field \(\vec{F} = x^2\hat{x} + y^2\hat{y} + z^2\hat{z}\) and evaluate it at the point \((1,1,1)\).

    Show Solution

    \(\nabla\cdot\vec{F} = \partial(x^2)/\partial x + \partial(y^2)/\partial y + \partial(z^2)/\partial z = 2x+2y+2z\). At \((1,1,1)\): \(2+2+2=6\).

    Problem 21

    Using Euler's formula, show that \(\cos\theta = \dfrac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}\).

    Show Solution

    Euler's formula gives \(e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\) and \(e^{-i\theta}=\cos\theta-i\sin\theta\). Adding: \(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}=2\cos\theta\), so \(\cos\theta = \dfrac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}\), as required.
