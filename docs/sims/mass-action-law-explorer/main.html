Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Start with the n₀ slider at the intrinsic point, where both n₀ and p₀ equal nᵢ, and confirm the marker sits exactly on the diagonal.
  • Predict: Before dragging n₀ up by several orders of magnitude, predict what will happen to p₀.
  • Observe: Drag n₀ across its full range and watch p₀ shrink correspondingly, always keeping the marker on the same hyperbola.

Connects to Chapter 9: Multiplying the non-degenerate electron and hole concentration formulas together, \(n_0p_0=N_Ce^{-(E_C-E_F)/k_BT}\times N_Ve^{-(E_F-E_V)/k_BT}=N_CN_Ve^{-E_g/k_BT}\), the Fermi level \(E_F\) cancels out completely — the product depends only on temperature and material, not on doping. Since this product equals \(n_i^2\) by definition, the result is the mass action law, \(n_0p_0=n_i^2\), true in any non-degenerate semiconductor at thermal equilibrium regardless of doping level.

What Each Control Does

n₀ slider (log scale)
Sets the electron concentration; the hole concentration p₀ is computed automatically to keep n₀p₀ = nᵢ² fixed.
Material/temperature dropdown
Selects the intrinsic carrier concentration nᵢ used to fix the hyperbola.

Challenge Questions

1. If doping raises n₀ far above nᵢ, what happens to p₀ according to the mass action law?

Since n₀p₀ = nᵢ² is a fixed product at a given temperature, raising n₀ (majority carrier, via donor doping) forces p₀ (minority carrier) to fall proportionally — this is exactly how doping suppresses the minority carrier concentration.

Drag n₀ up and watch how p₀ changes to keep the product constant.

2. Where in the mass action law derivation does the Fermi level E_F go?

n₀ contains e^(-(Ec-EF)/kT) and p₀ contains e^(-(EF-Ev)/kT); multiplying combines these exponents into e^(-(Ec-Ev)/kT) = e^(-Eg/kT), with E_F cancelling exactly — which is why the mass action law holds regardless of doping (which sets E_F).

Look at the exponents in n₀ and p₀'s formulas and add them together algebraically.
