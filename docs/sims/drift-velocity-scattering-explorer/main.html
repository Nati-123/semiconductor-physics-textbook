Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Set the electric field slider to 0, click Start, and watch the electron zig-zag randomly with no net direction.
  • Predict: Before raising the electric field, predict whether the zig-zag pattern will disappear or just gain an overall bias.
  • Observe: Raise the electric field and watch the random zig-zag path develop a clear rightward drift, while the scattering events (direction changes) keep happening at the same rate.

Connects to Chapter 11: An electron in a crystal is constantly scattering off lattice vibrations and impurity atoms, changing direction randomly many times per second — with no applied field, this random motion averages to zero net displacement. An applied electric field superimposes a small, steady drift velocity on top of this much larger random thermal motion. The ratio of this drift velocity to the field strength is the carrier's mobility, \(\mu = v_d/E\) — a single number that hides all the messy scattering detail.

What Each Control Does

Electric field slider
Sets the strength of the applied field, biasing the random walk toward the right.
Start/Stop button
Starts or pauses the animation. The sim loads paused so the initial (undisturbed) lattice can be inspected first.
Reset button
Returns the electron to the center and clears the accumulated drift measurement.

Challenge Questions

1. With the electric field slider at zero, what is the electron's average net displacement over time?

With no applied field, each random scattering event is equally likely to send the electron in any direction, so the net displacement averages to approximately zero over time.

Watch the electron's path with the field at zero for several seconds and see where it ends up relative to where it started.

2. What does mobility (μ = v_d/E) represent physically in this MicroSim?

Mobility captures how much net drift velocity results from a given field strength — a high-scattering environment (frequent direction changes) gives low mobility, since the field has little time to build up drift between collisions.

Compare the drift velocity readout to the field slider value and see what their ratio represents.
