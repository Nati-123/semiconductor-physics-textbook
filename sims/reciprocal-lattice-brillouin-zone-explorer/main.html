Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: In "Real vs. Reciprocal Lattice" view, drag the a_x slider from small to large and watch the reciprocal lattice (right panel) compress as the real lattice (left panel) expands.
  • Predict: Before unchecking "Square lattice," predict what shape the reciprocal lattice will become if you stretch a_x much larger than a_y in real space.
  • Observe: Uncheck "Square lattice," set a_x much larger than a_y, and confirm the reciprocal lattice points become widely spaced in the x-direction but closely spaced in y — the inverse relationship applies independently along each axis.
  • Switch views: Select "Brillouin Zone" and watch the shaded first-zone rectangle resize as you adjust a_x and a_y — note it always extends exactly half the reciprocal spacing in each direction.
  • Explain: Explain why the dashed perpendicular-bisector lines, not the reciprocal lattice points themselves, are what actually define the Brillouin zone boundary.

Connects to Chapter 5: This MicroSim visualizes the reciprocal lattice (spacing \(2\pi/a\) along each axis) constructed from a real-space lattice (Chapter 3), and the first Brillouin zone as the Wigner-Seitz primitive cell of that reciprocal lattice — the same k-space region whose boundary, \(k=\pm\pi/a\) in 1D, is exactly where the Kronig-Penney model's band gaps open.

What Each Control Does

View dropdown
Switches between "Real vs. Reciprocal Lattice" (side-by-side comparison) and "Brillouin Zone" (Wigner-Seitz construction).
a_x slider
Sets the real-space lattice constant along x; the reciprocal lattice spacing along x updates as \(2\pi/a_x\).
a_y slider
Sets the real-space lattice constant along y (only active when "Square lattice" is unchecked).
Square lattice checkbox
When checked, locks a_y to always match a_x, showing a square lattice; uncheck to explore a rectangular lattice independently along each axis.

Challenge Questions

1. As the real-space lattice constant a_x increases, what happens to the reciprocal lattice spacing along x?

Reciprocal lattice spacing is \(2\pi/a\), so a larger real-space lattice constant produces a smaller (more closely spaced) reciprocal lattice — an inverse relationship.

Drag the a_x slider to its largest value and compare the reciprocal-lattice spacing in the right panel to its value at the smallest a_x.

2. What construction defines the boundary of the first Brillouin zone shown in "Brillouin Zone" view?

The Brillouin zone is the Wigner-Seitz cell of the reciprocal lattice — the region enclosed by perpendicular bisector planes to the nearest reciprocal lattice points, shown as dashed lines in this MicroSim.

Switch to "Brillouin Zone" view and look at the dashed lines connecting to the shaded rectangle.

3. If a_x is stretched much larger than a_y in real space, what shape does the Brillouin zone become?

Because reciprocal spacing is inversely proportional to real-space spacing along each axis independently, stretching a_x (making it large) shrinks the reciprocal spacing along k_x, making the Brillouin zone narrow in k_x and, by comparison, tall in k_y.

Uncheck "Square lattice," set a_x much larger than a_y, and switch to "Brillouin Zone" view to see the resulting rectangle's proportions.
