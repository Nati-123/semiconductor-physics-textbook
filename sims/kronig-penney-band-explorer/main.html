Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: In "Transcendental Equation" view, drag the barrier strength P slider down to 0 and observe that all the red shaded (forbidden) regions disappear.
  • Predict: Before increasing P from 0 to 4, predict whether the allowed bands (unshaded regions) will widen or narrow.
  • Observe: Increase P and watch the red forbidden regions widen around each dashed Brillouin-zone-boundary line, while the allowed (unshaded) bands narrow.
  • Switch views: Select "E-k Diagram" and confirm that every gap in the plotted band curve lines up exactly with a dashed Brillouin zone boundary line.
  • Explain: Explain, using both views together, why "band gaps open at the Brillouin zone boundary" is the same physical statement seen two different ways.

Connects to Chapter 5: This MicroSim plots the Kronig-Penney transcendental equation, \(P\sin(\alpha a)/(\alpha a) + \cos(\alpha a) = \cos(ka)\), directly. Because \(\cos(ka)\) can only take values in \([-1,1]\), any energy where the left-hand side exceeds that range has no real-\(k\) solution — a band gap — and the E-k Diagram view shows these same gaps appearing exactly at the Brillouin zone boundaries, \(k=\pm n\pi/a\).

What Each Control Does

View dropdown
Switches between "Transcendental Equation" (the raw equation and shaded forbidden regions) and "E-k Diagram" (the resulting energy bands plotted against the Bloch wavevector).
Barrier strength P slider
Sets the Kronig-Penney barrier strength; P=0 is the free-electron limit (no gaps), and larger P widens the forbidden gaps.

Challenge Questions

1. What does a red-shaded region in "Transcendental Equation" view represent?

Red shading marks energies where the left-hand side of the transcendental equation falls outside \([-1,1]\), meaning no real Bloch wavevector k can satisfy the equation — a forbidden band gap.

Look at where the purple curve crosses above the "+1" or below the "−1" reference lines.

2. What happens to the shaded forbidden regions as you drag P down to 0?

At P=0, the periodic potential vanishes entirely, and the transcendental equation reduces to \(\cos(x)=\cos(ka)\), which always has a real solution — no forbidden regions remain.

Drag the P slider all the way to its minimum value and observe the shaded regions.

3. In "E-k Diagram" view, where do the gaps in the plotted band curve occur?

The plotted band structure always has gaps precisely at the dashed Brillouin zone boundary lines — this is the chapter's key geometric result connecting band formation to the reciprocal lattice.

Zoom in (mentally) on where a dashed vertical line crosses the band curve and see whether dots are missing right at that k value.
