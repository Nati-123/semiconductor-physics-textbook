Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Switch from full-wave to half-wave and watch the negative half-cycles of the output disappear entirely, along with roughly half the average DC output.
  • Predict: Before raising VF, predict whether V_DC will increase or decrease.
  • Observe: The full-wave output never touches zero except at brief instants, while the half-wave output sits at zero for half of every cycle — a much "choppier" waveform.

Connects to Chapter 18: A power diode is a p-n junction engineered for high voltage and current handling; a rectifier circuit arranges one or more power diodes to convert AC to DC, with full-wave bridge rectification giving roughly twice the average output of half-wave rectification for the same input.

What Each Control Does

Type
Half-wave (one diode, passes one polarity) or full-wave bridge (four diodes, uses both polarities).
Vpeak
Peak AC input voltage.
VF
Forward voltage drop per diode, subtracted from the output.

Challenge Questions

1. Roughly how does full-wave V_DC compare to half-wave V_DC for the same input?

Correct. Full-wave rectification uses both half-cycles of the AC input instead of discarding one, roughly doubling the average output.

Compare the VDC formulas for each type — one has an extra factor of 2 in the numerator.

2. Why does a full-wave bridge rectifier subtract 2×VF instead of 1×VF from the peak voltage?

Correct. In a four-diode bridge, current always passes through two diodes in series (one on each side of the load), so two forward drops are subtracted.

Think about the current path through a four-diode bridge rectifier — how many diodes does current pass through in series at any instant?
