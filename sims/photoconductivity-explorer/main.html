Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Double the lifetime τ and confirm Δσ exactly doubles, then double G instead and confirm the same doubling.
  • Predict: Before raising G by a factor of 100 (two decades on the slider), predict how many decades Δσ will move on the log-log chart.
  • Observe: The photoresistor bar's glow brightens smoothly as Δσ increases, giving a visual sense of scale alongside the numeric readout.

Connects to Chapter 17: Photoconductivity is the conductivity increase \(\Delta\sigma=q(\Delta n\mu_n+\Delta p\mu_p)\) produced by illumination, using the same steady-state excess carrier relation \(\Delta n=\Delta p=G\tau\) from Chapter 13. A photoconductor uses this effect directly as a light sensor.

What Each Control Does

G
Generation rate (illumination intensity), directly proportional to Δn.
τ
Minority carrier lifetime, also directly proportional to Δn.

Challenge Questions

1. If both G and τ are doubled simultaneously, what happens to Δσ?

Correct. Δn = Gτ, so doubling both G and τ quadruples Δn, and Δσ is directly proportional to Δn.

Write out Δn = G·τ — what happens to a product when both factors double?

2. What physical quantity does a photoconductor (light sensor) actually measure to infer light intensity?

Correct. A photoconductor is just a semiconductor slab with ohmic contacts; illumination changes its resistance, which is measured to infer light intensity.

Recall that a photoconductor is described as "simply a slab of semiconductor with two ohmic contacts" — what electrical property changes under illumination?
