Connects to Chapter 7: Column position (Group) directly predicts an element's role in semiconductor physics. Group IV elements (Si, Ge) have exactly 4 valence electrons and can bond purely covalently with themselves, forming elemental semiconductors. Group III elements (B, Al, Ga, In) have one fewer valence electron and act as acceptor dopants when substituted into a Group IV lattice; Group V elements (P, As, Sb) have one more and act as donor dopants. Pairing a Group III atom with a Group V atom (as in GaAs) — or a Group II atom with a Group VI atom (as in CdTe) — produces a compound semiconductor whose average valence electron count still matches silicon's.

Challenge Questions

1. Which group's elements act as donor dopants when substituted into silicon?

Group V elements (P, As, Sb) have one extra valence electron compared to silicon's 4, making them donor dopants.

2. Which pair of groups combine to form III-V compound semiconductors like GaAs?

A Group III atom and a Group V atom together average 4 valence electrons per atom, matching silicon — exactly what GaAs, InP, and other III-V compounds do.
