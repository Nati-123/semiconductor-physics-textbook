Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The purple-shaded region (n₀ > nᵢ) is the n-type side of the hyperbola; the gold-shaded region (n₀ < nᵢ) is the p-type side — the marker always sits on the hyperbola, never inside either shaded band.
  • The green dot marks the intrinsic point, where n₀ = p₀ = nᵢ exactly — this is where the hyperbola crosses the dashed n₀=p₀ diagonal.
  • Switching material or temperature moves the entire hyperbola (a different nᵢ), but the marker's log₁₀n₀+log₁₀p₀ sum on that curve is always log₁₀(nᵢ²).

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Start with the n₀ slider at the intrinsic point (drag until the marker overlaps the green dot) and confirm both n₀ and p₀ read out as equal to nᵢ.
  • Predict: Before dragging n₀ up by several orders of magnitude, predict what will happen to p₀ and whether the marker will move into the n-type or p-type shaded region.
  • Observe: Drag n₀ across its full range and watch p₀ shrink correspondingly, always keeping the marker on the same hyperbola.
  • Compare: Switch material from Silicon to Germanium at the same temperature and notice the entire hyperbola shifts — germanium's larger nᵢ moves the intrinsic point up and to the right.

Connects to Chapter 9: Multiplying the non-degenerate electron and hole concentration formulas together, \(n_0p_0=N_Ce^{-(E_C-E_F)/k_BT}\times N_Ve^{-(E_F-E_V)/k_BT}=N_CN_Ve^{-E_g/k_BT}\), the Fermi level \(E_F\) cancels out completely — the product depends only on temperature and material, not on doping. Since this product equals \(n_i^2\) by definition, the result is the mass action law, \(n_0p_0=n_i^2\), true in any non-degenerate semiconductor at thermal equilibrium regardless of doping level.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects Si, Ge, or GaAs, which sets the band gap and effective masses used to compute nᵢ.
Temperature slider
Sets T, recomputing nᵢ (and therefore the whole hyperbola) live via the same physics as the Effective Density of States Explorer.
n₀ slider (log scale)
Sets the electron concentration; the hole concentration p₀ is computed automatically to keep n₀p₀ = nᵢ² fixed.

Challenge Questions

1. If doping raises n₀ far above nᵢ, what happens to p₀ according to the mass action law?

Since n₀p₀ = nᵢ² is a fixed product at a given temperature, raising n₀ (majority carrier, via donor doping) forces p₀ (minority carrier) to fall proportionally — this is exactly how doping suppresses the minority carrier concentration.

Drag n₀ up and watch how p₀ changes to keep the product constant.

2. Where in the mass action law derivation does the Fermi level E_F go?

n₀ contains e^(-(Ec-EF)/kT) and p₀ contains e^(-(EF-Ev)/kT); multiplying combines these exponents into e^(-(Ec-Ev)/kT) = e^(-Eg/kT), with E_F cancelling exactly — which is why the mass action law holds regardless of doping (which sets E_F).

Look at the exponents in n₀ and p₀'s formulas and add them together algebraically.

3. Predict first: if you switch from Silicon to GaAs at the same temperature and leave n₀ unchanged, what happens to the marker?

GaAs's larger band gap gives it a much smaller nᵢ than silicon at the same T, so nᵢ² is smaller and the entire hyperbola shifts down — with n₀ held fixed, p₀ = nᵢ²/n₀ must decrease. Try it: set n₀ to a fixed value, switch material, and read the new p₀.

Switch the material dropdown from Silicon to GaAs without touching the n₀ slider, and compare the p₀ readout before and after.

Key Takeaway: The mass action law is a hyperbola, not a line: n₀ and p₀ trade off multiplicatively, not additively. Doping moves the marker along a fixed curve (majority carrier up, minority carrier down by the same factor), while changing material or temperature moves the entire curve by changing nᵢ² itself.
