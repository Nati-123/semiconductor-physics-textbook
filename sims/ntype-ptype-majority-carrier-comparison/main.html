Try This

  • Try this: Compare the two lattices side by side before checking the ionized-state box — one has a donor (blue), the other an acceptor (red).
  • Observe: Check "Show Ionized State" and see the donor side release a free electron while the acceptor side's hole moves away, leaving a fixed positive ion on the left and a fixed negative ion on the right.

Connects to Chapter 7: A silicon crystal doped with donor atoms is called n-type material because its majority carriers are negatively-charged free electrons; a crystal doped with acceptor atoms is called p-type material because its majority carriers are positively-charged holes. Chapter 8 develops n-type and p-type doping in full quantitative detail — this comparison previews the essential physical picture side by side.

What Each Control Does

Show Ionized State checkbox
Toggles both lattices between their neutral (freshly-doped) state and their ionized state, where the donor's electron and the acceptor's hole have become free carriers.

Challenge Questions

1. What is the majority carrier in n-type (donor-doped) material?

Donor atoms contribute free electrons, so n-type ("n" for negative) material's majority carriers are electrons.

Look at the carrier released on the donor (left) side of the comparison.
