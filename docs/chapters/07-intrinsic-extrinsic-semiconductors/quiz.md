3. A semiconductor with no band gap
4. A metal with semiconductor-like properties
</div>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">An intrinsic semiconductor is chemically pure, so its only carriers come from rare, thermally-broken covalent bonds.</p>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75;">Why does breaking a covalent bond in an intrinsic semiconductor always create exactly one electron and one hole?</p>

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>

2. Because two separate bonds must break simultaneously
3. It does not — sometimes only an electron is created

</div>

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">

<p style="color: #2E7D32; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 0;">Correct Answer: A</p>
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">A broken bond and the vacancy it leaves are two views of the same event, so intrinsic generation always creates one electron and one hole together.</p>
