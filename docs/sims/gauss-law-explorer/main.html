What Each Control Does

Radius slider
Sets the Gaussian surface's radius — this changes \(E\) at the surface, but never changes the total flux, as long as the charge stays enclosed.
Charge slider
Sets the enclosed charge \(q\) — flux scales directly and only with this value, exactly as Gauss's Law predicts.
Move Charge Outside Surface button
Relocates the charge outside the Gaussian surface, to demonstrate that only enclosed charge contributes to flux.

Challenge Questions

1. If you double the Gaussian surface's radius while the charge stays enclosed, what happens to the flux \(\Phi\)?

Gauss's Law says \(\Phi=Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0\), which has no \(r\) in it at all — flux depends only on enclosed charge.

Try it directly: note the flux at r=2.5, then double r to 5 and compare.

2. Using the same radius doubling, what happens to the electric field \(E\) at the surface?

\(E=k|q|/r^2\), so doubling r divides E by 4 — even though flux stays constant, the field spreads over 4x the surface area.

Read the "E at surface" value at r=2.5 and again at r=5.

3. If the charge is moved outside the Gaussian surface, what is the flux?

With no charge enclosed, \(Q_{\text{enc}}=0\), so \(\Phi=0/\varepsilon_0=0\) — field lines that enter the surface on one side must also exit it.

Click the toggle button and read the flux readout.

4. If you double the enclosed charge \(q\) (charge still inside), what happens to the flux?

\(\Phi=Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0\) is directly proportional to charge, so doubling \(q\) exactly doubles the flux.

Try it: note the flux at q=3, then set q=6 (if the slider allows) or compare ratios directly.

5. Why is flux unaffected by the Gaussian surface's radius, as long as the charge stays enclosed?

This is exactly what the boxed Gauss's Law equation states — the right-hand side, \(Q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0\), contains no reference to the surface's size or shape at all.

Look again at the boxed Gauss's Law equation in the chapter — what variables does it actually contain?
