Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Double IB on the BJT panel and confirm IC exactly doubles; double Vov on the MOSFET panel and confirm ID quadruples.
  • Predict: Before raising β, predict what happens to the slope of the BJT line.
  • Observe: The BJT curve is a straight line through the origin; the MOSFET curve is a parabola — visually different control laws for two very different physical mechanisms.

Connects to Chapter 18: Bipolar transistor basics describe current amplification, \(I_C=\beta I_B\), from minority carrier injection across two adjacent junctions (Chapter 15). MOSFET basics describe voltage-controlled channel conduction, \(I_D=(\mu_nC_{ox}/2)(W/L)(V_{GS}-V_T)^2\), built from the MOS capacitor and inversion layer of Chapter 16.

What Each Control Does

β, IB
BJT current gain and base current, setting collector current linearly.
Vov, W/L
MOSFET gate overdrive and geometry ratio, setting drain current quadratically (in overdrive) and linearly (in W/L).

Challenge Questions

1. If BJT base current doubles, what happens to collector current?

Correct. IC = β·IB is linear in IB, so doubling IB exactly doubles IC for fixed β.

Look at the shape of the BJT chart — is it a straight line or a curve?

2. If MOSFET gate overdrive voltage doubles, what happens to drain current?

Correct. ID ∝ (VGS−VT)², so doubling the overdrive voltage quadruples drain current.

Look at the shape of the MOSFET chart — it is a parabola, not a straight line.
