Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Sweep injection current from 0 to 100 mA and watch the curve's slope change sharply at the threshold line.
  • Predict: Before crossing 40 mA, predict whether output power will rise faster or slower than below threshold.
  • Observe: Below threshold, power rises very slowly (spontaneous emission); above threshold, power rises steeply and linearly (stimulated emission).

Connects to Chapter 20: The laser diode extends Chapter 17's LED spontaneous emission with stimulated emission once a population inversion builds up above threshold current.

Challenge Questions

1. What causes the sharp change in slope at the threshold current?

Correct. Once a population inversion is established above threshold, stimulated emission dominates, producing a much steeper power-current slope.

Recall what physically changes about the carrier population once threshold current is exceeded.

2. Below threshold current, a laser diode behaves most like which device covered in Chapter 17?

Correct. Below threshold, output is dominated by spontaneous emission, the same mechanism behind an LED.

Consider which Chapter 17 device relies purely on spontaneous recombination.
