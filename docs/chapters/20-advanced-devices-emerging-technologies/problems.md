### Problem 17

A silicon power device and a GaN power device are both rated at \(V_{BR}=900\ \text{V}\). Silicon: \(\mu_n=1350\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V}\cdot\text{s}\), \(E_{crit}=3\times10^5\ \text{V/cm}\), \(\varepsilon_s=1.035\times10^{-12}\ \text{F/cm}\). GaN: \(\mu_n=1250\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V}\cdot\text{s}\), \(E_{crit}=3.3\times10^6\ \text{V/cm}\), \(\varepsilon_s=0.855\times10^{-12}\ \text{F/cm}\). Find the ratio of silicon's specific on-resistance to GaN's.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Silicon: <span class="arithmatex">\(R_{on,sp}=4(900)^2/[(1350)(1.035\times10^{-12})(3\times10^5)^3]\approx0.0918\ \Omega\cdot\text{cm}^2\)</span>. GaN: <span class="arithmatex">\(R_{on,sp}=4(900)^2/[(1250)(0.855\times10^{-12})(3.3\times10^6)^3]\approx8.83\times10^{-5}\ \Omega\cdot\text{cm}^2\)</span>. Ratio: <span class="arithmatex">\(0.0918/8.83\times10^{-5}\approx1040\)</span> — silicon needs roughly 1000 times more specific on-resistance area to match GaN's performance at the same voltage.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 20

A GAA transistor (\(n=4\)) and a FinFET (\(n=2\)) use identical \(t_{si}\) and \(t_{ox}\). If the FinFET can be scaled to a minimum gate length of 20 nm before short-channel effects become unacceptable, estimate the GAA transistor's corresponding minimum gate length, assuming minimum gate length scales proportionally with natural length \(\lambda\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Ratio of natural lengths: <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda_{GAA}/\lambda_{FinFET}=\sqrt{n_{FinFET}/n_{GAA}}=\sqrt{2/4}=\sqrt{0.5}\approx0.707\)</span>. Minimum gate length: <span class="arithmatex">\(20\ \text{nm}\times0.707\approx14.1\ \text{nm}\)</span> — the GAA structure's stronger electrostatic control allows roughly 30% further gate-length scaling before short-channel effects become equally severe.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 21

Explain, connecting Chapters 2, 17, and 20, why a quantum dot's emission wavelength cannot be predicted from its bulk material's band gap alone.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Chapter 17's LED wavelength formula <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda=hc/E_g\)</span> assumes the bulk band gap alone sets emission energy. A quantum dot adds Chapter 2's particle-in-a-box confinement energy <span class="arithmatex">\(E_n=n^2h^2/(8mL^2)\)</span> on top of the bulk band gap, so the effective emitted photon energy is <span class="arithmatex">\(hf=E_g+E_n\)</span> — dependent on nanocrystal size <span class="arithmatex">\(L\)</span>, not on the bulk material's band gap alone.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 22

A future process roadmap projects combining GAA transistor scaling with SiC power delivery on the same chip package. Explain, using this chapter's physics, why these two technologies address different parts of the same overall system rather than competing with each other.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">GAA scaling maximizes logic transistor density and switching speed for computation, a problem solved by device *geometry* (more gate-controlled sides). SiC power delivery maximizes efficient high-voltage power conversion, a problem solved by device *material* (higher critical field). A single system can use dense GAA logic for computation and separate SiC power devices to efficiently deliver power to that logic — the two technologies solve different physical problems within the same system.</p>
</div>
</details>
