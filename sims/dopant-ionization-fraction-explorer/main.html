Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The left panel plots the ionized fraction fion(T) as a smooth S-curve rising from 0% to 100%.
  • The right (or, on narrow screens, lower) panel shows a small energy-level diagram: EC and the donor level ED (or EV and the acceptor level EA), with a handful of representative carriers that visibly hop from "bound at the dopant level" to "free in the band" as the ionized fraction rises.
  • The count of freed carriers shown in the diagram always matches the curve's current fion value.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Drag the temperature slider from low to high and watch both the curve's marker point and the diagram's carriers move together -- a low-T freeze-out state has almost all carriers still bound at the dopant level; a high-T state has almost all of them free in the band.
  • Predict: Before switching the ionization-energy slider to a larger value, predict whether the curve will shift toward higher or lower temperature.
  • Observe: Increase ED (or EA) and confirm the whole S-curve shifts toward higher temperature -- a more tightly-bound dopant needs more thermal energy before it substantially ionizes.
  • Compare dopants: Step through the donor presets (Sb, P, As) and compare how far right the curve shifts as the ionization energy grows from 39 to 54 meV.
  • Switch type: Change Type to Acceptor and confirm the same physics applies, now with holes escaping into the valence band instead of electrons into the conduction band.

Connects to Chapter 8: This MicroSim plots the simplified ionization-fraction model \(f_{ion}(T) = 1/(1+B\,e^{E_D/k_BT})\) introduced in this chapter's "Dopant Ionization and Ionization Energy" section, and connects it directly to the physical picture: each point on the curve corresponds to a specific fraction of dopant atoms that have released their carrier into the band. Chapter 7's hydrogenic model predicts the ionization energies used here as presets; the exact, non-simplified ionization statistics are developed in Chapters 9-10.

What Each Control Does

Type dropdown
Switches between Donor (n-type, electrons escape to EC) and Acceptor (p-type, holes escape to EV).
Dopant dropdown
Selects a common silicon dopant preset (or "Custom" to use the slider freely), setting the ionization energy to that species' measured value.
Ionization energy slider (ED / EA)
Sets the dopant's ionization energy directly; a larger value shifts the whole curve toward higher temperature.
Temperature slider
Moves the marker across the curve and drives the energy-level diagram's carrier count.

Challenge Questions

1. What physically happens when a donor atom becomes ionized?

Donor ionization does not break any covalent bond -- the donor's own four bonding electrons stay in place. Only the loosely-bound fifth electron gains enough thermal energy to leave, becoming a free conduction-band electron and leaving a fixed positive ion behind.

Re-read the physical explanation paragraph below the readout line, and compare it to Chapter 7's donor-atom bonding picture.

2. Comparing phosphorus (E_D = 45 meV) to arsenic (E_D = 54 meV) in silicon at the same temperature, which dopant has the higher ionized fraction?

A smaller ionization energy means less thermal energy is needed to ionize the dopant, so at any fixed temperature, phosphorus (45 meV) has a higher ionized fraction than arsenic (54 meV).

Switch between the Phosphorus and Arsenic presets at a fixed, low temperature and compare the f_ion readout.

3. At exactly the temperature where f_ion = 0.5, how many of the diagram's shown donor sites should be drawn as free electrons?

The diagram always shows round(f_ion × 6) of the 6 representative sites as free -- at f_ion = 0.5, that is 3 of 6, half of them.

Drag the temperature marker until the curve crosses the dashed 50% reference line, and count the free electrons in the diagram at that instant.

Key Takeaway: Dopant ionization is a smooth, temperature-driven statistical process, not an all-or-nothing switch -- the ionized fraction curve and the energy-level diagram are two views of the exact same physics: at any given temperature, some fraction of dopant atoms have released their carrier into the band, and that fraction is set entirely by how the dopant's ionization energy compares to the available thermal energy k_BT.
