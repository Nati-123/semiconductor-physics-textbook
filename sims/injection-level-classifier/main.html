Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The meter is continuous, not a switch: Δn/n₀ slides smoothly through low-level (green), transition (amber), and high-level (red) — there is no single field or doping value where the physics suddenly changes character.
  • The explanation box below the meter updates its wording as you cross each boundary, since the simplifying assumptions that are safe to use are different in each zone.

Guided Exploration

  • Predict: Before raising the excess-carrier slider, predict whether the needle will move left or right, and which zone it will enter first.
  • Try this: With doping (n₀) fixed, slowly raise the excess carrier slider and watch the needle sweep from Low-level through Transition into High-level injection.
  • Predict: Before raising doping instead of Δn, predict whether a higher n₀ makes high-level injection easier or harder to reach at the same Δn.
  • Observe: Raising n₀ pushes the needle back toward low-level injection for the same Δn, since the ratio Δn/n₀ shrinks.
  • Try this: Park the needle in the Transition band and read the explanation text — notice it explicitly says neither the low-level nor high-level shortcut is safe to use there.

Connects to Chapter 13: Low-level injection (\(\Delta n\ll n_0\)) is the regime assumed by most simple device equations, where majority carrier concentration is essentially unperturbed. High-level injection (\(\Delta n\) comparable to or exceeding \(n_0\)) perturbs both carrier populations significantly, requiring more careful treatment — this is a form of carrier injection pushed to an extreme, and it is why \(\tau\) is treated as injection-dependent in some device models.

What Each Control Does

Doping (sets n₀)
Sets the equilibrium majority carrier concentration n₀ used as the injection-level reference.
Excess Δn
Sets the excess carrier concentration being injected, compared directly against n₀ via the ratio Δn/n₀.

Challenge Questions

1. In low-level injection, which carrier population is significantly perturbed?

Since Δn≪n₀ in low-level injection, the majority carrier concentration barely changes, while the minority carrier population (starting from a much smaller base) is significantly perturbed.

Compare the size of Δn to n₀ when the meter reads "LOW-LEVEL INJECTION" — which population's base value is comparable to Δn?

2. Why does the sim show a "Transition" zone instead of just switching directly between low-level and high-level labels?

Both approximations rely on Δn/n₀ being either much less than or comparable to/greater than 1 — in between, neither shortcut is trustworthy, so device equations need the full (non-approximated) treatment there.

Read the explanation box while the needle sits between the green and red zones — what does it say about which assumptions are and are not safe?

3. Deep in high-level injection (Δn ≳ n₀), which approximation becomes useful?

Once the injected excess carriers dominate over the original equilibrium population, the excess electron and hole concentrations become essentially equal — this is the ambipolar approximation used throughout high-injection device analysis.

Push the Δn slider to its maximum and re-read the "HIGH-LEVEL INJECTION" explanation text.

Key Takeaway: Low-level and high-level injection are not two switch positions — they are the two ends of a continuous spectrum in Δn/n₀, each with its own simplifying assumption (majority ≈ n₀ at low level; Δn ≈ Δp at high level). The transition band in between is exactly where both shortcuts fail and the full, unapproximated carrier equations are needed.
