<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>

2. An electron in an atom's outermost occupied shell, available for chemical bonding
3. An electron that has been permanently removed from an atom

</div>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">A valence electron occupies an atom's outermost shell and is available to participate in chemical bonding. Silicon has 4 valence electrons, which determine its entire bonding behavior.</p>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75;">What is a covalent bond?</p>

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A bond formed by transferring an electron completely from one atom to another
2. A bond formed when two atoms each contribute one valence electron to a shared electron pair
3. A bond formed by delocalizing electrons across an entire crystal
4. The force holding a nucleus together
</div>

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Answer</summary>

<p style="color: #2E7D32; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 0;">Correct Answer: B</p>
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">A covalent bond forms when two atoms each contribute one valence electron to a shared pair, attracted electrostatically to both nuclei. It is the bond type used by silicon and germanium.</p>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75;">Why is a covalent bond described as directional?</p>

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because it points specifically along the line connecting the two specific atoms sharing the electron pair
2. Because it pulls equally in every direction around each atom
3. Because it has no fixed geometry at all
4. Because it only exists in metals

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">

<p style="color: #2E7D32; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 0;">Correct Answer: A</p>
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">A covalent bond's shared electron pair is localized between one specific pair of atoms, so the bond points along the line connecting those two nuclei — unlike ionic or metallic bonding, which are non-directional.</p>
<p style="color: #555; font-style: italic; margin-bottom: 0; margin-top: 8px;"><strong>Concept Tested:</strong> Covalent Bond</p>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75;">What is an ionic bond?</p>

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A bond formed by sharing an electron pair between two atoms
2. A bond formed when one atom transfers one or more valence electrons completely to another, producing oppositely charged ions
3. A bond formed only between identical atoms

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">An ionic bond forms when one atom transfers electrons completely to another, producing a cation and an anion held together by Coulomb attraction — non-directional, unlike a covalent bond.</p>
<p style="color: #555; font-style: italic; margin-bottom: 0; margin-top: 8px;"><strong>Concept Tested:</strong> Ionic Bond</p>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">An amorphous solid, such as amorphous silicon, preserves roughly the same local bonding environment a crystal would prefer, but has no long-range periodic order beyond a few atomic spacings.</p>
