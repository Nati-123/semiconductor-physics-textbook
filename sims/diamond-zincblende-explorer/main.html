Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: In Diamond mode, rotate the cell and follow one of the four gray bonds from an interior atom out to a corner or face atom — count how many bonds leave each interior atom.
  • Predict: Before switching to Zincblende mode, predict which atoms will change color: only the interior 4, only the outer 14, or both.
  • Observe: Switch to Zincblende (GaAs) and notice that every bond now connects a blue Ga atom to an orange As atom — never two atoms of the same color.
  • Explain: Explain why the diamond cubic structure has a much lower coordination number (4) than Face-Centered Cubic (12), even though both are built from FCC sublattices.

Connects to Chapter 3: This MicroSim renders the diamond cubic conventional unit cell as an FCC lattice (8 corners + 6 face centers) with a 2-atom basis repeated at \((0,0,0)\) and \((\tfrac14,\tfrac14,\tfrac14)a\) — equivalently, two interpenetrating FCC lattices offset along the body diagonal by \(\tfrac14\) of its length. Silicon crystallizes in this structure with lattice constant \(a = 0.543\) nm. Zincblende (e.g. GaAs) is geometrically identical but uses two different atomic species, one per sublattice.

Challenge Questions

1. How many atoms are in the diamond cubic conventional unit cell?

8 corners × 1/8 + 6 faces × 1/2 + 4 interior atoms × 1 = 1 + 3 + 4 = 8 atoms per cell.

Add up the shared fractions: 8 corners × 1/8, 6 faces × 1/2, and 4 fully-interior atoms × 1.

2. What is the coordination number of each atom in diamond cubic?

Each atom bonds tetrahedrally to exactly 4 nearest neighbors, visible as the 4 gray bonds leaving every interior atom in the MicroSim.

Count the bonds leaving any single interior atom in the 3D view.

3. In Zincblende mode, what distinguishes it from Diamond mode geometrically?

Diamond and zincblende share the exact same atom positions and bonding geometry — zincblende simply places a different element on each of the two interpenetrating FCC sublattices (e.g. Ga and As).

Toggle between the two modes and watch the sphere positions — do they move, or only change color?

4. In Zincblende (GaAs) mode, what species does each bond connect?

Because the two sublattices are the interior atoms and the corner/face atoms respectively, and every bond connects an interior atom to an outer-lattice atom, each bond always joins one Ga and one As atom.

Switch to Zincblende mode and look at the color of the two atoms at each end of any bond.

5. What is silicon's lattice constant in the diamond cubic structure?

Silicon has lattice constant a = 0.543 nm, one of the key numeric facts from the chapter.

This value is given directly in the chapter text and in the "Connects to Chapter 3" note above.
