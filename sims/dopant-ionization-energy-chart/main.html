  • Observe: Switch to "Germanium" and confirm every bar drops — germanium's larger dielectric constant and smaller effective mass both shrink ionization energy.

Connects to Chapter 7: All of these measured ionization energies are tens of meV — tiny compared to silicon's 1.12 eV band gap — which is exactly what the hydrogenic model predicts. Because \(k_BT\approx26\) meV at room temperature, these small ionization energies are easily overcome thermally, meaning essentially every donor and acceptor atom is ionized at room temperature — the "complete ionization" assumption used throughout Chapter 8.

Challenge Questions

1. Why are these dopant ionization energies (tens of meV) important for room-temperature behavior?

Because these ionization energies are similar in size to k_BT at room temperature (~26 meV), thermal energy is generally enough to ionize essentially every dopant atom — the basis for the "complete ionization" assumption in Chapter 8.
