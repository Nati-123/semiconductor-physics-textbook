Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • N_D and N_A are held fixed here — only temperature moves, both on the band diagram and on the trend chart below it.
  • The trend chart plots E_F−E_i across the whole temperature range at once, with a marker showing where the current T slider sits.
  • At low-to-moderate T, E_F sits far from E_i (doping dominates); as T rises, the curve bends back toward E_F−E_i=0 — the sample turning intrinsic.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Set N_D to a light doping level (around 10¹⁴ cm⁻³) and slide T from 150 K to 800 K, watching E_F approach E_i well before the slider's maximum.
  • Predict: Before raising N_D to a heavy level (around 10¹⁸ cm⁻³), predict whether the sample will reach intrinsic behavior sooner or later (at what temperature) than the lightly-doped case.
  • Observe: Repeat with heavy doping and confirm E_F stays close to E_C much longer, only bending toward E_i at a much higher temperature.
  • Compare: Switch material to GaAs (larger band gap, smaller nᵢ at a given T) and notice the intrinsic transition is pushed to an even higher temperature than silicon at the same doping.

Connects to Chapter 10: \(n_0\) depends on temperature only through \(n_i(T)\) (Chapter 9), which grows exponentially. At low-to-moderate T, \(n_i(T)\ll N_D-N_A\) and \(n_0\approx N_D-N_A\) is essentially temperature-independent, so \(E_F\) barely moves. Once \(n_i(T)\) grows to become comparable to (and then exceeds) the fixed net doping, \(n_0\to n_i(T)\) and \(E_F\to E_i\) — the same freeze-out/extrinsic/intrinsic story Chapter 8 introduced qualitatively, now traced exactly through \(E_C-E_F=k_BT\ln(N_C/n_0)\).

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects the effective masses, band gap, and Varshni constants used to compute N_C(T), N_V(T), and nᵢ(T).
N_D and N_A sliders (fixed)
Set the doping level held constant while temperature sweeps.
Temperature slider
Sets T; the band diagram and the trend-chart marker both update together.

Challenge Questions

1. Predict first: as temperature keeps rising for a doped sample, what does E_F eventually approach?

As T rises, nᵢ(T) eventually exceeds the fixed net doping, so n0→ni and the sample behaves as if intrinsic — which by definition means E_F→E_i.

Drag temperature to its maximum for a lightly-doped sample and watch where E_F ends up relative to E_i.

2. Why does a heavily-doped sample resist the intrinsic transition longer (need higher T) than a lightly-doped one?

The intrinsic transition happens once nᵢ(T) grows to be comparable to the fixed net doping N_D−N_A. A larger net doping simply requires nᵢ(T) to climb further (higher T) before that crossover occurs.

Compare the trend-chart curves for a light- and heavy-doping N_D setting.

Key Takeaway: Doping sets where E_F starts (at low-to-moderate T); temperature decides how long it stays there. Every doped semiconductor eventually becomes intrinsic-dominated at a high enough temperature — heavier doping only postpones that transition to a higher temperature, it doesn't prevent it.
