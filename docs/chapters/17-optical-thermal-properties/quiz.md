1. Igen is independent of depletion width
2. Igen = qGthWA is directly proportional to depletion width W
3. Widening the depletion region decreases ni
4. Widening the depletion region increases the diffusion coefficient
</div>

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Answer</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #2E7D32; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 0;">Correct Answer: B</p>
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">Since Igen = qGthWA is directly proportional to W, and reverse bias widens W (Chapter 15), generation current rises with reverse bias — a real leakage mechanism beyond the ideal diode equation.</p>

<p style="color: #1565C0; font-weight: 700; font-size: 1.08rem; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 14px;">Question 20</p>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75;">Why can silicon photodiodes work well as light sensors despite silicon being indirect-gap, even though silicon LEDs are impractical?</p>

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Absorption can be compensated by using a thicker device, but emission has no equivalent fix since it always competes against faster non-radiative recombination
2. Silicon is actually direct-gap for absorption but indirect-gap for emission
3. There is no real difference — both are equally impractical
4. Photodiodes do not rely on photon absorption at all
</div>

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Answer</summary>

<p style="color: #2E7D32; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 0;">Correct Answer: A</p>
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">A photodiode can simply be made thick enough to absorb nearly all incident light despite a smaller α; radiative recombination has no such compensation, since every recombination event competes directly against faster non-radiative pathways.</p>
