Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Predict → Explore → Observe → Explain: Before clicking the toggle, predict which isolated Fermi level (\(E_{Fp}\) or \(E_{Fn}\)) sits closer to its band edge for the doping shown. Then explore by toggling between "Before Contact" and "After Contact." Observe that the vertical gap between \(E_{Fp}\) and \(E_{Fn}\) before contact is visually identical to the \(qV_{bi}\) bracket after contact. Finally, explain why forcing those two separate Fermi levels into one single flat level is exactly what makes the bands bend.

What to Observe

  • The toggle button switches between two views of the same junction: Before Contact shows the p-side and n-side as separate, isolated materials, each with its own flat Fermi level; After Contact shows the standard bent-band equilibrium diagram with a single flat \(E_F\).
  • The gap between \(E_{Fp}\) and \(E_{Fn}\) in the "Before Contact" view is labeled \(qV_{bi}\) — it is the exact same energy as the \(qV_{bi}\) bracket drawn between the flat \(E_C\) regions in the "After Contact" view. Forcing two different Fermi levels into one is precisely what makes the bands bend.
  • Bands bend downward going from the p-side (higher electron energy) to the n-side (lower electron energy) — consistent with the built-in field pointing from n to p, shown in the Metallurgical Junction Explorer earlier in this chapter.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Raise N_A and N_D together and watch how little \(V_{bi}\) changes compared to how much N_A·N_D changes — a direct look at the logarithm's compressing effect.
  • Predict: Before switching material from Si to GaAs, predict whether \(V_{bi}\) will rise or fall, given that GaAs has a much smaller \(n_i\).
  • Observe: Raising temperature T increases \(n_i\) exponentially, which pulls \(V_{bi}\) back down even though \(kT/q\) itself is increasing — the two effects compete, and \(n_i\) wins.

Connects to Chapter 14: The built-in potential \(V_{bi}=(kT/q)\ln(N_AN_D/n_i^2)\) is the equilibrium electrostatic potential difference across a p-n junction, required so that a single Fermi level can describe both the p-side and n-side. It depends only on doping and material properties — never on device area or geometry.

What Each Control Does

Before/After Contact toggle
Switches between the two isolated pre-contact band diagrams and the single equilibrium post-contact diagram.
Material
Selects the semiconductor and its 300 K intrinsic carrier concentration and band gap, both used to compute \(n_i(T)\).
N_A, N_D
Set the acceptor and donor doping concentrations (log scale, \(10^{14}\)-\(10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\)).
T
Sets the temperature used in both \(kT/q\) and the \(n_i(T)\) calculation.

Challenge Questions

1. If N_A is increased by a factor of 1000, what happens to V_bi?

Correct. Because V_bi depends on the logarithm of N_A·N_D, multiplying N_A by 1000 only adds (kT/q)·ln(1000) ≈ 0.179 V at 300 K, regardless of the starting doping level.

Look carefully at the equation — N_A appears inside a natural log, not linearly.

2. Two junctions have identical doping but different cross-sectional areas. How do their V_bi values compare?

Correct. V_bi is set entirely by N_A, N_D, n_i, and T — all intensive material properties. Junction capacitance, not V_bi, is the quantity that depends on area.

Check the V_bi formula — does the junction's physical area appear anywhere in it?

3. In the "Before Contact" view, why are E_Fp and E_Fn drawn at different heights even though E_C and E_V are drawn at the same height in both boxes?

Both sides are the same material (same E_C-E_V gap), but N_A sets how close E_Fp sits to E_V, and N_D sets how close E_Fn sits to E_C. That difference in Fermi-level position is exactly what has to be reconciled once the two sides are joined.

Change N_A or N_D and watch only that side's Fermi level move — the gap between E_C and E_V stays fixed since the material doesn't change.

Key Takeaway: The built-in potential isn't an extra ingredient added to the junction — it's the direct, unavoidable consequence of forcing two materials with different Fermi levels into a single crystal with one Fermi level. The size of that reconciliation, \(qV_{bi}\), is set entirely by how far apart the two isolated Fermi levels started, which depends only on doping, material, and temperature — never on device area.
