Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The band diagram shows EC, the intrinsic level Ei (dashed, near midgap), and EV, with a colored EF line you drag directly via the slider.
  • The two gauges on the right show n₀ and p₀ on the same log scale (10⁰ to 10²¹ cm⁻³) — as one grows toward the top of its gauge, the other shrinks toward the bottom.
  • The orange "near-degenerate" shading within 3kBT of each band edge marks where the non-degenerate Boltzmann formulas above the diagram start losing accuracy.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Set EF so the line sits exactly on the dashed Ei line and confirm n₀ and p₀ read out as equal (both ≈ nᵢ).
  • Predict: Before dragging EF up toward EC, predict which gauge (n₀ or p₀) will rise and which will fall.
  • Observe: Drag EF continuously from near EV to near EC and watch n₀ rise by many orders of magnitude while p₀ falls by the same factor — the n₀·p₀ readout stays fixed at nᵢ² throughout (as long as the badge reads NON-DEGENERATE).
  • Explore: Drag EF past EC (or below EV) and watch the badge turn red — the formulas are still evaluated, but now explicitly flagged as invalid estimates.
  • Compare: Switch material to GaAs and repeat — notice how much smaller nᵢ (and therefore n₀=p₀ at EF=Ei) is compared to silicon.

Connects to Chapter 9: This MicroSim evaluates \(n_0=N_Ce^{-(E_C-E_F)/k_BT}\) and \(p_0=N_Ve^{-(E_F-E_V)/k_BT}\) directly as EF moves — the same non-degenerate effective-density-of-states equations this chapter's "Effective Density of States" section derives. It intentionally moves EF as the input, the reverse of Chapter 10's Fermi Level Position explorer, which instead computes EF as an output of a given doping level. Together the two views show the same equation read in both directions.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects Si, Ge, or GaAs, setting N_C, N_V, and the band gap used in both formulas.
Temperature slider
Sets T, which rescales N_C(T), N_V(T), and k_BT together.
E_F position slider
Directly sets E_F − E_V (in eV); the diagram and both carrier gauges update immediately.

Challenge Questions

1. As E_F is dragged upward toward E_C, what happens to n₀ and p₀?

Moving E_F toward E_C shrinks E_C−E_F, increasing n₀ = N_C·e^(−(E_C−E_F)/kT); it simultaneously grows E_F−E_V, decreasing p₀ = N_V·e^(−(E_F−E_V)/kT).

Drag the E_F slider up and watch both gauges at once.

2. When E_F sits exactly on the dashed E_i line, what is true of n₀ and p₀?

E_i is defined (Chapter 10) as exactly the E_F value where n₀=p₀=nᵢ — set the slider there and confirm both gauges match.

Drag E_F until the colored line lines up with the dashed E_i line and read both gauges.

3. Why does the badge turn red once E_F is dragged past E_C?

The formulas assume E−E_F ≫ k_BT (Chapter 8's non-degenerate condition). Once the slider pushes E_F to or past a band edge, that assumption is violated and the displayed n₀/p₀ are only rough estimates, not exact — the same caution Chapter 8's degenerate-semiconductor explorer raises from the doping side.

Re-read the info box that appears once the badge leaves NON-DEGENERATE.

Key Takeaway: E_C−E_F and E_F−E_V are not independent numbers to memorize separately — they are two views of one E_F position, and n₀ and p₀ move in opposite directions as E_F slides through the gap because they depend on these two complementary distances. This is the direct, visual meaning behind the effective-density-of-states equations this chapter derives algebraically.
