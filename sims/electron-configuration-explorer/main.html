Try This

  • Try this: Press the "Si (Z=14)" button and read the configuration readout — confirm it matches \(1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^2\) from the chapter text.
  • Predict: Before pressing "Na (Z=11)", predict how many valence electrons sodium will have, based on its single outer-shell electron discussed in the ionic bonding section.
  • Compare: Press "Na", "Si", and "Cl" in turn and compare the number of orange (valence) electrons on the outermost ring for each — 1, 4, and 7 respectively.
  • Observe: Slide Z slowly from 10 to 18 and watch the outermost ring refill from empty back up to 8 electrons, while the inner rings (green, core) stay fixed.
  • Explain: Explain why silicon (Z=14) and carbon (Z=6) both have exactly 4 valence electrons despite being in different rows of the periodic table.

Connects to Chapter 4: This MicroSim visualizes the electron-shell-filling logic behind the chapter's central claim: silicon's electron configuration \(1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\,3p^2\) leaves exactly 4 electrons in its outermost (valence) shell, and that single number, 4, is what determines silicon's preference for covalent, tetrahedral bonding over the metallic or ionic bonding preferred by elements with different valence electron counts (compare Na's 1 valence electron or Cl's 7).

Challenge Questions

1. How many valence electrons does silicon (Z=14) have?

Silicon's outermost shell (n=3) holds 4 electrons (from \(3s^2\,3p^2\)), shown as the 4 orange dots on the outer ring.

Press the "Si (Z=14)" button and read the "Valence electrons" readout below the diagram.

2. What color represents core (non-valence) electrons in this diagram?

Electrons in any shell below the outermost occupied shell are drawn green (core electrons); only the outermost ring is orange (valence electrons).

Check the legend box in the top-left of the canvas.

3. According to this MicroSim, how many valence electrons does sodium (Na, Z=11) have?

Sodium's configuration is \(1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^1\) — only 1 electron in its outermost (n=3) shell, matching the single valence electron discussed in the ionic bonding section.

Press the "Na (Z=11)" button and count the orange dots on the outer ring.
