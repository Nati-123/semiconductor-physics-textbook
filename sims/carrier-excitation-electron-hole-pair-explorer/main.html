Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Click Start with the default T = 300 K and Eg = 1.12 eV (silicon). Watch an occasional blue electron dot rise from the green valence band, through the gap, into the purple conduction band, leaving a red hole ring behind at its old site.
  • Predict: Before dragging the Eg slider up toward 3.0 eV, predict what will happen to how often new electron-hole pairs appear.
  • Observe: Drag Eg up and confirm the generation-activity meter shrinks and pairs appear far less often — a wider gap means far fewer thermally excited carriers.
  • Explore: Bring Eg back down and drag the Temperature slider from 100 K to 900 K. Confirm the opposite trend: higher T means more frequent generation events and a higher running-average pair count.
  • Watch for teal dashed lines: these mark recombination events, where a free electron falls back into a hole. Confirm the free-electron count (n) and hole count (p) in the readout are always exactly equal.

Connects to Chapter 6: This MicroSim visualizes carrier generation directly on the energy-space band diagram this chapter builds: an electron gains enough energy to cross the band gap \(E_g\) from the valence band (\(E_v\)) to the conduction band (\(E_c\)), leaving a hole behind. Because every generation event creates exactly one electron and one hole together, and every recombination event removes exactly one of each, the free-electron count and hole count in this MicroSim are always equal — the defining property of an intrinsic semiconductor, \(n=p=n_i\). The rising-with-T, falling-with-Eg trend you can see in the generation-activity meter is the same qualitative behavior behind Chapter 9's precise result \(n_i\propto T^{3/2}e^{-E_g/2k_BT}\); the rates shown here are visually exaggerated for a watchable animation, not physically exact. Chapter 7's Intrinsic Semiconductor Explorer shows the same physical process from a real-space, covalent-bond-breaking point of view — this MicroSim shows the energy-space counterpart, using the Ec/Ev/Eg vocabulary this chapter introduces.

What Each Control Does

Temperature T (K) slider
Sets the temperature used in the illustrative generation-rate formula; higher T means more frequent electron-hole pair generation.
Band gap Eg (eV) slider
Sets the energy gap electrons must cross; a larger Eg sharply reduces generation frequency, echoing the exponential Eg dependence of real intrinsic carrier concentration.
Start / Stop button
Starts or pauses the generation/recombination animation. The simulation begins stopped so you can inspect the initial all-bound state first.
Reset button
Stops the animation and returns every site to the bound (fully occupied valence band) state.

Challenge Questions

1. Every time an electron is thermally excited from the valence band to the conduction band, what else happens?

Each generation event removes one bound valence electron and creates one free conduction-band electron, leaving exactly one hole (an empty state) at the original valence-band site — electrons and holes are always created in pairs.

Watch a single generation event closely: count how many red hole rings appear compared to how many new blue electrons appear in the conduction band.

2. Why are the free-electron count n and hole count p always exactly equal in this MicroSim?

In an intrinsic (undoped) semiconductor, the only source of carriers is thermal bond-breaking / band-to-band excitation, which always produces one electron and one hole together, and recombination always removes one of each — so n = p = ni exactly, by the physical mechanism itself, not by an arbitrary rule.

Trace one generation event and one recombination event and count electrons and holes gained or lost in each.

3. Increasing the band gap Eg while holding T fixed does what to the generation rate?

A larger Eg means an electron needs much more energy to cross the gap, which is exponentially less likely at a given temperature — visible here as a sharply shrinking generation-activity meter and far less frequent events.

Drag Eg from 0.3 eV up to 3.0 eV and watch both the generation-activity meter and how often new pairs appear.

4. What does the teal dashed line represent when it appears?

The teal dashed line traces a recombination event: an existing free electron in the conduction band falls back down into an available hole in the valence band, removing both carriers and restoring that site to "bound."

Compare the orange dashed lines (generation, rising) to the teal dashed lines (recombination, falling) and note which direction each one travels.
