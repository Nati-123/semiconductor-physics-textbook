Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Drag the doping concentration slider to its minimum (10¹³ cm⁻³) and read the "1 dopant per N silicon atoms" ratio.
  • Predict: Before dragging to the maximum (10¹⁹ cm⁻³), predict roughly how many orders of magnitude the ratio will shrink by.
  • Observe: Notice that even at the "heavily doped" end of the slider, dopant atoms are still vastly outnumbered by silicon atoms.

Connects to Chapter 7: Silicon's atomic density is about \(5\times10^{22}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\), while typical doping concentrations range from about \(10^{13}\) to \(10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\) — meaning even "heavily doped" silicon still has dopant atoms outnumbered by host silicon atoms by a factor of thousands. This scale visualizer makes that enormous numeric gap concrete, on the same logarithmic axis used throughout carrier-concentration work in later chapters.

What Each Control Does

Doping concentration slider (log scale)
Sets the dopant concentration \(N_D\) from \(10^{13}\) to \(10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\).
