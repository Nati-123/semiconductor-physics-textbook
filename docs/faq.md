## Getting Started Questions

### What is this course about?

Semiconductor Physics introduces the quantum-mechanical and statistical foundations that explain how semiconductor materials behave and how that behavior is engineered into the diodes, transistors, and integrated circuits that underlie modern electronics. Students move from the atomic structure of crystalline solids, through energy band theory and carrier statistics, to the transport and junction physics that govern real devices.

For full details, see the [Course Description](course-description.md).

Start with [Chapter 1 - Physics and Math Foundations](chapters/01-physics-math-foundations/index.md) to begin building foundational skills.

### Who is this course designed for?

This course is designed for junior-level Electrical Engineering and Applied/Engineering Physics undergraduates. The material assumes calculus-based general physics, ordinary differential equations, and introductory modern/quantum physics.

### What prerequisites do I need before starting?

Before beginning this course, you should have:

- Calculus-based general physics (mechanics and electricity & magnetism)
- Ordinary differential equations
- Introductory modern/quantum physics (wave-particle duality, the Schrödinger equation at an introductory level)
- Basic linear circuit analysis (Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws) is helpful but not required

[Chapter 1](chapters/01-physics-math-foundations/index.md) reviews the classical physics and mathematics prerequisites before new material begins.

### How is this textbook organized?

The textbook is organized into eighteen chapters that build progressively from mathematical/physical foundations, through quantum mechanics and crystal structure, to band theory, carrier statistics and transport, and finally the p-n junction and semiconductor devices. Each chapter includes content, worked examples, diagrams, a quiz, and practice problems.

## Using the MicroSims

### What are MicroSims?

MicroSims are small, interactive p5.js simulations embedded directly in the chapter content. They let you manipulate variables (such as charge, distance, or vector components) and see the physical result immediately, reinforcing the equations in the text.

### Where can I find all the MicroSims?

See the [MicroSims overview page](sims/index.md) for a complete, chapter-grouped list.

## Technical Questions

### What math background do I need?

You should be comfortable with single- and multi-variable calculus, vectors, and ordinary differential equations. [Chapter 1](chapters/01-physics-math-foundations/index.md) reviews the specific mathematical tools (vector calculus, complex numbers, partial derivatives) used throughout the book.

### Does the site work on mobile devices?

Yes. The site is built with MkDocs Material and is fully responsive, including the interactive MicroSims.
