What to Observe

  • The purple curve is the exact Fermi-Dirac function; the gold curve is its Boltzmann approximation — they overlap almost perfectly for E well above E_F, and diverge as E approaches and passes E_F.
  • The green shaded region is the energy range where the two curves agree to within your selected tolerance — recomputed live as you change temperature or tolerance.
  • f(E) is the probability that an available state at energy E is occupied by an electron — it is not itself a count of electrons, just a probability between 0 and 1.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Click each temperature preset (77 K, 300 K, 500 K) in turn and watch the shaded zone's boundary move.
  • Predict: Before dragging the temperature slider up from 300 K, predict whether the shaded "good approximation" zone will widen or narrow in energy (eV).
  • Observe: Drag the marker slider (E − E_F) to the shaded zone's edge and confirm the percent-difference readout crosses your selected tolerance almost exactly there.
  • Explore: Switch the tolerance dropdown from 5% to 1% and watch the shaded zone shrink — a tighter tolerance requires being farther from E_F before the approximation is "good enough."

Connects to Chapter 9: The exact Fermi function, \(f(E)=1/[1+e^{(E-E_F)/k_BT}]\), is difficult to integrate analytically against the density of states. Whenever \(E-E_F\gg k_BT\) (conduction-band states in a non-degenerate semiconductor), the exact function is very well approximated by the much simpler Boltzmann approximation, \(f(E)\approx e^{-(E-E_F)/k_BT}\) — this is exactly the substitution used to derive the effective-density-of-states formula for \(n_0\) later in this chapter, and it is only valid outside a heavily-doped, degenerate regime like the one Chapter 8 warned about. The relative error of the Boltzmann approximation turns out to equal \(f_{Boltzmann}(E)\) itself, so the boundary for a tolerance \(\varepsilon\) is exactly \(E-E_F=k_BT\ln(1/\varepsilon)\) — the formula this MicroSim evaluates live.

What Each Control Does

Temperature slider / 77 K, 300 K, 500 K presets
Sets k_BT, which rescales both curves and the width of the good-approximation zone in energy.
Marker slider (E − E_F)
Reads off both f(E) values and their percent difference at a chosen energy.
Tolerance dropdown
Sets the acceptable relative-error threshold (1%, 5%, or 10%) defining the shaded "good approximation" zone and its numeric boundary.

Challenge Questions

1. Where does the Boltzmann approximation most closely match the exact Fermi function?

When E−E_F is a few times k_BT or more, the "+1" in the Fermi function's denominator becomes negligible compared to the exponential, and the Boltzmann approximation becomes excellent.

Compare the two curves at large positive E−E_F versus near E−E_F=0.

2. What happens to the shaded good-approximation zone's width (in eV) as temperature increases?

The approximation's validity boundary sits at a fixed multiple of k_BT, so as temperature (and therefore k_BT) increases, that boundary moves to a larger energy in eV, widening the shaded zone.

Drag the temperature slider from low to high and watch the shaded zone's right edge.

3. At T = 300 K (k_BT ≈ 0.0259 eV), roughly how far above E_F must a state be for the Boltzmann approximation to be accurate to within 1%?

The boundary is E−E_F = k_BT·ln(1/ε); for ε=0.01, ln(100)≈4.6, so the boundary sits at about 4.6 k_BT ≈ 0.119 eV at 300 K. Set the tolerance dropdown to 1% and read the boundary value directly off the readout to confirm.

Set the tolerance dropdown to 1% at T=300 K and read the "Boundary for accuracy" line below the chart.

Key Takeaway: The Boltzmann approximation is not "always good" or "always bad" — its accuracy depends continuously on how far a state sits above E_F relative to k_BT, and the exact boundary for any chosen tolerance is E−E_F=k_BT·ln(1/ε). This is precisely the condition a non-degenerate semiconductor's conduction-band states satisfy, and precisely why the approximation fails once doping pushes E_F close to (or past) a band edge.
