Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Predict: Before clicking anything, predict what happens to the potential barrier and the depletion width \(W\) when you jump from the Equilibrium preset to the Forward Bias preset.
  • Explore: Click the three preset buttons — Reverse Bias (\(V=-3\) V), Equilibrium (\(V=0\) V), and Forward Bias (\(V=+0.5\) V) — then fine-tune with the continuous bias slider between them.
  • Observe: Watch the depletion-width bar below the band diagram: it visibly narrows as the bands flatten under forward bias and stretches out under reverse bias, with a dashed "eq." tick marking the equilibrium width for comparison.
  • Explain: The barrier height readout always equals \(V_{bi}-V\) exactly — a positive (forward) V subtracts from \(V_{bi}\), a negative (reverse) V adds to it — and because \(W\) scales with the square root of the barrier, the width bar shrinks and grows more gently than the barrier itself.
  • Predict: What happens to the depletion width when forward bias increases?

Connects to Chapter 15: Applying forward bias lowers the junction's potential barrier from \(V_{bi}\) to \(V_{bi}-V\), narrowing the depletion region and sharply increasing diffusion current. Applying reverse bias raises the barrier to \(V_{bi}+V\), widening the depletion region and suppressing diffusion to a tiny drift-limited current.

What Each Control Does

NA, ND
Set doping concentrations, which set the equilibrium built-in potential \(V_{bi}\).
Bias V
Positive values are forward bias (barrier shrinks); negative values are reverse bias (barrier grows). Drag freely or click a preset to snap to a representative value.
Reverse Bias / Equilibrium / Forward Bias buttons
Snap the bias slider to \(-3\) V, \(0\) V, or \(+0.5\) V. The button closest to the slider's current value is always highlighted, even while dragging.

Challenge Questions

1. Under forward bias, what happens to the junction's potential barrier?

Correct. Forward bias subtracts from Vbi, lowering the barrier to Vbi − V and letting far more carriers diffuse across.

Recall the sign convention: forward bias applies the higher potential to the p-side, opposing the built-in field.

2. What happens to the depletion width under reverse bias?

Correct. Reverse bias raises the effective voltage in the W formula to Vbi + V, and since W grows with the square root of that voltage, W widens.

Look at the W(V) formula — does raising the voltage term inside the square root increase or decrease W?

3. What happens to the depletion width when forward bias increases?

Correct. Increasing forward bias lowers the barrier (Vbi − V), so W = √[2ε(Vbi−V)/q · (1/NA+1/ND)] shrinks — visible directly in the width bar narrowing toward the "eq." reference tick and below it.

Try clicking the Forward Bias preset and watch the width bar under the band diagram — does it grow or shrink compared to the equilibrium tick?
