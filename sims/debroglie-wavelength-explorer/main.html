What Each Control Does

Object dropdown
Selects a preset mass (electron, proton, neutron, baseball, bowling ball) or "Custom" to unlock the mass slider below.
Custom mass slider
Active only when Object = Custom. Sets mass on a log10 scale, spanning from subatomic (~\(10^{-31}\) kg) to macroscopic (~1 kg).
Velocity slider
Sets speed from 0 to \(10^7\) m/s (non-relativistic range) — appears in the denominator of \(\lambda=h/(mv)\).
Preset buttons
Jump directly to three worked-example configurations: a fast electron, a thermal neutron, and a thrown baseball.

Challenge Questions

1. If you double the velocity of a given particle, what happens to its de Broglie wavelength?

\(\lambda = h/(mv)\) is inversely proportional to \(v\), so doubling \(v\) halves \(\lambda\).

Look at where \(v\) appears in the formula — is it in the numerator or the denominator?

2. Why is the baseball's wavelength marker never visible on the ruler, even at maximum zoom?

The formula still applies — mass simply dominates the denominator so strongly that \(\lambda\) becomes far smaller than any atom, let alone a visible ruler tick.

Try the Baseball preset and read the exact numeric wavelength value, not just the ruler marker position.

3. An electron and a proton travel at the same speed. Which has the longer de Broglie wavelength?

Since \(\lambda \propto 1/m\) at fixed \(v\), and the electron is about 1836 times lighter than the proton, the electron has the much longer wavelength.

Set the same velocity for both the Electron and Proton presets and compare the numeric readouts.

4. The thermal neutron preset (~2200 m/s) gives a wavelength close in size to which reference marker?

Thermal neutrons have wavelengths on the order of angstroms, comparable to atomic spacing — this is exactly why neutron diffraction can probe crystal structure.

Click the "Thermal neutron" preset button and read the marker's position on the ruler.

5. Matter waves are only practically detectable when the de Broglie wavelength is comparable to what?

Wave effects like diffraction only become observable when \(\lambda\) is on the same order as the spacing of the diffracting structure — atomic spacing for electrons/neutrons, essentially never for baseballs.

Think about why electron diffraction experiments use crystal lattices as their "diffraction grating."
