Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Set the Ga fraction slider to exactly 0.50 and confirm a perfect alternating Ga/As lattice with zero defects.
  • Predict: Before dragging the slider away from 0.50, predict what kind of defect will appear.
  • Observe: Drag the slider toward Ga-rich or As-rich and watch red-outlined antisite defects (a Ga atom sitting on an As site, or vice versa) appear.

Connects to Chapter 7: Unlike an elemental semiconductor such as silicon, a compound semiconductor like GaAs must be grown with its two species in almost exactly a 1:1 ratio. Even tiny stoichiometric deviations create antisite defects (a Ga atom sitting where an As atom belongs, or vice versa) that disrupt the ideal bonding pattern and degrade electronic performance — a crystal-growth engineering challenge elemental semiconductors simply do not have. (This MicroSim exaggerates the defect fraction well beyond real ppm-level deviations so the effect is visible.)

What Each Control Does

Ga fraction slider
Sets the fraction of Group III (Ga) atoms in the lattice; 0.50 is perfectly stoichiometric GaAs.

Challenge Questions

1. What does a red-outlined atom represent in this MicroSim?

An antisite defect occurs when a Ga atom occupies a site that should hold As (or vice versa) — exactly the kind of defect off-stoichiometry growth introduces.

Move the Ga fraction slider away from 0.50 and see which atoms get outlined in red.
