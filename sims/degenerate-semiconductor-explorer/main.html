Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Start with a low donor concentration and watch the Fermi level sit well below the conduction band edge, deep in the gap.
  • Predict: Before increasing N_D toward 10^19-10^20 cm^-3, predict what will happen to the Fermi level's position.
  • Observe: Drag N_D past silicon's effective density of states (N_C ≈ 2.8×10^19 cm^-3) and watch the Fermi level cross into the conduction band, triggering the degenerate warning.

Connects to Chapter 8: The formula \(E_C-E_F = k_BT\ln(N_C/N_D)\) (a preview of Chapter 10's carrier-concentration equations) assumes the Fermi level stays safely inside the band gap — the non-degenerate approximation used throughout Chapters 9-10. Once doping approaches or exceeds \(N_C\), the effective density of states in the conduction band, this formula predicts the Fermi level moving up to or above the conduction band edge itself — a degenerate semiconductor, where the simple Boltzmann statistics break down and the material starts to behave somewhat like a metal.

What Each Control Does

Donor concentration slider (N_D)
Sets the doping concentration, which determines the Fermi level's computed position via \(E_C-E_F=k_BT\ln(N_C/N_D)\).

Challenge Questions

1. What happens to the computed Fermi level as N_D approaches or exceeds N_C?

As N_D approaches N_C, ln(N_C/N_D) shrinks toward zero (and becomes negative beyond N_C), pushing E_F up to and then above E_C — the signature of a degenerate semiconductor.

Drag N_D to its maximum and watch the E_F marker's position relative to the dashed E_C line.
