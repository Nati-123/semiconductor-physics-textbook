Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Compare the long-base (purple) and short-base (orange) curves side by side, then drag W' down toward 0 and watch the short-base curve get steeper right at the injection edge.
  • Predict: Before raising the applied voltage V, predict whether the shape of either curve will change, or only the peak value Δp(0).
  • Observe: When W' is set much larger than Lp, the short-base line and long-base curve nearly overlap near the injection edge — the short-base approximation breaks down once W' is not actually short.

Connects to Chapter 15: Minority carrier injection at the depletion edge is set by the law of the junction. The injected carriers then diffuse according to the continuity equation, giving an exponential decay in the long-base diode limit (quasi-neutral region much longer than the diffusion length) or a linear profile in the short-base diode limit (quasi-neutral region much shorter than the diffusion length, forced to zero at the nearby ohmic contact).

What Each Control Does

V
Sets the applied forward bias, which sets the peak injected concentration Δp(0) via the law of the junction.
Lp
Sets the minority carrier diffusion length used in the long-base exponential curve.
W'
Sets the quasi-neutral region width used in the short-base linear curve.

Challenge Questions

1. What shape does the short-base diode's excess carrier profile take?

Correct. The nearby ohmic contact forces the excess concentration to zero, and with negligible recombination in the short region, the steady-state solution is linear.

Think about what boundary condition the nearby ohmic contact imposes, and what continuity-equation solution satisfies it with negligible recombination.

2. Which regime, short-base or long-base, gives a steeper concentration gradient (and therefore a larger saturation current) for the same peak concentration?

Correct. The same concentration drop happens over the shorter distance W' rather than Lp, producing a steeper gradient and a larger saturation current, exactly as seen when W' is dragged well below Lp.

Compare the two curves when W' is set much smaller than Lp — which one falls off faster near the injection edge?
