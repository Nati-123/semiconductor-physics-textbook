Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The lattice on the left shows one phosphorus donor (P) and one boron acceptor (B) embedded in the silicon (Si) host lattice, with a legend explaining each symbol.
  • The bar chart shows ND and NA side by side; the gauge below it shows the signed net doping, with the marker sliding smoothly from the p-type side, through "ND = NA" at the center, to the n-type side as you adjust the sliders.
  • The badge names the material's net type -- n-type, p-type, or Compensated when ND and NA are close enough that they nearly cancel.
  • A small approximate Fermi-level indicator at the bottom shows EF moving between EV and EC as the net doping changes sign and magnitude.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Select the "Compensation Point (ND = NA)" preset and confirm the gauge marker sits exactly at center, the badge reads Compensated, and EF sits at Ei, despite both dopant concentrations being large.
  • Predict: Before dragging ND upward from that point, predict which way the gauge marker and EF will move.
  • Observe: Drag ND up and confirm the marker slides toward n-type and EF rises toward EC.
  • Explore: Select "Strongly Compensated" and compare it to "Compensation Point" -- both are compensated, but the strongly-compensated case has vastly more total dopant atoms (and therefore more scattering, though that is a later chapter's topic) for the same small net doping.
  • Cross the boundary: Starting from the n-type preset, slowly drag NA upward and watch the badge transition live: Net n-type → Compensated → Net p-type as NA passes ND.

Connects to Chapter 8: This MicroSim visualizes \(N_{\text{net}} = N_D - N_A\): the majority carrier type and concentration in a compensated semiconductor depend only on this difference, never on either concentration alone. The Fermi-level indicator uses the standard non-degenerate approximation \(E_F - E_i \approx k_BT\ln(N_{\text{net}}/n_i)\) (n-type) or \(E_i - E_F \approx k_BT\ln(N_{\text{net}}/n_i)\) (p-type) at T = 300 K, fixed for this preview; it warns when doping is heavy enough to approach the degenerate regime covered in the Degenerate Semiconductor Explorer.

Challenge Questions

1. A sample has N_D = 8×10¹⁵ cm⁻³ and N_A = 5×10¹⁵ cm⁻³. What is its net doping and type?

Nnet = ND − NA = 8×10¹⁵ − 5×10¹⁵ = 3×10¹⁵ cm⁻³. Since this is positive, the material is net n-type.

Set the sliders to these two values and read the Nnet card directly.

2. Why does a semiconductor with ND = NA behave as if intrinsic, even though it contains huge numbers of both donor and acceptor atoms?

Each donor's free electron and each acceptor's hole can recombine with one another, so when ND = NA, doping contributes zero net carriers -- the sample's carrier concentration is then governed by thermal (intrinsic) generation alone, exactly like undoped silicon.

Select the Compensation Point preset and read the connects-to explanation above.

3. As net doping increases from a small value to a very large n-type value, what happens to the approximate Fermi level EF?

Heavier net n-type doping raises EF - Ei = kBT ln(Nnet/ni), so EF rises from near Ei toward EC as net doping increases -- eventually approaching the degenerate regime if doping is heavy enough.

Drag ND far above NA and watch the red EF dot in the small band indicator move.

Key Takeaway: In a compensated semiconductor, majority carrier type and concentration are set entirely by the difference ND − NA, not by either concentration alone -- two heavily-doped but nearly equal dopant populations can behave almost exactly like a lightly-doped (or even intrinsic) sample.
