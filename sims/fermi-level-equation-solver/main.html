Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The Mode dropdown changes both which slider(s) are shown and which equation the solver panel walks through — the band diagram always reflects the result.
  • Each step shows the equation first, then the same equation with your current numbers substituted in, ending with the numeric result.
  • This is a practice tool, not just a display: try predicting the result before reading the final step.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: In "Given N_D, N_A → find E_F" mode, set N_D=10¹⁶, N_A=0 and follow the three steps to see nᵢ, then n₀, then E_C−E_F computed in sequence.
  • Predict: Before switching to "Given n₀ → find E_F" mode, predict whether entering the SAME n₀ you just computed will reproduce the same E_F.
  • Observe: Switch to "Given p₀ → find E_F" and confirm the mirror-image equation, E_F−E_V=k_BT·ln(N_V/p₀), is used instead.
  • Explore: Try "Given E_F → find n₀, p₀" and drag E_F toward E_C — for a deeper, continuously-animated version of this exact direction (with a full n₀/p₀ trend chart), see Chapter 9's Carrier Concentration and Fermi Level Explorer, linked below.

Connects to Chapter 10: All four modes are different views of the same two equations: \(n_0=N_Ce^{-(E_C-E_F)/k_BT}\) and \(p_0=N_Ve^{-(E_F-E_V)/k_BT}\), plus the exact carrier-concentration equation combining the mass action law and charge neutrality (Chapter 9). Given doping, solve for n₀ first, then invert the first equation for E_F. Given n₀ or p₀ directly, invert the corresponding equation immediately. Given E_F, evaluate both equations forward — the direction Chapter 9's Carrier Concentration and Fermi Level Explorer explores interactively with a continuous drag and trend chart.

What Each Control Does

Mode dropdown
Chooses which quantity is known and which equation the solver walks through.
Material dropdown
Selects the effective masses and band gap used in every mode's equations.
Temperature slider
Sets T (and k_BT), used throughout every mode's substitution.
Mode-specific slider(s)
The known quantity for the selected mode — N_D/N_A, n₀, p₀, or E_F position.

Challenge Questions

1. In "Given n₀ → find E_F" mode, which equation does the solver invert?

Given n₀ directly, the solver rearranges n₀=N_C·e^(−(E_C−E_F)/k_BT) into E_C−E_F=k_BT·ln(N_C/n₀) — no doping or charge neutrality needed at all.

Switch to that mode and read step 1 in the solver panel.

2. Why does "Given N_D, N_A → find E_F" mode need an extra step that the "Given n₀" mode does not?

Given N_D and N_A, n₀ is not yet known — it must be solved for first via the mass-action/charge-neutrality quadratic, and only then can E_C−E_F=k_BT·ln(N_C/n₀) be evaluated. Given n₀ directly skips straight to that last step.

Compare the step lists shown in the two modes.

Key Takeaway: Every "find E_F" or "find n₀,p₀" problem in this chapter reduces to the same handful of equations, used in a different order depending on what is already known. Learning to recognize which equation applies — and in what order to apply it — matters more than memorizing four separate procedures.
