Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The band diagram shows all four reference levels at once: EC, the dashed Ei (intrinsic Fermi level), the colored EF, and EV.
  • The info panel reports EF three different ways simultaneously — relative to EC, to EV, and to Ei — plus n₀, p₀, and nᵢ.
  • The orange "near-degenerate" shading within 3kBT of each band edge, and the badge below the readout, warn when the non-degenerate formulas stop being accurate.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: Pick the "Intrinsic" preset and confirm E_F sits almost exactly on the dashed E_i line, with E_F−E_i ≈ 0.
  • Predict: Before switching to the "n-type" preset, predict which direction E_F will move and whether E_F−E_i will become positive or negative.
  • Observe: Switch between "n-type" and "p-type" presets and watch E_F swing from near E_C to near E_V, passing through E_i in between.
  • Explore: Select the "Degenerate n-type" preset and watch the badge turn red as E_F is pushed to (or past) E_C — read the warning text that appears.

Connects to Chapter 10: Once \(n_0\) is known (from the exact carrier concentration equation), the Fermi level follows directly from \(E_C-E_F=k_BT\ln(N_C/n_0)\). The intrinsic Fermi level, \(E_i\), is the special case where \(n_0=p_0=n_i\); it sits close to the middle of the band gap, offset slightly toward the band with the smaller effective density of states. Doping shifts \(E_F\) away from \(E_i\) toward whichever band edge corresponds to the majority carrier — donors push it toward \(E_C\), acceptors push it toward \(E_V\).

What Each Control Does

Preset dropdown
Jumps directly to intrinsic, n-type, p-type, compensated, or heavily-doped (degenerate) doping; switches to "Custom" once you move a slider.
Material dropdown
Selects the effective masses and band gap used to compute N_C, N_V, and E_i's offset from exact midgap.
Temperature slider
Sets T, which affects both nᵢ and the k_BT scale in the E_F formula.
N_D and N_A sliders
Set donor and acceptor concentrations, determining n₀ and therefore E_F's position.

Challenge Questions

1. As N_D is increased far above N_A, what happens to E_F?

Heavy n-type doping raises n0 toward N_D, which (via E_C-E_F=k_BT ln(N_C/n_0)) shrinks E_C-E_F, moving E_F up toward E_C.

Drag N_D to its maximum with N_A at its minimum and watch the E_F line.

2. What does a positive value of E_F − E_i tell you about a sample?

E_F − E_i > 0 means E_F sits above the intrinsic reference, toward E_C — exactly the n-type direction, and (Chapter 9's carrier concentration equations) means n0 > ni.

Try the n-type and p-type presets and compare the sign of E_F − E_i in the readout for each.

3. Why does the badge turn orange or red at very heavy doping?

All of this chapter's equations assume a nondegenerate semiconductor (Chapter 8-9). Once doping is heavy enough that the computed E_F reaches or crosses a band edge, that assumption fails and the displayed position is only a rough estimate.

Try the "Degenerate n-type" preset and read the explanation text under the badge.

Key Takeaway: E_F's position is not three unrelated numbers — E_C−E_F, E_F−E_V, and E_F−E_i are three ways of describing the exact same point, related by the fixed band gap and the intrinsic level's location. Doping moves that one point; it never changes E_C, E_V, or (to more than a small correction) E_i.
