Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Switch material from Silicon to GaAs (much lower κ) at the same power and watch ΔT roughly triple.
  • Try this: Raise the shared temperature T slider and watch I_gen climb by orders of magnitude — n_i(T) grows exponentially with T, so a device running hot leaks far more than the same device at room temperature.
  • Predict: Before raising depletion width W, predict whether Igen will grow, shrink, or stay the same.
  • Observe: At typical parameter values, the generation-current bar sits several orders of magnitude above the fixed diffusion-current reference bar — a direct visual of how much generation current can dominate real diode leakage, and this gap widens further as T rises.

Connects to Chapter 17: Thermal conductivity \(\kappa\) governs how efficiently a device sheds the steady-state heat it dissipates, \(\Delta T=Pt/(\kappa A)\). The device's operating temperature \(T\) also sets the intrinsic carrier concentration \(n_i(T)\) (Chapter 9's Varshni model), so thermal generation rate \(G_{th}=n_i(T)/\tau_0\) inside a depletion region — and the resulting generation current \(I_{gen}=qG_{th}WA\) — both grow with the same T that Panel 1 is trying to conduct away. This is why thermal design (Panel 1) and leakage current (Panel 2) are the same engineering problem, not two unrelated ones.

What Each Control Does

P
Power dissipated through the slab, directly proportional to ΔT (Panel 1 only).
Material
Sets thermal conductivity κ, inversely proportional to ΔT (Panel 1 only).
Shared T
Device lattice temperature, used by both panels — it sets the ambient baseline in Panel 1 and, via n_i(T), directly drives the generation current in Panel 2.
τ0
Generation lifetime; longer τ0 means fewer thermally-generated carriers per second (Panel 2 only).
W
Depletion width; Igen scales directly with W (Panel 2 only).

Challenge Questions

1. Doubling the depletion width W has what effect on the generation current Igen?

Correct. Igen = qGthWA is directly proportional to W, so doubling W doubles Igen.

Look at where W appears in the Igen formula — is it linear, squared, or in the denominator?

2. Why does reverse bias on a real silicon diode tend to increase leakage current beyond the ideal diode equation's prediction?

Correct. Reverse bias widens W (Chapter 15), and since Igen is proportional to W, generation current increases beyond what the ideal diode equation alone predicts.

Recall how reverse bias affects depletion width, and how that width enters the Igen formula.
