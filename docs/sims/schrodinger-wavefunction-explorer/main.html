Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Uncheck "Wave packet" to switch to Eigenstate mode, press Start, and watch the Re[ψ] and Im[ψ] curves ripple in place while the shaded |ψ|² band underneath stays perfectly flat and unchanging.
  • Predict: Before checking "Wave packet" again, predict what will happen to the flat |ψ|² band once you switch to a superposition of several k-components.
  • Observe: In Wave Packet mode, watch the |ψ|² hump drift sideways and slowly widen over time — this is the same spreading behavior connected to the Heisenberg uncertainty tradeoff from earlier in the chapter.
  • Compare: Increase the packet width Δk slider — a wider spread of k-components produces a *narrower* initial packet in space, which then spreads faster. This is Heisenberg's \(\Delta x \Delta p \gtrsim \hbar/2\) in action.
  • Explain: Explain, using the two modes, why a single eigenstate is called a "stationary state" even though its complex phase is visibly rotating.

Connects to Chapter 2: This MicroSim animates the general wavefunction concepts from the "Schrödinger Equation, Wavefunction, and Probability" section — the wavefunction \(\psi(x,t)\) is complex-valued (hence the separate Re and Im curves), and the physically measurable quantity is always the real, non-negative probability density \(|\psi(x,t)|^2\). The single-eigenstate mode uses the plane wave \(\psi=Ae^{i(kx-\omega t)}\) with free-particle dispersion \(\omega \propto k^2\); the wave-packet mode sums seven such components with Gaussian weights, so their differing \(\omega\) values dephase over time, producing real spreading. Animation time here is a dimensionless, dramatized "phase-time," not real seconds — a genuine free electron's phase would rotate far too fast (sub-femtosecond) to animate at human timescales.

What Each Control Does

Start / Stop button
Starts or pauses the time evolution of ψ(x,t); the displayed curves and density freeze in place while stopped.
Wave packet checkbox
Switches between a single plane-wave eigenstate (unchecked) and a 7-component Gaussian wave packet (checked); toggling it resets the animation time.
Central k₀ slider
Sets the central wavenumber shared by all components — higher k₀ means shorter spatial wavelength and faster phase rotation.
Packet width Δk slider
Sets the spacing between the 7 k-components in Wave Packet mode (ignored in Eigenstate mode) — a larger Δk spread produces a more tightly localized initial packet that spreads more quickly.
Animation speed slider
Scales how quickly the illustrative "phase-time" advances each frame.

Challenge Questions

1. In Eigenstate mode, what happens to the probability density |ψ|² while the animation runs?

A single plane-wave eigenstate has time-independent |ψ|² — this is exactly what "stationary state" means, even though Re[ψ] and Im[ψ] individually ripple as the complex phase rotates.

Switch to Eigenstate mode (uncheck "Wave packet"), press Start, and watch the shaded density band at the bottom while the curves above it ripple.

2. What happens to the probability density in Wave Packet mode that does NOT happen in Eigenstate mode?

Summing several k-components with different omega values (the dispersion relation) causes the components to interfere constructively in one region and destructively elsewhere, producing a localized, moving, spreading probability hump — impossible with only one k-component.

Toggle between the two modes and compare the shape and motion of the shaded |ψ|² band at the bottom of the canvas.

3. Why does a real free electron's wavefunction phase rotate far too fast to animate at real-time, human-observable speed?

With realistic nm-scale wavenumbers, the free-particle dispersion \(\omega=\hbar k^2/2m\) gives angular frequencies around 10¹⁶ rad/s — a full phase cycle in a small fraction of a femtosecond, which is why this MicroSim uses a dramatized, illustrative timescale instead.

Reread the "Connects to Chapter 2" note above the controls section.

4. Increasing the packet width Δk slider (spreading the k-components further apart) makes the initial wave packet in space:

This is Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: a wider spread of momentum components (Δk) forces a narrower initial position spread, but that same wide spread of components dephases faster, so the packet spreads more quickly over time.

Set Δk to its minimum, note the initial packet width, then set it to its maximum and compare — clear the pattern by re-toggling the checkbox between comparisons.

5. What physical quantity does the shaded |ψ|² panel represent?

|ψ(x,t)|² is the Born-rule probability density — the relative likelihood of detecting the particle near position x, not a literal charge cloud or a definite trajectory.

Reread the "Connects to Chapter 2" note, which restates the Born interpretation of |ψ|².
