Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The curve is a decaying exponential, Δp(x)=Δp(0)e^(−x/Lp) — steepest right at the injection point (x=0), flattening out as x grows.
  • The dashed Lp line always sits at exactly the point where Δp has fallen to 37% (1/e) of Δp(0), no matter what Dp, τp, or Δp(0) are set to.
  • Turning on "Show continuity-equation terms" reveals that the diffusion term and the recombination term are numerically equal at every position — that equality is exactly what makes the exponential profile a valid steady-state solution.

Guided Exploration

  • Predict: Before increasing τp, predict whether the diffusion length Lp will grow or shrink, and by what factor if τp quadruples.
  • Try this: Drag the position marker along the curve and watch Δp(x) fall off exponentially as you move away from the injection point at x=0.
  • Observe: Increasing either Dp or τp stretches the curve outward and moves the dashed Lp line to the right, since \(L_p=\sqrt{D_p\tau_p}\).
  • Try this: Raise or lower Δp(0) and confirm the curve's shape (and Lp) stays exactly the same — only its height scales, since Lp depends only on Dp and τp, not on the boundary concentration.
  • Predict: Before opening "Show continuity-equation terms," predict whether the diffusion term will be larger, smaller, or equal to the recombination term at the marker.

Connects to Chapter 13: The continuity equation governs how excess carrier concentration evolves in space and time under diffusion, drift, generation, and recombination. Its steady-state solution for carriers injected at one point and diffusing into a long field-free region is a decaying exponential, characterized by the diffusion length \(L_p=\sqrt{D_p\tau_p}\) — the average distance a minority carrier diffuses before recombining. This exact exponential profile is the steady-state carrier profile reused directly in Chapter 15 (p-n junction under bias) to describe minority carriers injected across a forward-biased junction.

What Each Control Does

Δp(0) (boundary concentration)
Sets the excess hole concentration at the injection point x=0. Scales the whole curve up or down without changing its shape or Lp.
Dp (diffusion coefficient)
Sets how quickly holes spread by diffusion, stretching the profile and Lp.
τp (lifetime)
Sets how long holes survive before recombining, also stretching the profile and Lp.
Position marker
Reads off the excess hole concentration at a chosen distance from the injection point.
"Show continuity-equation terms" button
Reveals the diffusion and recombination terms of the steady-state continuity equation at the marker position, and confirms they are equal.

Challenge Questions

1. What does the diffusion length Lp represent physically?

Lp=√(Dpτp) is the characteristic length scale over which excess carriers diffuse before recombining, marking where the profile falls to 1/e of its peak.

Look at where the dashed Lp line falls on the curve — what fraction of the peak value has the curve dropped to there?

2. If you double Δp(0) without changing Dp or τp, what happens to Lp?

Lp=√(Dpτp) depends only on the diffusion coefficient and lifetime — the boundary concentration only scales the curve's height, not its characteristic decay length.

Change the Δp(0) slider and watch the dashed Lp line — does its x-position move at all?

3. With "Show continuity-equation terms" open, why are the diffusion term and the recombination term always equal at the marker?

Substituting Δp(x)=Δp(0)e^(−x/L) into the steady-state continuity equation shows Dp·d²(Δp)/dx² = Δp/τp holds identically for every x, precisely because L was defined as √(Dpτp) — that is the whole reason the exponential is the correct steady-state solution.

Try dragging the position marker to a different x while the panel is open — do the two terms stay equal there too?

Key Takeaway: The exponential profile Δp(x)=Δp(0)e^(−x/Lp) is the steady-state solution of the continuity equation precisely because its curvature makes the diffusion term Dp·d²(Δp)/dx² exactly cancel the recombination loss Δp/τp at every position — a balance that only holds once transients have died out (typically many lifetimes after a step change) and with no time-varying boundary condition or additional bulk generation. Lp=√(Dpτp) sets the curve's shape and decay distance; Δp(0) only sets its height.
