Try This

  • Try this: Drag wavelength from 400 nm down to 150 nm and watch the printed feature bar shrink.
  • Predict: Before raising numerical aperture, predict whether CD will grow or shrink, and what will happen to depth of focus.
  • Observe: Depth of focus shrinks much faster than CD improves as NA increases, since DOF depends on \(1/NA^2\).

Connects to Chapter 19: Photolithography's minimum resolvable feature size follows the Rayleigh criterion \(CD=k_1\lambda/NA\); shrinking wavelength and increasing numerical aperture have driven decades of semiconductor scaling.

What Each Control Does

Wavelength λ
Exposure light wavelength in nanometers.
Numerical aperture NA
Lens numerical aperture; larger NA improves resolution but shrinks depth of focus.
Process constant k1
Process-dependent constant capturing how close to the theoretical limit a given process can print.

Challenge Questions

1. Which change shrinks the minimum resolvable feature size CD?

Correct. Since CD = k1·λ/NA, decreasing wavelength directly shrinks CD.

Look at how CD is defined in terms of λ, NA, and k1.

2. Why does increasing NA to shrink CD create a manufacturing challenge?

Correct. DOF = k2·λ/NA² falls off faster than CD improves, demanding tighter wafer flatness and alignment.

Compare how CD and DOF each depend on NA — one is linear, the other is squared.
