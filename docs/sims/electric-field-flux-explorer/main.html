Connects to Chapter 1: This MicroSim computes the boxed electric field equation \(\vec{E}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\hat{r}\) and the flux equation \(\Phi_E=EA\cos\theta\) from the Electric Flux subsection. Compare your readings to the Concept Check just after Gauss's Law.

What Each Control Does

Charge slider
Sets \(q\) in nanocoulombs — changes both the field's magnitude and its direction (field points away from a positive charge, toward a negative one).
Distance slider
Sets the separation \(d\) between the charge and the test surface — governs the \(1/d^2\) falloff of \(E\) at the surface's location.
Angle slider
Sets \(\theta\), the angle between the field and the surface's normal vector — this is what directly controls flux through \(\cos\theta\), from maximum at 0° to zero at 90°.

Challenge Questions

1. At \(\theta=0°\), the surface's normal is aligned with the field. What is \(\cos(0°)\), and is flux at its maximum or minimum?

\(\cos(0°)=1\), the largest possible value of cosine, so \(\Phi_E=EA\cos\theta\) is at its maximum.

Set the angle slider to 0 in the simulation and read the flux value directly.

2. At \(\theta=90°\), what is the flux \(\Phi_E\)?

\(\cos(90°)=0\), so \(\Phi_E=EA\cos\theta=0\) regardless of how strong \(E\) is — the surface is parallel to the field, so no field lines pass through it.

Set the angle slider to 90 and read the flux readout.

3. At \(\theta=0°\), if you double the distance \(d\) (keeping the charge fixed), what happens to the flux?

Since \(E\propto 1/d^2\) and \(A\), \(\theta\) stay fixed, doubling \(d\) divides \(\Phi_E=EA\cos\theta\) by 4, the same way it divides \(E\) by 4.

Set the angle to 0°, note the flux at d=2.5, then move d to 5 and compare.

4. If the charge is negative, which direction do the field arrows point?

A negative charge's field points inward, toward the charge — the opposite of a positive charge's outward-pointing field.

Set the charge slider to a negative value and look at the arrow directions.

5. Which equation correctly gives the electric flux used in this simulation?

Flux is the field's component perpendicular to the surface, times the area — that is exactly \(EA\cos\theta\), using cosine (not sine) of the angle to the normal.

Re-read the boxed Electric Flux equation in the chapter just above this MicroSim.
