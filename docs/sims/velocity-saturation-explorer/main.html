Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

What to Observe

  • The gray dashed line is the naive linear prediction \(v_d=\mu E\); the purple curve is the actual saturating velocity — they overlap at low field and diverge at high field.
  • The shaded orange-red region marks where the linear approximation exceeds your selected error tolerance — its left edge is the numeric crossover reported below the chart.
  • The dashed green line marks \(v_{sat}\), the value the purple curve asymptotically approaches.

Guided Exploration

  • Try this: At low field (or the "Low" preset), confirm the actual drift velocity curve (purple) tracks the dashed linear-mobility line (v=μE) closely.
  • Predict: Before dragging the field marker to its maximum (or clicking "High"), predict whether drift velocity will keep growing proportionally or level off.
  • Observe: At high field, watch the actual curve bend away from the dashed line and flatten toward the saturation velocity, even though the linear line keeps climbing.
  • Explore: Switch the tolerance dropdown from 5% to 1% and watch the shaded "approximation fails" region grow — a tighter tolerance is exceeded at a lower field.

Connects to Chapter 12: The simple relation \(v_d=\mu E\) (Chapter 11) only holds at low-to-moderate field strength. At high field, carriers gain so much kinetic energy between collisions that scattering becomes far more frequent and effective, and drift velocity stops growing linearly, approaching a material-dependent saturation velocity, \(v_{sat}\), instead — an effect that directly limits how fast short-channel transistors can switch.

What Each Control Does

Carrier type, temperature, and doping controls
Set the low-field mobility used to compute the linear-regime slope (via Chapter 11's Matthiessen's-rule model).
Electric field marker / Low, Moderate, High presets
Reads off drift velocity at a chosen field strength, compared to the linear-mobility prediction.
Tolerance dropdown
Sets the acceptable relative-error threshold (1%, 5%, or 10%) defining the shaded "approximation fails" zone and its numeric crossover field.

Challenge Questions

1. At very high electric field, what does drift velocity approach?

At high field, increased scattering prevents drift velocity from growing further, and it levels off near a material-dependent saturation velocity.

Drag the field marker to its maximum value and see where the purple curve flattens.

2. Predict first: if you switch the tolerance from 5% to 1%, what happens to the field at which the linear approximation is judged to "fail"?

A 1% tolerance is stricter than 5%, so the approximation is judged to fail at a lower field — the shaded region and its left edge both move toward lower E.

Switch the tolerance dropdown and watch the shaded region's left edge move.

Key Takeaway: "Low field" is not an absolute number — it is a comparison between μE and v_sat. The same field can be safely linear for a low-mobility carrier but already saturating for a high-mobility one, which is exactly why short-channel device design must check this ratio explicitly rather than assuming v=μE always holds.
