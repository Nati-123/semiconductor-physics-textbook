<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75;">Why can the Kronig-Penney transcendental equation have no real solution for certain energies?</p>

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Because \(\cos(ka)\) on the right-hand side is restricted to \([-1,1]\), while the left-hand side can exceed this range for some energies
2. Because energy is never conserved in a periodic potential
3. Because electrons cannot have negative energy
4. Because the equation is always solvable for every energy
</div>

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Answer</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #2E7D32; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 0;">Correct Answer: A</p>
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.75; margin-bottom: 0;">\(\cos(ka)\) can only take values between \(-1\) and \(+1\); whenever the left-hand side falls outside that range, no real \(k\) exists, and that energy is forbidden — a band gap.</p>
<p style="color: #555; font-style: italic; margin-bottom: 0; margin-top: 8px;"><strong>Concept Tested:</strong> Kronig-Penney Model</p>
</div>
</details>
