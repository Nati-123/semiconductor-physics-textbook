Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Cycle through all three device types at bias = 0 and compare their equilibrium band diagrams side by side.
  • Predict: Before dragging bias positive on the P-N Junction, predict which way the bands will bend.
  • Observe: All three device types use the exact same construction procedure: flat bands in each neutral region, smooth bending at the junction, and a bias-dependent shift — only the specific materials and presence of an oxide layer differ.

Connects to Chapter 18: Band diagram construction is a single general procedure — flat bands away from junctions, continuous Fermi level(s) at equilibrium, smooth bending at each junction, and bias-dependent shifts — that produces every band diagram used throughout this course, whether for a p-n junction (Chapters 14-15), a Schottky junction, or a MOS capacitor (Chapter 16).

What Each Control Does

Device type
Selects which structure's band diagram to construct.
Bias
Normalized bias condition — negative is reverse bias/depletion, positive is forward bias/accumulation, zero is equilibrium/flat-band.

Challenge Questions

1. What do all three device types' band diagrams have in common?

Correct. Despite looking different, all three follow the same four-step construction procedure described in this chapter.

Compare all three device types at bias=0 — what structural pattern do they share?

2. What makes the MOS capacitor's band diagram structurally different from the other two?

Correct. The MOS capacitor's gate oxide is an insulator with no meaningful conduction/valence band diagram of its own in this simplified picture, unlike the direct semiconductor-to-semiconductor or metal-to-semiconductor contact in the other two structures.

Look at what sits between the gate and the semiconductor in the MOS capacitor diagram, compared to the direct contact in the other two.
