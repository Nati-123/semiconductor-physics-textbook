Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Switch between metals and watch Φ_Bn change directly with Φ_M, while Φ_Bp changes in the opposite direction (since they must sum to the band gap).
  • Predict: Before switching doping type from n-type to p-type at the same concentration, predict whether Φ_S will increase or decrease.
  • Observe: Raising doping concentration always pulls Φ_S toward χ (for n-type) or toward χ+Eg (for p-type), since heavier doping pulls EF closer to the nearby band edge.

Connects to Chapter 16: A metal's work function and a semiconductor's electron affinity (combined with its doping-dependent Fermi level) determine the barrier height at a metal-semiconductor junction. The band bending required to align Fermi levels at contact gives the built-in potential, directly analogous to the p-n junction's V_bi from Chapter 14.

What Each Control Does

Metal
Sets the metal work function Φ_M, which directly sets the electron barrier height Φ_Bn = Φ_M − χ.
Doping type
Switches between n-type and p-type semiconductor, changing how Φ_S is computed.
N
Sets doping concentration, which moves EF and therefore Φ_S.

Challenge Questions

1. What sets the electron barrier height Φ_Bn on n-type material?

Correct. Φ_Bn = Φ_M − χ (the Schottky-Mott rule) depends only on the metal and the semiconductor's electron affinity, not on doping.

Look at the Φ_Bn formula shown in the results card — which two quantities does it subtract?

2. Why do Φ_Bn and Φ_Bp always sum to the band gap Eg?

Correct. Φ_Bn measures from the metal Fermi level to EC, and Φ_Bp measures from the metal Fermi level to EV on the other side — together they span exactly EC to EV, which is Eg.

Think about what EC minus EV equals, and where the metal Fermi level sits relative to both band edges at the interface.
