Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Select "Silicon" and read its full property card: crystal structure, band gap, mobility, melting point.
  • Predict: Before switching to "Germanium", predict whether its band gap will be larger or smaller than silicon's.
  • Observe: Switch through all three materials and drag the temperature slider on each, watching the band-gap number and the marker on the curve update together.

Connects to Chapter 7: Each material's properties are fixed by its atomic structure and bonding, but its band gap specifically shrinks somewhat as temperature rises — the lattice expands and atomic vibrations increase, both of which reduce the gap. This dashboard uses the empirical Varshni equation, \(E_g(T)=E_g(0)-\dfrac{\alpha T^2}{T+\beta}\), to compute the temperature-dependent band gap for whichever material is selected.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects silicon, germanium, or GaAs and loads its property card and Varshni parameters.
Temperature slider
Sets the temperature (0–600 K) used to compute the current band gap via the Varshni equation.

Challenge Questions

1. What happens to a material's band gap as temperature increases, according to this dashboard?

The Varshni equation predicts a shrinking band gap with rising temperature, since lattice expansion and increased atomic vibration both act to reduce the gap.

Drag the temperature slider up on any material and watch the band gap readout.

2. Which of the three materials is direct-gap, as noted on its property card?

GaAs is direct-gap; silicon and germanium are both indirect-gap, as covered in Chapter 6.

Read the "Band gap type" line on each material's property card.
