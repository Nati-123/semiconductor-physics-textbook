What to Observe

  • The gold tangent line's slope at the marker position is exactly \(dp/dx\) (or \(dn/dx\)) — steeper tangent, larger diffusion current.
  • D is not a fixed constant here — it is computed live from the Einstein relation using the temperature and doping sliders, so changing T changes J_diffusion even at a fixed gradient.
  • The second chart plots J_diffusion(x), J_drift(x), and J_total(x) together across the whole bar — watch where the total curve crosses zero.
  • Switching the carrier type flips the sign of J_diffusion for the same decaying profile, while J_drift keeps the same sign structure — conventional current direction depends on the carrier's charge, and diffusion current depends on it twice (once through the flux direction, once through the charge sign) while drift current only depends on it once.

Connects to Chapter 12: Fick's law states that diffusion current is proportional to the local concentration gradient, \(dp/dx\) or \(dn/dx\) — steeper gradients drive more current. Combined with Chapter 11's drift current, the total current density at any point is simply the sum, \(J=J_{\text{drift}}+J_{\text{diffusion}}\), the fundamental equation underlying carrier transport in every semiconductor device studied in the chapters ahead — including the exact drift-diffusion balance that sets up equilibrium at a p-n junction (Chapter 14). Here, \(D\) itself comes from the Einstein relation rather than being assumed fixed, tying this MicroSim directly to the previous section's result.

What Each Control Does

Carrier type dropdown
Switches between a hole (p-type) and electron (n-type) concentration profile, flipping the diffusion-current sign convention accordingly.
Decay length L slider
Sets how quickly the concentration profile decays with position, controlling the concentration gradient's steepness.
Position marker slider
Selects the position at which the local gradient, diffusion current, and cancellation field are evaluated.
Electric field slider
Adds a drift current contribution (proportional to the local carrier concentration), combined with diffusion into the total.
Temperature and doping sliders
Set the majority-carrier mobility (Matthiessen's rule) and therefore D via the Einstein relation.
"Snap to cancellation field" button
Sets the electric field to the value where drift current exactly cancels diffusion current at the marker position, so J_total ≈ 0 there.

Challenge Questions

1. Where along the concentration profile is diffusion current largest?

Fick's law makes diffusion current proportional to the gradient dp/dx, not the concentration itself — the steepest part of the curve produces the largest diffusion current.

Drag the position marker to different points and compare the tangent line's steepness to the diffusion current readout.

2. Predict first: if you raise the temperature slider without moving the position marker or changing L, what happens to J_diffusion?

J_diffusion = -qD_p(dp/dx); even with dp/dx fixed, D_p = μ_p·k_BT/q changes with T both directly (the k_BT factor) and indirectly (mobility's own T-dependence), so J_diffusion changes too.

Watch the "D_p = ..." readout below the title as you drag the temperature slider.

3. Predict first: with the same decaying concentration profile, you switch the carrier type from Holes to Electrons without changing anything else. What happens to J_diffusion at the marker?

Both carriers physically diffuse from high to low concentration, but conventional current direction depends on the carrier's charge sign too — so the same decaying profile produces diffusion current in opposite conventional directions for electrons vs. holes.

Switch the carrier dropdown at a fixed marker position and L, and compare the sign of J_diffusion in the results card before and after.

4. At the marker position, you click "Snap to cancellation field." What is now true there?

Drift and diffusion currents can individually be large and nonzero while exactly canceling in sum — this is precisely the condition that sets up the built-in field at a p-n junction in equilibrium (Chapter 14).

Look at the J_diffusion and J_drift lines in the results card right after snapping — neither reads zero, but J_total does.

Key Takeaway: Diffusion current depends on two independent things — how steep the concentration profile is (geometry) and how easily carriers move through the material (D, itself set by temperature and doping through mobility). Changing either one changes J_diffusion, even if the other stays fixed. Drift and diffusion can be individually large yet exactly cancel in sum — the same balance that holds a p-n junction in equilibrium — and switching carrier type flips the diffusion current's sign for an identical concentration profile, because conventional current direction depends on both the flux direction and the carrier's charge sign.
