Try This

  • Try this: Select "Insulator" and observe the wide white (empty, g(E)=0) gap between the green valence band and blue conduction band, with the dashed E_F line sitting in the middle of that empty gap.
  • Predict: Before switching to "Semiconductor", predict whether the gap will be wider or narrower than the insulator's.
  • Observe: Switch to "Semiconductor" and confirm the gap is much narrower, though the qualitative picture (full valence band, empty conduction band at T=0) is otherwise identical.
  • Switch to "Metal" and notice that E_F now sits inside a region where the density-of-states curve is nonzero on both sides — no gap at all.
  • Switch to "Semimetal" and zoom in (visually) on how thin the density-of-states curve is right at E_F, compared to the Metal preset.
  • Drag the thermal energy kT slider up from 0 and watch the sharp occupied/empty boundary near E_F blur into a smooth gradient in both panels.

Connects to Chapter 6: This MicroSim draws the density of states \(g_v(E)\propto\sqrt{E_v-E}\) and \(g_c(E)\propto\sqrt{E-E_c}\) for a valence-like and conduction-like band, shaded by the Fermi-Dirac occupation probability \(f(E)=1/[1+\exp((E-E_F)/k_BT)]\). Whether the two bands overlap in energy (Metal, Semimetal) or are separated by a real gap (Semiconductor, Insulator), and how large that gap or overlap is, is exactly what this chapter uses to classify a material's band structure.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects a preset band-edge configuration (valence-band top, conduction-band bottom, and Fermi level) representative of a metal, insulator, semiconductor, or semimetal.
Thermal energy kT slider
Sets \(k_BT\) in the Fermi-Dirac distribution; larger values smear the sharp T=0 occupied/empty boundary near E_F.

Challenge Questions

1. In the Insulator preset, where does the Fermi level E_F sit?

E_F sits inside the wide band gap, where the density of states is exactly zero — consistent with the fact that no real state exists at E_F in an insulator.

Look at where the dashed E_F line crosses relative to the green (valence) and blue (conduction) shaded regions.

2. What distinguishes the Metal preset from the Semiconductor and Insulator presets?

The Metal preset's valence-like and conduction-like bands overlap in energy, so E_F sits where g(E) > 0 — real states exist right at E_F, unlike the semiconductor and insulator cases.

Compare where the green and blue curves are nonzero relative to E_F in the Metal preset versus the Semiconductor preset.

3. What happens to the sharp occupied/empty boundary near E_F as you increase the thermal energy kT slider?

Increasing kT smooths the Fermi-Dirac step function, smearing the occupied/empty boundary over an energy range of order kT around E_F, visible as a fading gradient rather than a hard edge.

Drag the kT slider from its minimum to a larger value and watch the boundary near the dashed E_F line.
