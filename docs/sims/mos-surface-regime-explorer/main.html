Connects to Chapter 16: Sweeping gate voltage moves the semiconductor surface through accumulation, depletion mode, weak inversion, and strong inversion. Strong inversion begins exactly when the surface electron concentration reaches the bulk doping concentration NA, which happens precisely at \(\psi_s=2\phi_F\) — the condition that defines the threshold voltage.

What Each Control Does

ψs
Surface potential; sweeping it moves through all four regimes.
NA
Substrate doping, which sets φF and therefore where the weak- and strong-inversion boundaries fall.

Challenge Questions

1. At what surface potential does strong inversion begin?

Correct. At ψs = 2φF, the surface electron concentration ns equals the bulk hole concentration NA, defining the onset of strong inversion.

Watch where the ns(ψs) curve crosses the dashed NA reference line on the chart.

2. What happens to ns as ψs increases, according to the plotted curve?

Correct. ns = np0·e^(ψs/VT) grows exponentially with ψs, which is why it appears as a straight line on this log-scale chart.

Notice that the y-axis is a log scale — what does a straight line on a log-scale y-axis mean about the underlying growth?
