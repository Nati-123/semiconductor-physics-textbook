Connects to Chapter 7: A semiconductor's melting point directly limits how it can be processed. Silicon's high melting point (1414°C) lets it survive the high-temperature oxidation and diffusion steps used in standard fabrication; germanium's much lower melting point (938°C) restricts the thermal budget available for processing it; and GaAs, while its melting point is reasonably high (1238°C), is mechanically more fragile and loses arsenic preferentially at high temperature, requiring specialized growth techniques.

Challenge Questions

1. Why does silicon's high melting point matter for chip fabrication?

Standard silicon processing (thermal oxidation, dopant diffusion/annealing) requires sustained high temperatures well below silicon's melting point, giving it a wide thermal processing window.
