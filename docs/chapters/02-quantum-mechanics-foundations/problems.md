## Easy

### Problem 1

State the de Broglie hypothesis in words (no equation).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Every particle with momentum, not just light, has an associated wave character; its wavelength is inversely proportional to its momentum. This extends wave-particle duality, first observed for light, to all matter.</p>
</div>

### Problem 2

Write the de Broglie wavelength formula and identify the SI unit of each quantity in it.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda = h/p = h/(mv)\)</span>; wavelength in meters (m), Planck's constant \(h\) in joule-seconds (J·s), momentum \(p\) in kg·m/s, mass \(m\) in kilograms (kg), speed \(v\) in meters per second (m/s).</p>

### Problem 4

State the Heisenberg uncertainty principle as an inequality, and explain in one sentence what it means.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(\Delta x\,\Delta p \geq \hbar/2\)</span>: the uncertainties in a particle's position and momentum cannot both be made arbitrarily small at the same time — their product has a fundamental, non-negotiable lower bound.</p>

What boundary conditions must the particle-in-a-box wavefunction \(\psi(x)\) satisfy at \(x=0\) and \(x=L\), and why?

### Problem 6

Write the normalization condition for a wavefunction \(\psi(x)\) defined over all space, and explain its physical meaning in one sentence.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>

<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(x)|^2\,dx = 1\)</span>: since the particle must be found somewhere, the total probability of finding it anywhere in space must equal exactly 1.</p>

### Problem 7

An electron has momentum \(p = 2.0\times10^{-24}\) kg·m/s. Find its de Broglie wavelength.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">

</div>
</details>

### Problem 9

Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron with kinetic energy \(KE = 50\) eV.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(KE = 50\times1.602\times10^{-19} = 8.01\times10^{-18}\ \text{J}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(v=\sqrt{2KE/m}=\sqrt{2(8.01\times10^{-18})/(9.109\times10^{-31})}=4.19\times10^{6}\ \text{m/s}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(p=mv=3.82\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg·m/s}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda=h/p=(6.626\times10^{-34})/(3.82\times10^{-24})=1.73\times10^{-10}\ \text{m}=0.173\ \text{nm}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 10

An electron is confined to a region \(\Delta x = 5.0\times10^{-11}\) m. Estimate the minimum uncertainty in its momentum, and convert this to a velocity uncertainty.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(\Delta p \geq \hbar/(2\Delta x) = (1.0546\times10^{-34})/(2\times5.0\times10^{-11}) = 1.05\times10^{-24}\ \text{kg·m/s}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(\Delta v = \Delta p/m = (1.05\times10^{-24})/(9.109\times10^{-31}) = 1.16\times10^{6}\ \text{m/s}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 11

Verify that \(\psi_2(x) = \sqrt{2/L}\,\sin(2\pi x/L)\) is properly normalized on \(0<x<L\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(\int_0^L|\psi_2|^2\,dx = \dfrac{2}{L}\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin^2(2\pi x/L)\,dx\)</span>. Using <span class="arithmatex">\(\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12(1-\cos2\theta)\)</span>, the integral evaluates to <span class="arithmatex">\(L/2\)</span> (the cosine term vanishes at both limits since <span class="arithmatex">\(\sin(4\pi)=\sin(0)=0\)</span>). So the total is <span class="arithmatex">\((2/L)(L/2)=1\)</span>, confirming normalization.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 12

Find the ground-state energy of an electron confined to a box of width \(L=2.0\) nm.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(E_1 = \dfrac{h^2}{8mL^2} = \dfrac{(6.626\times10^{-34})^2}{8(9.109\times10^{-31})(2.0\times10^{-9})^2} = 1.51\times10^{-20}\ \text{J} = 0.0940\ \text{eV}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 13

For an electron confined to a box of width \(L=0.5\) nm, find the wavelength of a photon absorbed in a transition from \(n=1\) to \(n=2\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(E_1 = h^2/(8mL^2) = 1.504\ \text{eV}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(E_2 = 4E_1 = 6.016\ \text{eV}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(\Delta E = 3E_1 = 4.512\ \text{eV} = 7.23\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda = hc/\Delta E = (6.626\times10^{-34})(2.998\times10^{8})/(7.23\times10^{-19}) = 2.75\times10^{-7}\ \text{m} = 275\ \text{nm}\)</span> (ultraviolet).</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 14

If the width \(L\) of a particle-in-a-box is halved, by what factor does the ground-state energy \(E_1\) change?

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Since <span class="arithmatex">\(E_1 \propto 1/L^2\)</span>, halving <span class="arithmatex">\(L\)</span> multiplies <span class="arithmatex">\(L^2\)</span> by <span class="arithmatex">\(1/4\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(E_1\)</span> increases by a factor of <span class="arithmatex">\(4\)</span> — the ground-state energy quadruples.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 15

An electron of energy \(E=1.0\) eV approaches a rectangular barrier of height \(V_0=2.0\) eV and width \(L=0.5\) nm. Estimate the transmission probability.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(V_0-E = 1.0\ \text{eV} = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(\kappa = \sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}/\hbar = 5.12\times10^{9}\ \text{m}^{-1}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(\kappa L = (5.12\times10^{9})(5.0\times10^{-10}) = 2.56\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(T\approx e^{-2\kappa L} = e^{-5.12} \approx 5.95\times10^{-3}\)</span>, about a 0.6% chance of tunneling through.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 16

A proton (\(m_p=1.673\times10^{-27}\) kg) and an electron each have kinetic energy \(1.0\) eV. Which has the longer de Broglie wavelength, and by roughly what factor?

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Since <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda = h/\sqrt{2mKE}\)</span> for fixed <span class="arithmatex">\(KE\)</span>, wavelength scales as <span class="arithmatex">\(1/\sqrt{m}\)</span>. The electron, being far less massive, has the longer wavelength, by a factor of <span class="arithmatex">\(\sqrt{m_p/m_e} = \sqrt{1836} \approx 42.8\)</span>. Numerically, <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda_e \approx 1.23\ \text{nm}\)</span> versus <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda_p \approx 0.0286\ \text{nm}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>

## Difficult

### Problem 17

For the ground state (\(n=1\)) of a particle in a box of width \(L\), find the probability of finding the particle in the middle third of the box, \(L/3 < x < 2L/3\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(P = \displaystyle\int_{L/3}^{2L/3}\dfrac{2}{L}\sin^2(\pi x/L)\,dx = \dfrac{1}{L}\Big[x-\dfrac{L}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x/L)\Big]_{L/3}^{2L/3}\)</span>. At <span class="arithmatex">\(x=2L/3\)</span>: <span class="arithmatex">\(\sin(4\pi/3)=-\sqrt3/2\)</span>, giving bracket value <span class="arithmatex">\(0.805L\)</span>. At <span class="arithmatex">\(x=L/3\)</span>: <span class="arithmatex">\(\sin(2\pi/3)=+\sqrt3/2\)</span>, giving bracket value <span class="arithmatex">\(0.195L\)</span>. So <span class="arithmatex">\(P = (0.805L-0.195L)/L \approx 0.609\)</span>, about 60.9% — noticeably higher than the classical (uniform) probability of 1/3, since the ground-state probability density peaks at the center of the box.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 18

Show that the normalization constant \(A=\sqrt{2/L}\) for \(\psi_n(x)=A\sin(n\pi x/L)\) on \(0<x<L\) is the same for every positive integer \(n\), not just \(n=1\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Require <span class="arithmatex">\(A^2\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin^2(n\pi x/L)\,dx=1\)</span>. Using <span class="arithmatex">\(\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12(1-\cos2\theta)\)</span>: <span class="arithmatex">\(\displaystyle\int_0^L\sin^2(n\pi x/L)\,dx = \dfrac12\Big[x-\dfrac{L}{2n\pi}\sin(2n\pi x/L)\Big]_0^L = \dfrac12\big[L-0-0+0\big]=\dfrac{L}{2}\)</span>, since <span class="arithmatex">\(\sin(2n\pi)=0\)</span> for every integer <span class="arithmatex">\(n\)</span>. So <span class="arithmatex">\(A^2(L/2)=1\)</span> regardless of <span class="arithmatex">\(n\)</span>, giving <span class="arithmatex">\(A=\sqrt{2/L}\)</span> for every eigenstate.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 19

An electron in a box has ground-state energy \(E_1=0.50\) eV. Find the box width \(L\), then find the wavelength of a photon that would excite the electron from \(n=1\) to \(n=3\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">Solving <span class="arithmatex">\(E_1=h^2/(8mL^2)\)</span> for <span class="arithmatex">\(L\)</span>: <span class="arithmatex">\(L^2 = h^2/(8mE_1) = (6.626\times10^{-34})^2/[8(9.109\times10^{-31})(8.01\times10^{-20})] = 7.52\times10^{-19}\ \text{m}^2\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(L \approx 8.67\times10^{-10}\ \text{m} = 0.867\ \text{nm}\)</span>. For the <span class="arithmatex">\(n=1\to n=3\)</span> transition: <span class="arithmatex">\(E_3=9E_1=4.50\ \text{eV}\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(\Delta E=8E_1=4.00\ \text{eV}=6.41\times10^{-19}\ \text{J}\)</span>. <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda=hc/\Delta E = 3.10\times10^{-7}\ \text{m}=310\ \text{nm}\)</span> (ultraviolet).</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 20

Use the Heisenberg uncertainty principle with \(\Delta x \approx L = 1.0\) nm to estimate the minimum kinetic energy of an electron confined to a box of width \(L\), and compare this order-of-magnitude estimate to the exact ground-state energy \(E_1=0.376\) eV computed in Worked Example 7.

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;"><span class="arithmatex">\(\Delta p \geq \hbar/(2\Delta x) = (1.0546\times10^{-34})/(2\times10^{-9}) = 5.27\times10^{-26}\ \text{kg·m/s}\)</span>. Estimating <span class="arithmatex">\(KE\approx(\Delta p)^2/(2m) = (5.27\times10^{-26})^2/(2\times9.109\times10^{-31}) \approx 1.53\times10^{-21}\ \text{J} \approx 0.0095\ \text{eV}\)</span>. This is roughly 40 times smaller than the exact value of <span class="arithmatex">\(0.376\)</span> eV. The uncertainty principle only guarantees a lower bound and correct order-of-magnitude scaling with <span class="arithmatex">\(L\)</span>; it does not reproduce the exact numerical prefactor, which requires solving the boundary-value problem exactly, as done in the Particle in a Box section.</p>
</div>
</details>

### Problem 21

A tunneling barrier has decay constant \(\kappa = 3.0\times10^{9}\ \text{m}^{-1}\). Find the barrier width \(L\) required for the transmission probability to equal \(1.0\times10^{-6}\).

<details style="margin-top: 1rem;">
<summary style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; cursor: pointer;">Show Solution</summary>
<div style="background: #E7F7E7; border: 2px solid #81C784; border-radius: 10px; padding: 18px 22px; margin-top: 10px;">
<p style="color: #333; line-height: 1.85; margin: 0;">From <span class="arithmatex">\(T\approx e^{-2\kappa L}\)</span>, take the natural log of both sides: <span class="arithmatex">\(\ln T = -2\kappa L\)</span>, so <span class="arithmatex">\(L = -\ln(T)/(2\kappa) = -\ln(1.0\times10^{-6})/(2\times3.0\times10^{9}) = 13.82/(6.0\times10^{9}) = 2.30\times10^{-9}\ \text{m} = 2.30\ \text{nm}\)</span>.</p>
</div>
</details>
