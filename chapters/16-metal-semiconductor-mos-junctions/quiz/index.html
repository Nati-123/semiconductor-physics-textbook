    Quiz: Metal-Semiconductor and MOS Junctions

    Test your understanding of metal-semiconductor and MOS junctions covered in Chapter 16 with these 26 questions.

    Question 1

    What is a metal-semiconductor junction?

    1. A junction formed wherever a metal is brought into intimate contact with a semiconductor
    2. Two semiconductors of different doping joined together
    3. A junction that only exists inside a MOS capacitor
    4. A junction that requires an insulating layer between the two materials
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    A metal-semiconductor junction forms wherever a metal contacts a semiconductor directly — every bond pad, gate electrode, and wire contact in a real device.

    Concept Tested: Metal-Semiconductor Junction

    Question 2

    What is a metal's work function?

    1. The energy required to remove an electron from the metal's Fermi level to the vacuum level
    2. The energy gap between conduction and valence bands
    3. The metal's electrical conductivity
    4. The metal's melting temperature
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Work function qΦM measures how tightly a metal's electron sea holds its electrons, from the Fermi level to vacuum.

    Concept Tested: Work Function

    Question 3

    What is electron affinity, and how does it depend on doping?

    1. The energy from vacuum to EC; it is a fixed material property independent of doping
    2. The energy from vacuum to EF; it changes strongly with doping
    3. The band gap; it depends on temperature only
    4. The energy needed to ionize a dopant atom
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    Electron affinity qχ is the fixed energy from vacuum to EC; it is the semiconductor's own work function ΦS = χ + (EC−EF)/q that depends on doping.

    Concept Tested: Electron Affinity

    Question 4

    What is the Schottky-Mott formula for barrier height on n-type material?

    1. ΦB = ΦM − χ
    2. ΦB = ΦM + χ
    3. ΦB = χ − ΦM
    4. ΦB = Eg/2
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    ΦB = ΦM − χ is the idealized Schottky-Mott barrier height for electrons on n-type material.

    Concept Tested: Barrier Height

    Question 5

    Why do the electron and hole barrier heights on the same junction always sum to the band gap Eg?

    1. They don't — this is only true for one specific metal
    2. Together they span the full distance from EC to EV at the interface
    3. Because ΦM always equals Eg
    4. Because doping cancels out completely
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    ΦBn measures from the metal Fermi level to EC, ΦBp from the metal Fermi level to EV — together they span EC to EV, exactly Eg.

    Concept Tested: Barrier Height

    Question 6

    What is a Schottky barrier?

    1. A rectifying depletion region at a metal-semiconductor junction
    2. An insulating layer in a MOS capacitor
    3. A type of ohmic contact
    4. A p-n junction under reverse bias
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    A Schottky barrier is the rectifying depletion region formed at a metal-semiconductor junction when work-function alignment produces a barrier.

    Concept Tested: Schottky Barrier

    Question 7

    What formulas describe a Schottky barrier's depletion width and peak field?

    1. Entirely different formulas from any p-n junction result
    2. The same one-sided-junction formulas from Chapter 14, treating the metal as an infinitely-doped side
    3. Only the ideal diode equation
    4. There is no depletion region in a Schottky barrier
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    The Schottky barrier's electrostatics reuse the same one-sided p-n junction formulas from Chapter 14, with the metal treated as an infinitely-doped "other side."

    Concept Tested: Schottky Barrier

    Question 8

    What current mechanism dominates in a Schottky diode?

    1. Minority-carrier diffusion, exactly as in a p-n diode
    2. Thermionic emission of majority carriers over the barrier
    3. Avalanche multiplication
    4. Zener tunneling
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Schottky diode current is dominated by thermionic emission — majority carriers with enough thermal energy going directly over the barrier.

    Concept Tested: Schottky Diode

    Question 9

    Why do Schottky diodes typically switch faster than p-n diodes?

    1. They have a larger band gap
    2. There is no minority-carrier injection or storage to remove when switching off
    3. They operate at higher temperature
    4. They have a larger junction area
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Schottky diodes are purely majority-carrier devices, so there is no stored minority charge to remove when switching off, unlike a p-n diode.

    Concept Tested: Schottky Diode

    Question 10

    What is an ohmic contact?

    1. A rectifying, diode-like junction
    2. A low-resistance metal-semiconductor connection with symmetric, linear I-V behavior
    3. A contact that only works at high temperature
    4. Another name for a Schottky barrier
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    An ohmic contact is engineered to have essentially no barrier, giving low-resistance, symmetric current flow in both directions.

    Concept Tested: Ohmic Contact

    Question 11

    On p-type material, which condition produces a rectifying contact?

    1. ΦM = ΦS exactly
    2. ΦM > ΦS
    3. ΦM < ΦS
    4. Doping type never matters
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    On p-type material, a rectifying contact forms when ΦM < ΦS — the opposite rule from n-type material.

    Concept Tested: Rectifying Contact

    Question 12

    What three layers make up a MOS capacitor?

    1. Two semiconductors and a metal
    2. A gate, an insulating gate oxide, and a semiconductor
    3. Two metals and an oxide
    4. A p-n junction and a Schottky barrier
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    A MOS capacitor stacks a gate, an insulating gate oxide, and a semiconductor substrate.

    Concept Tested: MOS Capacitor

    Question 13

    What role does the gate oxide play in a MOS capacitor?

    1. It conducts DC current between gate and substrate
    2. It blocks DC current while transmitting the electric field
    3. It is a second semiconductor layer
    4. It has no electrical function
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    The gate oxide is an excellent insulator, blocking DC current while still transmitting the electric field that controls the surface electrostatically.

    Concept Tested: Gate Oxide

    Question 14

    What is the flat-band voltage?

    1. The gate voltage at which the semiconductor bands show no bending at all
    2. The threshold voltage of a MOSFET
    3. The voltage at which the oxide breaks down
    4. Always exactly 0 V
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    VFB = ΦM − ΦS is the gate voltage that exactly cancels the built-in bending, leaving the semiconductor bands flat.

    Concept Tested: Flat-Band Voltage

    Question 15

    What happens at the semiconductor surface in the accumulation region?

    1. Majority carriers are repelled from the surface
    2. Majority carriers pile up at the surface
    3. Minority carriers form a conducting channel
    4. The surface becomes intrinsic
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    In accumulation (ψs < 0 for p-type), majority carriers are attracted to and accumulate at the surface.

    Concept Tested: Accumulation Region

    Question 16

    What happens at the semiconductor surface in depletion mode?

    1. Majority carriers pile up at the surface
    2. Majority carriers are repelled, exposing fixed ionized dopant charge
    3. An inversion layer forms immediately
    4. The oxide becomes conductive
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    In depletion mode (0 < ψs < φF), majority carriers are repelled from the surface, exposing fixed ionized dopant charge, analogous to a p-n junction depletion region.

    Concept Tested: Depletion Mode

    Question 17

    What is the inversion layer, and why does it matter for a MOSFET?

    1. A layer of fixed oxide charge; it has no functional role
    2. A thin layer of minority carriers at the surface; it is the MOSFET's conducting channel
    3. The metal gate itself
    4. A layer of majority carriers in the bulk
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    The inversion layer is a thin sheet of minority carriers at the surface, forming the MOSFET's conducting channel once strong inversion is reached.

    Concept Tested: Inversion Layer

    Question 18

    What is the threshold voltage formula?

    1. VT = VFB + 2φF + Qdep,max/Cox
    2. VT = VFB − φF
    3. VT = Cox/Qdep,max
    4. VT = φF only
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    VT combines the flat-band correction, the surface potential at threshold (2φF), and the voltage needed across the oxide to support maximum depletion charge.

    Concept Tested: Threshold Voltage

    Question 19

    Making the gate oxide thinner (increasing Cox) has what effect on threshold voltage's third term, Qdep,max/Cox?

    1. It increases
    2. It decreases
    3. It stays exactly the same
    4. It becomes negative
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Since Qdep,max does not depend on oxide thickness, a larger Cox (thinner oxide) directly shrinks the ratio Qdep,max/Cox.

    Concept Tested: Threshold Voltage

    Question 20

    What does the surface potential ψs represent?

    1. The total gate voltage
    2. The band bending at the semiconductor surface, measured relative to the bulk
    3. The oxide's dielectric constant
    4. The metal's work function
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Surface potential ψs quantifies band bending at the surface relative to the bulk, with ψs = 0 defined at flat-band.

    Concept Tested: Surface Potential

    Question 21

    What is the oxide capacitance formula?

    1. Cox = εox·tox
    2. Cox = εox/tox
    3. Cox = tox/εox
    4. Cox = 1/(εox·tox)
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Cox = εox/tox follows directly from the parallel-plate capacitor formula applied to the gate oxide.

    Concept Tested: Oxide Capacitance

    Question 22

    What surface potential condition marks the onset of strong inversion?

    1. ψs = 0
    2. ψs = φF
    3. ψs = 2φF
    4. ψs = −φF
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: C

    Strong inversion begins at ψs = 2φF, where surface minority-carrier concentration equals bulk majority-carrier concentration.

    Concept Tested: Strong Inversion

    Question 23

    At the strong-inversion threshold, how does surface electron concentration ns compare to bulk hole concentration NA (p-type substrate)?

    1. ns is always much smaller than NA
    2. ns exactly equals NA
    3. ns is always much larger than NA
    4. There is no relationship between them
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    At ψs = 2φF, ns = np0·e^(2φF/VT) works out to exactly NA — the defining condition of strong inversion.

    Concept Tested: Strong Inversion

    Question 24

    What characterizes weak inversion?

    1. Surface minority carrier concentration growing but still below the bulk majority concentration
    2. The surface is fully accumulated
    3. The surface has zero band bending
    4. The oxide is conducting
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    In weak inversion (φF < ψs < 2φF), minority carrier concentration is growing rapidly but has not yet reached the bulk majority concentration — responsible for subthreshold MOSFET conduction.

    Concept Tested: Weak Inversion

    Question 25

    What determines the electrostatic state of the semiconductor surface in a MOS capacitor?

    1. Current flowing through the gate oxide
    2. Gate voltage, acting purely electrostatically since no current flows through the oxide
    3. The metal's melting point
    4. The semiconductor's crystal structure alone
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: B

    Because no DC current flows through the insulating gate oxide, the semiconductor surface's condition is set entirely by electrostatics, controlled by gate voltage.

    Concept Tested: Semiconductor Surface

    Question 26

    Why do real fabrication processes typically need a dedicated threshold-adjustment implant, beyond just choosing gate material, doping, and oxide thickness?

    1. Because the ideal VT formula's three terms alone often land at an unusably low, marginal, or even negative value for practical gate/doping/oxide combinations
    2. Because threshold voltage cannot be calculated at all without an implant
    3. Because oxide capacitance is otherwise undefined
    4. Because Schottky diodes require it
    Show Answer

    Correct Answer: A

    As the chapter's worked examples showed, realistic gate/doping/oxide combinations can give a low or even negative ideal VT — a dedicated implant adds controlled extra charge to bring VT to a usable target value, exactly the physics that sets up the MOSFET devices in Chapter 18.

    Concept Tested: Threshold Voltage
