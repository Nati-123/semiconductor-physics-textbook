Connects to Chapter 8: This MicroSim combines a simplified dopant-ionization-fraction model with the Chapter 7 intrinsic-carrier trend to sketch the three characteristic temperature regions: freeze-out (few dopants ionized), extrinsic/saturation (essentially all dopants ionized, carrier concentration ≈ N_D), and intrinsic (thermally-generated carriers overwhelm the doping). The exact charge-neutrality equations behind this curve are developed quantitatively in Chapters 9-10; here, the combination \(n(T)\approx N_D f_{ion}(T) + n_i(T)\) is used only to sketch the qualitative shape.

What Each Control Does

Donor concentration slider (N_D)
Sets the doping concentration, which sets the height of the extrinsic plateau.
Ionization energy slider (E_D)
Sets the dopant's ionization energy, which controls how far the freeze-out region extends.

Challenge Questions

1. In the extrinsic (saturation) region, what does the carrier concentration approximately equal?

In the extrinsic region, essentially all donors are ionized and intrinsic generation is still negligible, so carrier concentration levels off at approximately N_D — the flat plateau in the middle of the curve.

Look at the flat, middle portion of the curve and compare its height to the N_D slider value.

2. Why does the curve eventually rise steeply again at very high temperature, regardless of doping level?

At high enough temperature, intrinsic thermal generation (which grows exponentially with T) eventually produces more carriers than the fixed donor concentration can supply, and the material behaves as if intrinsic.

Compare the rightmost part of the curve (high T) to the intrinsic carrier trend from Chapter 7.
