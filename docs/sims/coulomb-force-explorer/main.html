Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Click Reset, then read off all four values (\(F\), \(E\), \(V\), \(U\)) at the default settings.
  • Predict: Before dragging \(r\) from 1 nm to 2 nm, predict which will shrink more: \(F\) or \(V\).
  • Observe: Flip the sign of \(q_2\) only (keep its magnitude) and watch which readouts change sign and which stay exactly the same.
  • Explain: Explain why \(E\) and \(V\) do not depend on \(q_2\) at all, even though the diagram shows the field "at \(q_2\)'s location."

Connects to Chapter 1: This MicroSim computes the four boxed equations from the Coulomb's Law and Electric Potential sections — \(F=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}\), \(E=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1}{r^2}\), \(V=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1}{r}\), and \(U=q_2V(r)\). Compare your readings to Worked Examples 3 and 9.

What Each Control Does

q₁ slider
Sets the source charge — both \(E\) and \(V\) (measured at \(q_2\)'s location) depend only on \(q_1\), never on \(q_2\).
q₂ slider
Sets the test charge — it appears in \(F\) and \(U\) (which describe the interaction between both charges), but not in \(E\) or \(V\).
r slider
Sets the separation in nanometers — governs the \(1/r^2\) falloff of \(F\) and \(E\), and the slower \(1/r\) falloff of \(V\) and \(U\).
Reset to Defaults
Returns all three sliders to the chapter's example values (\(q_1=+2\) nC, \(q_2=-1\) nC, \(r=1\) nm).

Challenge Questions

1. Starting from the defaults, if you double \(r\) from 1 nm to 2 nm, what happens to \(F\)?

\(F\propto 1/r^2\), so doubling \(r\) divides \(F\) by \(2^2=4\).

Try it in the simulation: set r=1, note F, then set r=2 and compare.

2. Using the same doubling of \(r\), what happens to \(V\)?

\(V\propto 1/r\), so doubling \(r\) only divides \(V\) by 2 — it falls off more slowly than \(F\).

Compare the exponent on \(r\) in the \(V\) equation to the exponent in the \(F\) equation.

3. If \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) have the same sign, what is the sign of \(U\)?

\(U=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1q_2}{r}\); the product of two same-sign charges is always positive, regardless of r.

Set both q1 and q2 positive in the simulation and read the sign of U.

4. If you flip the sign of \(q_2\) only (keep its magnitude the same), which readouts change sign?

F and U both contain the product \(q_1q_2\), so they flip sign. E and V depend only on \(q_1\), so they are unaffected by q2's sign.

Look at which equations actually contain q2 as a variable.

5. Which quantity falls off the slowest as separation \(r\) increases?

V (and U) depend on \(1/r\), while F and E depend on the much steeper \(1/r^2\).

Compare the exponents on r in each boxed equation from the chapter.
