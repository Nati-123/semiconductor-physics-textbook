Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Select Simple Cubic, Body-Centered Cubic, and Face-Centered Cubic in turn and watch the "Atoms per conventional cell" and "Atomic packing factor" values climb from 1 → 2 → 4 and 0.524 → 0.680 → 0.740 as more atoms are added to the same cube.
  • Predict: Before selecting Diamond Cubic, predict what the coordination number will be, knowing it is built from two interpenetrating FCC lattices offset by \((1/4,1/4,1/4)a\) — then check the readout and the gray tetrahedral bonds.
  • Compare: Switch between Zinc Blende (GaAs) and Rock Salt (NaCl) and compare the legend colors and bond geometry — Zinc Blende bonds are tetrahedral (CN=4), Rock Salt has no drawn bonds but an octahedral CN=6 arrangement.
  • Observe: Turn on "Primitive cell (instead of conventional)" for Face-Centered Cubic and see the purple rhombohedral primitive cell (1 atom) drawn inside the same conventional cube (4 atoms).
  • Explore Miller indices: Turn on "Show plane / direction overlay" for Simple Cubic, set the plane to (1 1 1), and rotate until you see the shaded triangular plane connecting three cube corners.
  • Explain: Explain why silicon and germanium crystallize in Diamond Cubic while GaAs crystallizes in the geometrically identical Zinc Blende structure.

Connects to Chapter 3: This MicroSim renders all nine crystal structures introduced in the chapter using the standard touching-sphere and interpenetrating-sublattice constructions: the three cubic Bravais lattices (SC, BCC, FCC), the hexagonal close-packed structure (HCP), the two tetrahedrally-coordinated diamond-family structures (Diamond Cubic, Zinc Blende), and the three ionic/compound structures (Rock Salt, Cesium Chloride, Wurtzite). Diamond Cubic and Zinc Blende are the two structures most central to semiconductor physics: silicon and germanium — the workhorse elemental semiconductors — crystallize in Diamond Cubic, while most III–V compound semiconductors such as GaAs, GaP, and InP crystallize in the geometrically identical but two-species Zinc Blende structure.

What Each Control Does

Crystal structure dropdown
Switches between all nine structures, updating atom positions, species colors, bonds, and every value in the readout panel below the canvas.
Click-and-drag on the 3D view
Rotates (orbits) the structure freely so you can inspect atom positions from any angle (uses p5.js \(\texttt{orbitControl()}\)).
Right-click-and-drag (or two-finger drag) on the 3D view
Pans the camera sideways without changing the viewing angle.
Scroll / pinch on the 3D view
Zooms the camera in and out.
Atom labels checkbox
Prints each species' chemical symbol (e.g. Si, Ga, As, Na, Cl, Cs) directly above every atom it draws.
Unit-cell edges checkbox
Shows or hides the black wireframe boundary of the current cell (conventional cube, or hexagonal prism for HCP/Wurtzite).
Nearest-neighbor bonds checkbox
Draws gray tetrahedral bonds for the diamond-family structures (Diamond Cubic, Zinc Blende, Wurtzite); has no visual effect for structures without tetrahedral bonding, as noted in the readout panel.
Primitive cell checkbox
Replaces the black conventional-cell wireframe with a purple primitive-cell parallelepiped (the smallest repeating cell, containing exactly one lattice point).
Show plane / direction overlay checkbox
Reveals the Miller-index plane (h k l) and direction [u v w] controls below, and shades/draws them on cubic structures (disabled for the hexagonal structures HCP and Wurtzite, which require 4-index Miller–Bravais notation).
Atom size slider
Scales every sphere's drawn radius, useful for separating overlapping atoms or emphasizing the touching-sphere condition.
Plane (h k l) fields
Sets the Miller indices of the shaded plane; a plane satisfying \(hX+kY+lZ=1\) in fractional cell coordinates is clipped to the cube and shaded blue.
Direction [u v w] fields
Draws an orange arrow from the origin along the crystallographic direction \([uvw]\).
Reset Camera button
Returns the view to its original default orbit position and zoom level.
Fullscreen button (top-right of the canvas)
Expands the MicroSim to fill the browser window for a larger, more detailed view; press Escape or the button again to exit.

Challenge Questions

1. How many atoms per unit cell does the Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) structure contain?

8 corner atoms × 1/8 each = 1, plus 1 full body-center atom = 2 atoms per cell, matching the readout panel.

Select BCC and read the "Atoms per conventional cell" value directly from the readout panel below the canvas.

2. Which structure has the highest coordination number?

FCC and HCP are tied at coordination number 12 — both are the closest possible packings of identical spheres, giving them the highest packing fraction (0.740) of any structure in this MicroSim.

Cycle through the structures in the dropdown and compare the "Coordination number" line in the readout panel.

3. What is the coordination number of Diamond Cubic and Zinc Blende, and why is it lower than FCC's?

Select Diamond Cubic and count the gray tetrahedral bonds leaving any interior atom — exactly 4, reflecting the covalent tetrahedral bonding of silicon and germanium.

Turn on "Nearest-neighbor bonds" for Diamond Cubic and count the bonds leaving one atom.

4. Which real semiconductor materials crystallize in the Diamond Cubic structure, versus the Zinc Blende structure?

Diamond Cubic requires the two interpenetrating sublattices to hold the same element (Si-Si or Ge-Ge bonds), while Zinc Blende places two different species on the sublattices (e.g. Ga and As) — the readout panel's "Example materials" line confirms this for each structure.

Select Diamond Cubic and Zinc Blende in turn and read the "Example materials" and "Semiconductor relevance" lines in the readout panel.

5. In the Cesium Chloride (CsCl) structure, why is the Bravais lattice classified as Simple Cubic rather than Body-Centered Cubic, even though there is an atom at the cube's body center?

A true Bravais lattice requires every lattice point to see an identical environment. In CsCl the corner and body-center sites hold different ions, so translating by (1/2,1/2,1/2)a does not map the crystal onto itself — the correct description is a Simple Cubic lattice with a 2-atom (Cs, Cl) basis, exactly as shown in the readout panel's "Bravais lattice / basis" line.

Select CsCl and read the "Bravais lattice / basis" line in the readout panel — it explicitly explains why this is Simple Cubic, not BCC.

6. What structural relationship does Wurtzite have to Hexagonal Close-Packed (HCP)?

Just as Zinc Blende is the compound analog of Diamond Cubic (both built on FCC), Wurtzite is the compound analog of HCP — select Wurtzite (GaN) and compare its blue/orange two-sublattice structure to the single-species HCP structure.

Switch between HCP and Wurtzite (GaN) and compare the "Bravais lattice / basis" and legend entries in the readout panel.
