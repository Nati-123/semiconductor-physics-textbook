<h2 style="color: #5A3EED !important; border-bottom: 2px solid #5A3EED; padding-bottom: 0.3rem; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 2rem;">Description</h2>

<p style="color: #555; line-height: 1.85; font-size: 1.02rem; margin-bottom: 1.2rem;">
This MicroSim lets students choose an object — electron, proton, neutron, baseball, bowling ball, or a custom mass — and a velocity, then computes its de Broglie wavelength <span class="arithmatex">\(\lambda = h/(mv)\)</span> using <span class="arithmatex">\(h = 6.626\times10^{-34}\)</span> J·s. A log-scale ruler plots the computed wavelength alongside reference lengths (atomic diameter, visible-light wavelength, red blood cell diameter) to make it visually obvious that matter waves only matter at quantum scales.
</p>

<h2 style="color: #5A3EED !important; border-bottom: 2px solid #5A3EED; padding-bottom: 0.3rem; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 2rem;">How to Use</h2>

<ol style="padding-left: 1.2rem; margin: 0.8rem 0 1.2rem 0;">
<li style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem; line-height: 1.75; color: #333;"><strong style="color: #5A3EED;">Choose an object</strong> from the dropdown, or select "Custom" to unlock the mass slider</li>
<li style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem; line-height: 1.75; color: #333;"><strong style="color: #5A3EED;">Drag the velocity slider</strong> and watch the wavelength readout and ruler marker respond</li>
<li style="margin-bottom: 0.6rem; line-height: 1.75; color: #333;"><strong style="color: #5A3EED;">Click a preset button</strong> to jump to a worked-example configuration</li>
<li style="margin-bottom: 0; line-height: 1.75; color: #333;"><strong style="color: #5A3EED;">Compare the red marker</strong> to the green reference lines to judge whether the wavelength is quantum-scale or immeasurably small</li>
