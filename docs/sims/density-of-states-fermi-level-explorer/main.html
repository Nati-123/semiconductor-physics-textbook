Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Select "Insulator" and observe the wide pale-gray (empty, g(E)=0) gap between the green valence band and purple conduction band, with the dashed red EF line sitting in the middle of that empty gap.
  • Predict: Before switching to "Intrinsic Semiconductor", predict whether the gap will be wider or narrower than the insulator's.
  • Observe: Switch to "Intrinsic Semiconductor" and confirm the gap is much narrower, though the qualitative picture (full valence band, empty conduction band at T=0) is otherwise identical.
  • Compare: Switch between "n-type Semiconductor" and "p-type Semiconductor" and watch EF move — up toward Ec for n-type, down toward Ev for p-type — a preview of Chapters 7-8's doping story.
  • Switch to "Metal" and notice that EF now sits inside a region where the density-of-states curve is nonzero on both sides — no gap at all.
  • Switch to "Semimetal" and compare how thin the density-of-states curve is right at EF, compared to the Metal preset.
  • Change the Temperature preset from 0 K to 77 K to 300 K to 600 K (or drag the T slider) and watch the sharp occupied/empty boundary near EF blur into a wider smooth gradient as T rises. Read the growing f(Ec) value in the numeric readout as confirmation.

Connects to Chapter 6: This MicroSim draws the density of states \(g_v(E)\propto\sqrt{E_v-E}\) and \(g_c(E)\propto\sqrt{E-E_c}\) for a valence-like and conduction-like band, shaded by the Fermi-Dirac occupation probability \(f(E)=1/[1+\exp((E-E_F)/k_BT)]\), with \(k_BT\) computed from the Temperature \(T\) (K) control. Whether the two bands overlap in energy (Metal, Semimetal) or are separated by a real gap (semiconductor variants, Insulator), and how large that gap or overlap is, is exactly what this chapter uses to classify a material's band structure.

What Each Control Does

Material dropdown
Selects a preset band-edge configuration (valence-band top \(E_v\), conduction-band bottom \(E_c\), and Fermi level \(E_F\)) representative of a metal, insulator, semimetal, or an intrinsic/n-type/p-type semiconductor.
Temperature preset dropdown
Jumps the temperature slider directly to 0 K, 77 K (liquid nitrogen), 300 K (room temperature), or 600 K.
Temperature T (K) slider
Sets the absolute temperature, converted internally to thermal energy \(k_BT\) for the Fermi-Dirac distribution; higher T smears the sharp T=0 occupied/empty boundary near \(E_F\) over a wider energy range.

Challenge Questions

1. In the Insulator preset, where does the Fermi level E_F sit?

E_F sits inside the wide band gap, where the density of states is exactly zero — consistent with the fact that no real state exists at E_F in an insulator.

Look at where the dashed E_F line crosses relative to the green (valence) and blue (conduction) shaded regions.

2. What distinguishes the Metal preset from the semiconductor and Insulator presets?

The Metal preset's valence-like and conduction-like bands overlap in energy, so E_F sits where g(E) > 0 — real states exist right at E_F, unlike the semiconductor and insulator cases.

Compare where the green and purple curves are nonzero relative to E_F in the Metal preset versus the Intrinsic Semiconductor preset.

3. What happens to the sharp occupied/empty boundary near E_F as you raise the Temperature T?

Raising T increases kT = kB·T in the Fermi-Dirac distribution, smearing the occupied/empty boundary over a wider energy range around E_F, visible as a fading gradient rather than a hard edge. The f(Ec) readout grows accordingly.

Step through the Temperature presets from 0 K to 600 K and watch the boundary near the dashed E_F line, and the f(Ec) readout at the bottom of the control panel.

4. Compared to the Intrinsic Semiconductor preset, where does E_F move for n-type and p-type semiconductor presets?

An n-type semiconductor has extra electrons, which raises the occupation probability near the conduction band, pulling E_F up toward Ec; a p-type semiconductor has extra holes, which pulls E_F down toward Ev. Chapters 7-8 explain exactly how doping produces this shift.

Switch between the n-type and p-type presets and read the E_F value in the numeric readout, comparing it to Ec and Ev.
