<h2 style="color: #5A3EED !important; border-bottom: 2px solid #5A3EED; padding-bottom: 0.3rem; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 2rem;">Description</h2>

<p style="color: #555; line-height: 1.85; font-size: 1.02rem; margin-bottom: 1.2rem;">
This MicroSim fires individual particles, one at a time, at a barrier with one or two slits and a detector screen beyond it. Each particle's landing position is sampled from the Fraunhofer interference intensity distribution, so any single detection looks random, but the accumulated pattern of many detections reveals interference fringes when both slits are open — and a single smooth band when only one slit is open. This is the classic demonstration that quantum particles are always detected individually (particle behavior) while the statistics of many detections follow a wave-interference pattern (wave behavior).
</p>

<ul style="list-style: none; padding-left: 0.8rem; margin: 0.5rem 0 1rem 0;">

</ul>

<h3 style="color: #5A3EED; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 1.5rem; margin-bottom: 0.6rem;">During the Simulation (10 minutes)</h3>

<ol style="padding-left: 1.2rem; margin: 0.5rem 0 1rem 0;">
<li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.75; color: #333;">Start with two slits open and watch the first 10-20 particles — note how scattered/unpredictable individual hits look</li>
<li style="margin-bottom: 0.5rem; line-height: 1.75; color: #333;">Let the pattern build past 100 particles and identify the bright and dark fringe bands</li>
<li style="margin-bottom: 0; line-height: 1.75; color: #333;">Clear the pattern, switch to one slit, rebuild it, and compare the two final patterns side by side (screenshot or sketch both)</li>
</ol>
