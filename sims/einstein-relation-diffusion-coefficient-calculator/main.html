Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: At room temperature (300 K) and low doping, note the computed diffusion coefficient and compare it to silicon's commonly cited \(D_n\approx35\ \text{cm}^2/\text{s}\).
  • Predict: Before raising the doping concentration, predict whether D will rise or fall, given how mobility responds to doping.
  • Observe: Raise the temperature and watch D increase — both because \(k_BT\) grows directly and because mobility itself changes with temperature.

Connects to Chapter 12: The Einstein relation, \(D=\mu k_BT/q\), connects the diffusion coefficient \(D\) directly to Chapter 11's mobility \(\mu\) — both are set by exactly the same scattering physics, since a carrier that scatters less both drifts faster in a field and diffuses faster down a concentration gradient. This is why measuring mobility alone is enough to predict diffusion behavior, without a separate diffusion experiment.

Challenge Questions

1. According to the Einstein relation, what two quantities is the diffusion coefficient directly proportional to (at fixed mobility)?

D = μk_BT/q shows D is directly proportional to both mobility μ and absolute temperature T (with q as a fixed constant of proportionality).
