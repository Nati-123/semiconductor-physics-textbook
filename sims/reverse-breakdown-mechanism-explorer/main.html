  • Try this: Sweep the doping slider from \(10^{14}\) to \(10^{19}\ \text{cm}^{-3}\) and watch the estimated breakdown voltage fall by several orders of magnitude.
  • Predict: Before crossing the ~6 V dashed line, predict which schematic panel (Avalanche or Zener) will become highlighted.
  • Observe: The Avalanche panel's depletion region is drawn wide, giving carriers room to accelerate; the Zener panel's is drawn narrow, short enough for direct tunneling.

Connects to Chapter 15: Reverse breakdown occurs via two distinct mechanisms. Avalanche breakdown is an impact-ionization chain reaction that dominates in lightly-doped junctions with wide depletion regions. Zener breakdown is quantum-mechanical tunneling that dominates in heavily-doped junctions with narrow depletion regions — typically below a breakdown voltage of about 5-6 V in silicon.

What Each Control Does

N
Sets the lightly-doped side concentration, which sets the estimated avalanche breakdown voltage \(V_{BR}\propto1/N\).

Challenge Questions

1. As doping concentration N increases, what happens to the estimated avalanche breakdown voltage?

Correct. Since VBR ≈ εEcrit²/(2qN), breakdown voltage is inversely proportional to doping — heavier doping means a thinner depletion region that reaches the critical field at a lower voltage.

Look at where N appears in the VBR formula — is it in the numerator or denominator?

2. A diode has a measured breakdown voltage of about 3 V. Which mechanism most likely dominates?

Correct. A breakdown voltage this low requires very heavy doping and a correspondingly thin depletion barrier, characteristic of Zener tunneling rather than avalanche multiplication.
