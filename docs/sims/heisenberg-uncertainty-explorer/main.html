What Each Control Does

Δx slider
Sets the position uncertainty on a log scale from 1 pm to 1 µm — narrower confinement means a smaller \(\Delta x\).
Particle type dropdown
Selects electron or proton, which converts the minimum momentum uncertainty into a minimum velocity uncertainty \(\Delta v_{min} = \Delta p_{min}/m\).

Challenge Questions

1. If you decrease \(\Delta x\) by a factor of 10, what happens to the minimum \(\Delta p\)?

\(\Delta p_{min} = \hbar/(2\Delta x)\) is inversely proportional to \(\Delta x\), so shrinking \(\Delta x\) by 10x grows \(\Delta p_{min}\) by 10x.

Look at where \(\Delta x\) appears in the formula for \(\Delta p_{min}\) — numerator or denominator?

2. At the same \(\Delta x\), why does the proton have a smaller minimum \(\Delta v\) than the electron?

\(\Delta p_{min}\) depends only on \(\Delta x\), not mass, so it is identical for both particles — but dividing by the proton's ~1836x larger mass gives a much smaller \(\Delta v_{min}\).

Switch the particle dropdown at a fixed \(\Delta x\) and compare the \(\Delta p\) and \(\Delta v\) readouts separately.

3. What does the widening momentum-spread curve represent as \(\Delta x\) shrinks?

The uncertainty principle is a fundamental property of nature, not a measurement flaw — confining the position more tightly fundamentally widens the range of possible momenta.

Re-read the distinction between "measurement error" and "fundamental uncertainty" in the chapter text.

4. Which expression correctly gives the minimum momentum uncertainty?

Rearranging \(\Delta x\,\Delta p \geq \hbar/2\) for the minimum case gives \(\Delta p_{min} = \hbar/(2\Delta x)\).

Start from \(\Delta x\,\Delta p \geq \hbar/2\) and solve for \(\Delta p\) directly.

5. Confining an electron inside a nucleus-sized region (~1 fm) instead of an atom-sized region (~0.1 nm) would make \(\Delta p_{min}\):

A nucleus is roughly 100,000 times smaller than an atom, so \(\Delta p_{min} = \hbar/(2\Delta x)\) becomes roughly 100,000 times larger — this is part of why electrons are not found inside nuclei.

Drag \(\Delta x\) to its smallest slider value and note how large \(\Delta p_{min}\) and \(\Delta v_{min}\) become.
