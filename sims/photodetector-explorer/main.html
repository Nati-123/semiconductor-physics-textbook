Guided Exploration & Challenge Questions

Try This

  • Try this: Increase quantum efficiency toward 1.0 and watch responsivity and photocurrent rise together.
  • Predict: Before lowering photon energy, predict whether responsivity will increase or decrease.
  • Observe: At fixed quantum efficiency, lower photon energy (longer wavelength) gives higher responsivity, since each watt of optical power now carries more photons.

Connects to Chapter 20: A photodetector is Chapter 17's photodiode operated to convert optical power into photocurrent, characterized by responsivity \(\mathcal{R}=\eta q/hf\).

What Each Control Does

Optical power
Incident optical power on the photodetector, in milliwatts.
Quantum efficiency η
Fraction of incident photons producing a collected electron-hole pair.
Photon energy hf
Energy per incident photon in electron volts, related to wavelength.

Challenge Questions

1. At fixed quantum efficiency, why does lower photon energy give higher responsivity?

Correct. Since R = ηq/hf, a smaller hf in the denominator increases R — more, lower-energy photons are needed to carry the same total optical power.

Look at where hf appears in the responsivity formula.

2. If quantum efficiency doubles, what happens to photocurrent at fixed optical power and photon energy?

Correct. Since responsivity is directly proportional to η, doubling η doubles both responsivity and photocurrent.

Try setting η to 0.4 and then 0.8 at fixed power and photon energy, and compare photocurrent.
